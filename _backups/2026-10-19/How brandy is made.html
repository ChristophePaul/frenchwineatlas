The great French brandies

Cognac — twice-distilled finesse

Cognac is twice-distilled in a copper alambic charentais. Thin ugni blanc wine becomes the cloudy brouillis, then the bonne chauffe yields the pure cœur. Years in Limousin oak — losing an angels' share each year — and a final assemblage and reduction make the smooth amber spirit, graded VS to XO.

Armagnac — France's oldest brandy

Armagnac is usually distilled once, in a continuous alambic armagnaçais. Lower-strength distillation keeps more flavour, so Armagnac is richer and more rustic than Cognac. Aged in Gascon black oak, it is proudly sold as single vintages — and as clear, unaged blanche armagnac.

Marc & Eau-de-Vie — from pomace and fruit

Marc distils the pomace left after pressing (Italy's grappa is the same idea), while a fruit eau-de-vie distils fermented fruit — pear, cherry (kirsch), plum. Grape marcs are often oak-aged; fruit spirits usually stay crystal-clear to keep their orchard freshness.
