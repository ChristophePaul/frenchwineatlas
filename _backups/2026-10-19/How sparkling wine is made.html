The four roads to bubbles

Champagne — Méthode Traditionnelle

Champagne is the benchmark for the méthode traditionnelle: a still base wine is blended (assemblage), bottled with a liqueur de tirage, and fermented a second time inside the bottle (prise de mousse). Long ageing sur lie, then remuage, dégorgement and dosage finish the wine. Only grapes grown and hand-harvested in the Champagne region may carry the name.

Crémant — the method beyond Champagne

Crémant is the very same method used outside Champagne — Alsace, Loire, Bourgogne, Limoux, Bordeaux, Die, Jura, Savoie. Hand-harvest and whole-bunch pressing are required; the main difference is a shorter minimum ageing sur lie (9 months). Often excellent value for a true bottle-fermented sparkler.

Méthode Ancestrale — the original ‘pét-nat’

The méthode ancestrale is older than Champagne and far simpler: a single fermentation is bottled before it finishes, trapping natural CO₂ with no added yeast or sugar and no dosage. Today's pétillant naturel revives it — lightly fizzy, often cloudy. Classics: Blanquette de Limoux ancestrale, Bugey-Cerdon, Clairette de Die, Gaillac.

Méthode Charmat — the tank method

The méthode charmat (tank method / cuve close) runs the second fermentation in a big pressurised tank instead of the bottle, then bottles under pressure. Fast and fruit-forward, it is ideal for aromatic grapes — the method behind Prosecco and Asti.
