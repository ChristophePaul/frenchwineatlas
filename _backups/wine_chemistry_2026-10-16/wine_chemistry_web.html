GRAPE / VARIETAL

Compounds present in the berry before fermentation. Skin, pulp, seeds, juice. These vary by variety, terroir, and ripeness of the grape.

CompoundFormulaAromaConc.Description
Glucose / Fructose C₆H₁₂O₆ Sweet 15–25% Primary fermentable sugars (150–250 g/L). Converted to ethanol + CO₂.
Tartaric acid C₄H₆O₆ Tart, sharp 2–8 g/L Dominant stable acid (2000–8000 ppm). Persists through fermentation.
Malic acid C₄H₆O₅ Green apple 1–6 g/L Sharp acid decreases with ripeness. Converted to lactic acid during MLF.
Linalool C₁₀H₁₈O Lavender, floral 10–2000 ppb Key terpene in Muscat/Riesling. Threshold ~15 ppb. Bound forms released in aging.
β-Damascenone C₁₃H₁₈O Rose, cooked apple 0.05–10 ppb Norisoprenoid; threshold ~0.05 ppb. Potentiates all other fruit aromas.
β-Ionone C₁₃H₂₀O Violet, raspberry 0.01–1 ppb From β-carotene. Threshold ~0.007 ppb. Hallmark of Cabernet Sauvignon.
IBMP (methoxypyrazine) C₉H₁₄N₂O Green pepper, grass 1–40 ppb Threshold ~2 ppb. Cool-climate marker. Destroyed by UV light and sunlight.
Anthocyanins C₁₅H₁₁O⁺·glycoside (color) 50–5000 ppm Red pigments in skins (50 mg/L Pinot → 5 g/L Tannat). Stabilize during aging.
Condensed tannins Polymer (catechins) Astringent 500–4000 ppm Main astringency source (0.5–4 g/L red wine). Polymerize and soften with age.
Cys-3MH / GSH-3MH (precursors) Cys / GSH conjugates Odorless 1–20 µg/L Odorless precursors to potent thiols (3MH, 4MMP). Released by yeast enzymes.
Glutathione C₁₀H₁₇N₃O₆S — 10–200 ppm Antioxidant tripeptide protecting varietal thiols from oxidative browning.
Citric acid C₆H₈O₇ Citrus 100–500 ppm Minor acid; consumed by LAB during MLF, generating diacetyl + acetic acid.

ALCOHOLIC FERMENTATION

Saccharomyces cerevisiae: Converts sugars to ethanol + CO₂ while generating hundreds of secondary metabolites that define the wine’s aromatic backbone

CompoundFormulaAromaConc.Description
Ethanol C₂H₅OH Warming, solvent 8–16% Primary product (80,000–160,000 ppm). Solvent, preservative, and aroma carrier.
Glycerol C₃H₈O₃ Sweet, viscous 5–15 g/L 3rd most abundant after water and ethanol. Contributes body and mouthfeel.
Isoamyl acetate C₇H₁₄O₂ Banana, pear drops 0.5–5 ppm Key acetate ester. Threshold ~0.03 mg/L. Higher at cool fermentation temps.
Ethyl acetate C₄H₈O₂ Solvent / nail polish 30–200 ppm Most abundant ester. Fault above ~120 mg/L. Produced by yeast and bacteria.
Ethyl hexanoate / butyrate C₈–C₆ ethyl esters Apple, pineapple 0.01–0.5 ppm Fruity ethyl esters; threshold ~0.005–0.02 mg/L. Decrease with bottle age.
Isoamyl alcohol C₅H₁₂O Fusel, banana 50–400 ppm Most abundant fusel alcohol. Harsh above 300–400 mg/L. From leucine catabolism.
2-Phenylethanol C₈H₁₀O Rose water, floral 10–80 ppm Pleasant fusel alcohol from phenylalanine. Threshold ~10 mg/L.
Acetaldehyde C₂H₄O Green apple, bruised 10–75 ppm Main aldehyde. Binds SO₂. High levels signal oxidation. Up to 500 ppm in Sherry.
Diacetyl C₄H₆O₂ Butter, cream 0.2–4 ppm Butter aroma. Threshold 0.2 mg/L (white), 1 mg/L (red). Produced by yeast + LAB.
Succinic acid C₄H₆O₄ Sour, salty-bitter 0.5–1.5 g/L Main non-volatile yeast acid (500–1500 ppm). Unique salty-bitter-sour taste.
Acetic acid C₂H₄O₂ Vinegar 0.2–0.6 g/L Volatile acidity. Fault above 0.9 g/L (white) / 1.2 g/L (red).
3MH / 3MHA / 4MMP (thiols) C₆H₁₄OS / C₆H₁₂OS Passionfruit, boxwood 1–4000 ppb Potent varietal thiols released by yeast. 4MMP threshold ~1 ppb; 3MH ~60 ppb.
H₂S / Methanethiol H₂S / CH₃SH Rotten egg / cabbage 1–80 ppb Reductive faults from N-deficient fermentation. Removed by aeration or Cu treatment.
Mannoproteins / β-Glucans Glycoprotein / Polysaccharide — 100–600 ppm Yeast cell wall polysaccharides. Improve mouthfeel and tartrate/foam stability.

MALOLACTIC FERMENTATION

Oenococcus oeni: converts malic acid → lactic acid + CO₂. Transforms acidity, texture and aromatic complexity.

CompoundFormulaAromaConc.Description
L-Malic → L-Lactic acid C₄H₆O₅ → C₃H₆O₃ Sharp → soft, creamy 1–4 g/L Core MLF reaction. Raises pH by 0.1–0.3 units. Softens overall acid profile.
Diacetyl (MLF) C₄H₆O₂ Butter, cream 0.2–5 ppm Key MLF aroma compound. Can spike to 5+ mg/L during MLF. From citric acid + pyruvate.
Acetoin C₄H₈O₂ Yogurt, creamy 1–20 ppm Diacetyl reduction product. Threshold ~150 mg/L — usually sub-threshold.
2,3-Butanediol C₄H₁₀O₂ Faint sweetness 200–900 ppm Further acetoin reduction. Non-aromatic; contributes marginally to body.
Ethyl lactate C₅H₁₀O₃ Creamy, milky 10–100 ppm Lactic acid + ethanol. Accumulates slowly post-MLF. Adds textural softness.
Acetic acid (MLF contribution) C₂H₄O₂ Vinegar risk +50–200 ppm From citric acid catabolism by LAB. Higher risk with elevated citric acid in must.
Biogenic amines (histamine, tyramine) N-heterocycles — 0–30 ppm Decarboxylation of amino acids by LAB. EU guideline <10 mg/L for histamine.
pH shift — — +0.1–0.3 pH Raises pH 0.1–0.3 units. Affects SO₂ efficacy, color, and microbial stability.

AGING (Barrel / Bottle / Lees)

Oak extraction, oxidation, tannin evolution, and slow reductive chemistry over months to decades in barrel and bottle.

CompoundFormulaAromaConc.Description
Vanillin C₈H₈O₃ Vanilla, sweet 1–20 ppm Oak lignin breakdown. Threshold ~0.1 mg/L. American > French oak. Decreases with reuse.
Eugenol C₁₀H₁₂O₂ Clove, spice 0.05–1 ppm Oak phenylpropanoid. Threshold ~0.5 mg/L. Higher in American oak barrels.
Guaiacol / 4-methylguaiacol C₇H₈O₂ / C₈H₁₀O₂ Smoke, toast, bacon 0.01–0.5 ppm From toasted oak lignin. Threshold ~0.065–0.095 mg/L. Heavier toast = more.
Whisky lactone C₉H₁₆O₂ Coconut, cedar 0.05–2 ppm Hallmark of American oak. cis-isomer threshold ~0.067 mg/L. French oak: trace only.
Furfural C₅H₄O₂ Caramel, almond 0.5–10 ppm Hemicellulose degradation during toasting. Usually below sensory threshold (~14 mg/L).
Ellagitannins Polyphenol (oak) — 10–200 ppm Water-soluble oak tannins. Enable micro-oxygenation and modulate astringency.
Sotolon C₆H₈O₃ Curry, fenugreek, walnut 1–400 ppb Oxidative aging marker. Threshold ~7 ppb. Defines Sherry, Vin Jaune, Madeira character.
TDN (petrol note) C₁₃H₁₆ Petrol, kerosene 1–150 ppb Develops in Riesling over 10–30 years. Threshold ~20 ppb. Prized by collectors.
Pyranoanthocyanins (vitisin A/B) Pigmented adducts (color) 10–200 ppm Stable pigments from anthocyanins + pyruvate/acetaldehyde. Brick-red hues in aged reds.
Tannin polymers / T-A adducts Polymer Softer astringency variable Tannin-anthocyanin coupling via oxidation or acetaldehyde bridging. Stabilize color.
DMS (bottle aging) C₂H₆S Truffle, black olive 10–100 ppb Increases slowly in bottle from SMM hydrolysis. Adds umami below ~25 ppb threshold.
Mannoproteins (autolytic) Glycoprotein — 100–800 ppm Released during yeast autolysis on lees. Key to Champagne foam stability and mouthfeel.

MICROBIAL / SPOILAGE

Unwanted organisms produce characteristic fault compounds Brettanomyces, acetic acid bacteria, aberrant LAB, and chemical faults

CompoundFormulaAromaConc.Description
CompoundFormulaAromaConc.Description
Brettanomyces Metabolites
4-Ethylphenol (4-EP) C₈H₁₀O Barnyard, leather, band-aid 100–4000 ppb Primary Brett compound. Threshold 140–440 ppb. From p-coumaric acid. Very persistent.
4-Ethylguaiacol (4-EG) C₉H₁₂O₂ Smoke, spice, bacon 10–400 ppb Co-occurs with 4-EP (~10:1 ratio). Threshold ~33 ppb. From ferulic acid via Brett.
Isovaleric acid C₅H₁₀O₂ Rancid cheese, sweaty 0.2–2 ppm Brett and LAB product. Threshold ~0.4–1 mg/L. Persistent; very difficult to mask.
Acetic Acid Bacteria
Acetic acid (bacterial) C₂H₄O₂ Vinegar (fault) 0.6–3 g/L Acetobacter oxidizes ethanol. Fault above 0.9 g/L (white) / 1.2 g/L (red). Legal limits apply.
Ethyl acetate (bacterial) C₄H₈O₂ Nail polish (fault) 120–500 ppm Accompanies high VA. Fault threshold ~120 mg/L. Legal limit typically 150 mg/L.
TCA (cork taint) C₇H₅Cl₃O Musty, wet cardboard 1–100 ppb Threshold ~1.4 ppb. Sub-threshold levels suppress aroma perception. Most common wine fault.
Mousy taint (ATHP, ETPY) N-heterocycles Mouse cage, popcorn 5–200 ppb Only perceived after saliva raises pH. Common in low-sulfite natural wines.
Mannitol C₆H₁₄O₆ Sweet, viscous (fault) 0.2–3 g/L LAB fructose reduction in stuck/high-sugar musts. Fault in dry wines — syrupy sweetness.
Geranium taint C₈H₁₄O Geranium leaf, metallic 0.1–5 ppb Sorbic acid metabolized by LAB → 2-ethoxyhexa-3,5-diene. Threshold ~0.1 mg/L.
Gluconic acid C₆H₁₂O₇ — 0.1–5 g/L Gluconobacter product in damaged/botrytised fruit. Strong SO₂ binder. Signals compromised fruit.
