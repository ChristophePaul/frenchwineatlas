@@H1 Pfingstberg
@@NAT Pfingstberg
This grand cru rests on a limestone-sandstone substratum, with a proportion of clay increasing towards its lower part. At its summit the subsoil is dominated by a micaceous sandstone (Buntsandstein) with a limestone-sandstone texture, whereas at the base there is a shelly limestone sandstone (Muschelkalk) with a marl-limestone-sandstone soil texture. The forest at the top of the Pfingstberg guarantees a constant water supply and tempers climatic variations. Its very free-draining sandstone soil thus avoids any excess of water.

@@H1 Rangen
@@NAT Rangen
The Rangen is the most southerly grand cru of Alsace and is situated at the mouth of a valley in the communes of Thann and Vieux-Thann, covering an area of 22 ha in a single block. Its altitude (350 m to 450 m) and the proximity of the Vosges massif, together with above-average rainfall (850 mm/year), characterise a late climate with slow ripening. A sunny exposure (south-south-west to south), a steep slope (90 % on average), a dark soil colour and the proximity of the river Thur make it possible to obtain high levels of ripeness at the end of the season. A long ripening period gives the wines great complexity.
The particular and unique terroir of the Rangen, of Carboniferous age (Devono-Dinantian), is composed of volcanic-sedimentary rocks. The stones strewn over it come from hard rocks, namely greywackes, volcanic tuffs and a flow rock, a brown micaceous andesite. This layer has a thickness varying between 40 and 60 cm above the fissured parent rock, which allows the roots to penetrate to a considerable depth. The nature of the ground and the depth of the soil characterise a lean environment, poor in clays but of quality, and therefore favourable to obtaining great wines and small natural yields.

#### 2° – Information on the quality and characteristics of the products
White wines for laying down par excellence, they are characterised by a marked freshness based on a dominant tartaric acidity linked to good ripeness of the grapes. The name of the appellation may be supplemented by customary denominations, provided that the wines come exclusively from the varieties that may be designated under the denomination concerned. They show great substance, much complexity and strong aromatic power with various nuances. Of great length on the palate, they gain in complexity over time.
Four types of wine can be distinguished:
- dry, mineral wines;
- aromatic, fruity, rich, full-bodied wines.
These two types of wine show a deep colour intensity ranging up to golden yellow / slightly amber old gold.
- Wines benefiting from the term “vendanges tardives” often display very exotic aromas of candied fruit, with a fresh finish.
- Wines benefiting from the term “sélection de grains nobles” are more concentrated, powerful wines, often showing aromas of fruit paste.
These two types of wine show a deep colour intensity ranging up to amber yellow. Wines benefiting from these last two terms are sweet wines of very long keeping. They are of remarkable concentration and great aromatic persistence. The terms “vendanges tardives” and “sélection de grains nobles” are systematically followed by the denominations Gewurztraminer, Muscat, Muscat Ottonel, Pinot gris and Riesling, and the indication of the vintage is compulsory.

@@H1 Schoenenbourg
@@NAT Schoenenbourg
Situated at the outlet of a small, closed and cool valley, this grand cru is remarkably protected from the prevailing west and north winds. Rather late, warming up slowly in spring and ensuring slow, regular ripening of the grapes, the lieu-dit is warm and subject to a large day–night temperature range. Clayey-marly, the soil is deep, rich in Keuper marl and gypsum veins. It contains a greater or lesser proportion of pink sandstone and glacial sands. @@H1 Sommerberg
@@NAT Sommerberg
It lies in the extension of the summit of the Galtz. The very steep slope of the grand cru makes it very sunny. It rests on acid soils derived from granitic sand (arène). Its relief is characterised by a succession of three amphitheatres separated by outcrops of the parent rock. @@H1 Sonnenglanz
@@NAT Sonnenglanz
Projecting into the plain, on a limestone promontory of Oligocene marls over pebbles, this grand cru is early-ripening and rather dry. These conditions are favourable to high ripeness of the grapes, often associated with the development of noble rot. This calcareous brown soil, relatively heavy, stony and deep, induces a slow start of vegetation linked to the very gradual warming of the soil. The geological nature of the soil ensures excellent retention of water and nutrients while allowing good drainage of rainwater.

@@H1 Vorbourg
@@NAT Vorbourg
The particularly dry and sunny mesoclimate, at the foot of the “Grand Ballon” and the “Petit Ballon”, makes it an early lieu-dit with high levels of ripeness. The soils are marl-limestone, with Bajocian oolitic limestones and Oligocene calcareous conglomerates. The presence of a dry, warm wind (föhn) reinforces the high ripeness. Topped by limestone heathland, the lieu-dit has no significant water reserve.

As a translation of this usage, the controlled designation of origin “Alsace grand cru Vorbourg” was recognised in 1992. The wines come from parcels that have been the subject of a rigorous and precise delimitation inspired by the historical cores of production. This recognition was built around the white varieties, although before the German period (before 1870) the vineyards of the commune of Rouffach were better known for their red wines than for their white wines. The reds were more expensive and more appreciated than the whites. The production of high-quality red wines from the pinot noir variety has always persisted. This production, linked to the terroir of the Vorbourg grand cru, was recognised in 2024.
The only varieties selected are the following: riesling B, gewurztraminer Rs, pinot gris G, muscat à petits grains blancs B, muscat à petits grains roses Rs, muscat ottonel B. They may be vinified and marketed under their respective denomination. The pinot noir variety is used solely for the production of red wines. For red wines, enrichment is prohibited. These wines are marketed after a minimum ageing period of 11 months.
Since the law of 5 July 1972, the wines must be bottled within the Bas-Rhin and Haut-Rhin departments, traditionally in the “Vin du Rhin”-type flute described in the decree of 1955.
The vineyard, trained on high trellises in order to benefit from an optimal leaf area, is subject to more restrictive production conditions than the controlled designation of origin “Alsace”. The minimum planting density is 4,500 plants per hectare, and 5,500 plants per hectare for pinot noir. The grapes are harvested by hand. Wines benefiting from the terms “vendanges tardives” and “sélection de grains nobles” come from overripe and fragile grapes, also harvested by hand. These wines must undergo a minimum ageing period of 18 months.
#### 2° – Information on the quality and characteristics of the products
*The white wines:*
White wines for laying down par excellence, they are characterised by a marked freshness based on a dominant tartaric acidity linked to good ripeness of the grapes. The name of the appellation may be supplemented by customary denominations, provided that the wines come exclusively from the varieties that may be designated under the denomination concerned. They show great substance, much complexity and strong aromatic power with various nuances. Of great length on the palate, they gain in complexity over time.
Four types of wine can be distinguished:
- dry, mineral wines;
- aromatic, fruity, rich, full-bodied wines.
The white wines of the Vorbourg are ample and massive, with a fine minerality and a marked salinity. Their finish is tannic. Fruit aromas (citrus, white peach, pear…) dominate. The ripe acidity forms the backbone of the wine. The colour is of sustained intensity, ranging up to golden yellow.
- Wines benefiting from the term “vendanges tardives” often display very exotic aromas of candied fruit, with a fresh finish.
- Wines benefiting from the term “sélection de grains nobles” are more concentrated, powerful wines, often showing aromas of fruit paste.
These two types of wine show a deep colour intensity ranging up to amber yellow. Wines benefiting from these last two terms are sweet wines of very long keeping. They are of remarkable concentration and great aromatic persistence. The terms “vendanges tardives” and “sélection de grains nobles” are systematically followed by the denominations Gewurztraminer, Muscat, Muscat Ottonel, Pinot gris and Riesling, and the indication of the vintage is compulsory.
*The red wines:*
The colour of the red wines is deep and bright. Aromas of small red and black fruits mingle with mineral notes. The wines are ample, with a dense tannic structure and silky tannins. They are racy, fine and crystalline wines, persistent and with high ageing potential.
