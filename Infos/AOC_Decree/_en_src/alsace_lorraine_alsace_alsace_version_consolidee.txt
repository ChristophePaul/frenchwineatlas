# CHAPTER I
## I. – Name of the designation
Only wines meeting the specific provisions set out below may claim the controlled designation of origin “Alsace” or “Vin d’Alsace”, initially recognised by ordinance no. 45-2675 of 2 November 1945.
This specification is drawn up in accordance with the provisions of ordinance no. 45-2675 of 2 November 1945 and without prejudice to law no. 72-628 of 5 July 1972.
## II. – Geographical denominations and complementary terms
1° – The name of the controlled designation of origin may be followed by a sub-regional, communal or local geographical denomination, according to the provisions defined in points 2° and 3° below.
2° – The name of the controlled designation of origin may be followed by the following complementary geographical denominations for wines meeting the production conditions set for each of these complementary geographical denominations in the present specification:
- “Bergheim”;
- “Blienschwiller”;
- “Coteaux du Haut-Koenigsbourg”;
- “Côtes de Barr”;
- “Côte de Rouffach”;
- “Klevener de Heiligenstein”;
- “Ottrott”;
- “Rodern”;
- “Saint-Hippolyte”;
- “Scherwiller”;

- “Val Saint Grégoire”;

3° – The name of the controlled designation of origin may be followed by the name of a lieu-dit, for wines meeting the production conditions set for the indication of a lieu-dit in the present specification.
4° – The name of the controlled designation of origin, whether or not followed by a complementary geographical denomination or the name of a lieu-dit, may be supplemented by one of the following customary denominations, provided that the wines come exclusively from the grape varieties that may be designated under the denomination concerned and according to the production conditions set out in the present specification:

## VI. – Management of the vineyard
### 1° – Training methods
a) – Planting density
**GENERAL PROVISIONS**
*“Alsace” or “Vin d’Alsace”*
The vines have a minimum planting density of 4,000 plants per hectare. They may not have a spacing between rows greater than 2.50 metres. The vines have a spacing within the row of between 0.75 metre minimum and 1.50 metres maximum.
*“Alsace” or “Vin d’Alsace” followed by the name of a lieu-dit*
The vines have a minimum planting density of 4,500 plants per hectare. They may not have a spacing between rows greater than 2 metres. The vines have a spacing within the row of between 0.75 metre minimum and 1.50 metres maximum.
*“Alsace” or “Vin d’Alsace” followed by a complementary geographical denomination*
The vines have a minimum planting density of 4,500 plants per hectare. They may not have a spacing between rows greater than 2.20 metres. The vines have a spacing within the row of between 0.75 metre minimum and 1.50 metres maximum.
*“Alsace” or “Vin d’Alsace”, whether or not followed by a complementary geographical denomination or the name of a lieu-dit, supplemented by the terms “vendanges tardives” or “sélection de grains nobles”*
The vines have a minimum planting density of 4,500 plants per hectare. They may not have a spacing between rows greater than 2 metres. The vines have a spacing within the row of between 0.75 metre minimum and 1.50 metres maximum.
**PARTICULAR PROVISION**
From 25 October 2011, the date of the first approval of the present specification, the grubbing-up of rows within a parcel may not result in a spacing between the widest rows greater than 3 metres.
b) – Pruning rules
*“Alsace” or “Vin d’Alsace”*
The vines are pruned by single or double Guyot pruning with a maximum of 24 free eyes per plant.
*“Alsace” or “Vin d’Alsace” followed by the name of a lieu-dit or followed by a complementary geographical denomination*
The vines are pruned by single or double Guyot pruning with a maximum of 22 free eyes per plant.
*“Alsace” or “Vin d’Alsace”, whether or not followed by a complementary geographical denomination or the name of a lieu-dit, supplemented by the terms “vendanges tardives” or “sélection de grains nobles”*
The vines are pruned by single or double Guyot pruning with a maximum of 20 free eyes per plant.
c) – Trellising and foliage height rules
*“Alsace” or “Vin d’Alsace”*
- The foliage is trained on a vertical trellis. The height of trellised foliage may not be less than 0.675 times the spacing between rows. This height is measured between the attachment wire and the upper limit of the foliage after trimming. The distance between the carrying wire and the attachment wire may not exceed 0.40 metre.
*“Alsace” or “Vin d’Alsace” followed by the name of a lieu-dit or followed by a complementary geographical denomination*
- The foliage is trained on a vertical trellis. The height of trellised foliage may not be less than 0.675 times the spacing between rows. This height is measured between the attachment wire and the upper limit of the foliage after trimming. The distance between the carrying wire and the attachment wire may not exceed 0.30 metre.
*“Alsace” or “Vin d’Alsace”, whether or not followed by a complementary geographical denomination or the name of a lieu-dit, supplemented by the terms “vendanges tardives” or “sélection de grains nobles”*
- The foliage is trained on a vertical trellis. The height of trellised foliage may not be less than 0.675 times the spacing between rows. This height is measured between the attachment wire and the upper limit of the foliage after trimming. The distance between the carrying wire and the attachment wire may not exceed 0.30 metre.

e) – Threshold of missing vines
The percentage of dead or missing vine plants referred to in Article D. 645-4 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code is set at 20 %.
f) – Cultivation condition of the vine
The parcels are managed so as to ensure a good overall cultivation condition of the vine, in particular its sanitary condition and the upkeep of its soil.
### 2° – Other cultivation practices
a) In order to preserve the characteristics of the soils, which constitute a fundamental element of the terroir:
- Headlands wider than one metre are grassed, with the exception of vines that cannot be mechanised, known locally as “Kammerle”.
- Partial or total covering of the soil and of the vines with sheeting is prohibited.
- However, for evaluation purposes, covering of the soil with sheeting is authorised under the following conditions. The material must be permeable, allow gas and water exchanges, and its composition must not include petrochemical polymers. The evaluation is carried out subject to the signature of an agreement between the INAO, the ODG and the authorised operator concerned, conforming to the framework agreement approved by the competent national committee of 29 and 30 June 2023. In this context, the area of the parcels subject to the evaluation is limited to 5 % of the total area of the holding declared under the AOC.
b) Vine plantings and replantings are carried out with plant material all of whose components are produced in protected zones or have undergone hot-water treatment as defined by the order in force relating to the control of flavescence dorée.
### 3° – Irrigation
a) Irrigation may be authorised in accordance with the provisions of Article D. 645-5 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code. Irrigation is prohibited for parcels that have been the subject of a prior declaration of parcel assignment with a view to the potential claim of complementary geographical denominations, lieux-dits and the terms “vendanges tardives” or “sélection de grains nobles”.
b) Overhead sprinkling of the foliage is prohibited.
c) The application of fertiliser (fertigation) is prohibited.
## VII. – Harvest, transport and ripeness of the grapes

a) – The wines come from grapes harvested at good ripeness. The date of the start of the harvest is set in accordance with the provisions of Article D. 645-6 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code and of Article 2 of ordinance no. 45-2675 of 2 November 1945 on the definition of the designations of origin of the wines of Alsace.
b) – Wines liable to benefit from the name of a lieu-dit made from the pinot gris G and pinot noir N varieties come from grapes harvested by hand.
c) – Wines liable to benefit from the terms “vendanges tardives” and “sélection de grains nobles” come from grapes harvested by hand.
d) – Containers used for transporting the harvest must be sheltered from bad weather. Washing of the harvest transport containers after each emptying is compulsory.

## X. – Link with the geographical area
### 1° – Information on the geographical area
a) – Description of the natural factors contributing to the link
**Geographical location**
A Rhineland vineyard, one of the most northerly in France and in Europe, the Alsace vineyard, established mainly on the eastern face of the Vosges foothills, is located in 119 communes of the Bas-Rhin and Haut-Rhin departments. Stretching 120 km in length, often for less than 1 km in width, the vineyard extends from a small enclave around Cleebourg in the north, then from Marlenheim level with Strasbourg, down to Thann level with Mulhouse in the south.
**A highly diversified landscape**
The Alsace vineyard is set on a succession of sub-Vosgian hills, interspersed with deep valleys. This very undulating landscape offers diverse orientations with an often marked relief. The altitude of the plantings lies mostly between 200 m and 400 m.
**A highly contrasted climate**
The climate is clearly semi-continental: temperature differences are large. Winters are cold (the average temperature in January is 1.9 °C) and summers hot and dry (the average temperature in July is above 20 °C). The sum of effective temperatures (above 10 °C) during the vine’s growing period (1 April – 30 September) reaches 1,253 °C. The grape ripening period is characterised by an alternation of hot days and cool nights.
The Vosges massif acts as a protective screen against oceanic influences, a role amplified by a föhn effect, especially in autumn. This protective screen favours:
- high sunshine (1,800 h/year in Colmar);
- low rainfall: the summits of the Vosges receive annual precipitation of the order of 2,000 mm, whereas over the vineyard as a whole annual precipitation lies between 500 mm and 650 mm;
- excellent autumn and late-season conditions;
- a diversity of mesoclimates linked to the diversity of the landscape, the situations of the valleys and the position of the Vosges summits.
**A complex geology**
The subsidence of the north–south oriented Rhine Graben created an extensive network of geological faults forming a mosaic of small compartments presenting very varied geo-pedological situations. The vineyard lies in the fracture zone between the sedimentary Rhine plain and the crystalline Vosges massif, where all the layers of ground that had accumulated during the various geological periods have been reworked by erosion, thus offering very varied geo-pedological situations:
- brown soils on schist, granite or sandstone (Vosges substrate), shallow, free-draining and tending to acidity;
- colluvial soils of alluvial fans, and alluvial soils, sandy-gravelly, little developed, free-draining and tending to acidity (edge of the alluvial plain);
- calcareous brown soils or rendzina-type soils of the sub-Vosgian hills, as well as interbedded conglomerates and marls, with a good water regime.
b) – Description of the human factors contributing to the link
In turn the southern vineyard of the German Empire, then the northern vineyard of the French Republic, the Alsace vineyard has developed over the centuries the cultivation of very aromatic varieties, thereby forging its own identity. These varieties are chasselas B, sylvaner B, pinot blanc B and auxerrois B, riesling B, muscat à petits grains B, muscat à petits grains Rs, muscat ottonel B, gewurztraminer Rs, savagnin rose Rs, pinot gris G and pinot noir N.
Despite the vicissitudes of history, the professionalism and tenacity of the Alsace wine-growers, together with an excellent viticultural training system for almost a century and a half, have enabled the quality and specificities of Alsace wines to be recognised by the markets. All these efforts to rally around the identity term “Alsace” were rewarded by the recognition of Alsace wines as a designation of origin and the creation of the committee of experts for Alsace wines by the Ordinance of 2 November 1945. The controlled designation of origin “Alsace” was then recognised in 1962. Alsace viticulture has been managed since 1912 by the Association des Viticulteurs d’Alsace, today recognised as the defence and management body.
The “Vin du Rhin”-type flute, described in the decree of 1955, is traditionally used for packaging the still wines of Alsace. The decree of 13 May 1959 reserves the use of this flute for a limited number of designation-of-origin vineyards, including the wines of Alsace. The law of 5 July 1972, passed after the great demonstration by 4,000 wine-growers in Colmar, made bottling of wines of the controlled designation of origin “Vin d’Alsace” or “Alsace” compulsory within the Bas-Rhin and Haut-Rhin departments. This law confirms the wine-growers’ determination to preserve the quality of Alsace wines, and in particular their aromatic character, which suffered from bulk transport over long distances.
The wines come from parcels that have been the subject of a rigorous and precise delimitation, inspired by the historical cores of production. The Alsace vineyard has favoured high trellising of the foliage.
The terms “vendanges tardives” and “sélection de grains nobles” have been used in their German form in Alsace for more than 2 centuries. At the beginning of the 1970s, the producers of the region wished to protect the originality of these productions. They requested the codification of their usages, which became effective on 1 March 1984. These terms are reserved for the varieties gewurztraminer Rs, muscat à petits grains B, muscat à petits grains Rs, muscat ottonel B, pinot gris G and riesling B. These wines, made from overripe and fragile grapes, are harvested by hand and must undergo a minimum ageing period of 18 months.
The diversity of soil and climate situations of the sub-Vosgian hills is the basis of the communal and local indications frequently used to identify highly qualitative productions with particular characters derived from their terroir. It is customary to mention on the label the names of geographical units smaller than that of the controlled designation of origin “Alsace”, such as those of communes or lieux-dits.
In the Middle Ages and until the phylloxera crisis, the different varieties were also cultivated mixed together. In Alsace, these wines were historically called “zwicker” (blend). They then evolved, with the contribution of the so-called “noble” varieties, into “Edelzwicker” wines, a customary denomination complementary to the controlled designation of origin.
The Alsace wine landscape is characterised by grouped settlements, in often fortified villages surrounded by vines with a highly fragmented parcel structure. This original landscape is the legacy of the history of the many conflicts that have marked the region, and it demonstrates the economic importance of the vine, which has endured over the generations. Today the vineyard has 5,000 holdings of very different sizes, built on the family model, for 15,000 ha in production, forming a true geographical and human continuum. This diversity of structure has enabled the vineyard to occupy all markets. Thanks to a rigorous and active collective professional organisation, the very dynamic marketing of the production is harmoniously shared among the three professional families (independent wine-growers, cooperative cellars and grower-merchants).
### 2° – Information on the quality and characteristics of the products
Still white wines make up the great majority of production. The vineyard also produces red and rosé wines from the single variety pinot noir N.
The name of the controlled designation of origin may be supplemented by customary denominations because the wines come exclusively from the varieties that may be designated under the denomination concerned.
The wines are characterised by a marked freshness based on a dominant natural tartaric acidity linked to good ripeness of the grapes. They show an intense aromatic expression and good volume, punctuated by a length on the palate often underlined by a slight saturation in carbon dioxide (CO2), expressed locally by the term “spretsig”, enhancing the minerality and salinity that stimulate salivation. Recognised for centuries for their ageing potential (vins de garde), during which they improve, their great complexity and aromatic power become richer over time.
Six types can be distinguished:
- Dry, mineral wines with taut, ripe acidity. They are generally linked to the following customary denominations: Chasselas, Sylvaner, Pinot blanc, Pinot or Klevner, Auxerrois, Riesling, Muscat and Muscat Ottonel.
- Fruity wines, more supple and marked by high dry extract. They are generally linked to the customary denominations Gewurztraminer and Pinot gris.
The customary denomination “Edelzwicker” also falls within these two types. The above-mentioned customary denominations do not, however, fall strictly within these two main types, and may also, in various particular situations, find their optimal and original expression within the two types described.
Within this whole, wines benefiting from a complementary geographical denomination or the name of a lieu-dit constitute an original feature. The complementary geographical denominations and the lieux-dits fall within these two types of wine by combining the conditions for the optimal expression of each variety selected.
These two types of wine have a colour ranging, most often, from pale yellow with green highlights to golden yellow.
- Wines benefiting from the term “vendanges tardives” often display very exotic aromas of candied fruit, with a fresh finish.
- Wines benefiting from the term “sélection de grains nobles” are more concentrated, powerful wines, often showing aromas of fruit paste.
These two types of wine show a deep colour intensity ranging up to amber yellow. They are wines of very long keeping. They are of remarkable concentration and great aromatic persistence. These two terms are systematically followed by the customary denominations Gewurztraminer, Muscat, Muscat Ottonel, Pinot gris and Riesling and must bear the indication of the vintage.
- Ambitious, fine, elegant red wines linked to the customary denomination Pinot noir. They show various shades of red of sustained intensity. Certain complementary geographical denominations fall solely within this type of wine, combining the conditions for the optimal expression of pinot noir.
- Fresh, fruity and light red or rosé wines that may be designated Pinot noir, “clairet” or “Schillerwein”. They most often have a transparent pink to red colour of variable intensity.
### 3° – Causal interactions
A northern vineyard, protected from oceanic influences by the Vosges massif, it enjoys optimal sunshine. It is mainly this situation that gives the wines a common taste identity.
The diversity of viticultural situations, the variety of soils and subsoils and the very particular mesoclimates have led Alsace producers to retain a very wide palette of mainly aromatic varieties, favouring the maintenance of plant biodiversity and resisting any temptation to simplify the range of varieties. In the Alsace context, this plant diversity is the guarantee of the best expression of the terroir, forged around a strong “Alsace” identity.
The choice of varieties has oriented production essentially towards very aromatic wines. The diversity of viticultural situations, with very varied geo-pedological conditions, has offered Alsace producers the possibility of finding, for each of the varieties that has become established, its conditions of optimal expression. The rigorous and precise delimitation, inspired by the historical cores of production, the complementary, communal and local denominations and the corresponding planting plan give concrete form to this determination to achieve a good match.
The protection of the Vosges massif gives Alsace a dry, very sunny semi-continental climate that allows optimal development of the vine, framed by demanding minimum rules for the sugar content of the grapes. The location on the hillsides and the climate, characterised by the alternation of hot days and cool nights throughout the grape ripening period, combined with a high planting density and foliage height, ensure good ripeness of the grapes (balance of sugars and tartaric acidity), preserve freshness, and develop and concentrate the aromas of the wines and their length on the palate. The average altitude of the vineyard (200 m to 400 m), which corresponds to the optimal thermal belt, makes it possible to ensure a qualitative balance of the natural tartaric acidity. All these conditions thus contribute to forming the characteristics of the wines of Alsace.
The excellent late-season climatic conditions, often favourable to concentration on the vine and to the development of noble rot, allow the production of wines from overripe harvests. Manual harvesting, more demanding minimum sugar content rules and the prohibition of enrichment preserve this potential. The minimum ageing of eighteen months allows the wines to improve, their great complexity and aromatic power becoming richer over time.
The proximity of the Rhine already constituted in the Middle Ages an important communication route which has always favoured the export of the region’s wines to the countries at the mouth of the river and beyond. Dispatches outside the national territory and exports still represent more than a quarter of production today.
Driven by all the wine-growers, who have developed direct sales there, the Alsace Wine Route, which also showcases the historic and still living alliance with Alsace gastronomy, is the most attractive tourist site of the region. It constitutes a major asset of the Alsace economy.
“Highly esteemed white wines” according to André Jullien in 1822, they are “dry with a very pronounced aromatic bouquet”. They were formerly transported as far as Mainz, where they were used in blends with Rhine wines to give them strength and enrich their aromatic palette. Jules Guyot in 1866 said of the white wines of Alsace that they “offer much sought-after qualities, ranking very high on the scale of white wines”. The white wines from the riesling B variety were moreover his preference, “remarkable for their good taste, their strength and their longevity”.

a) – For wines presented under the customary denomination “Edelzwicker”, the indication of one or more customary denominations may not appear on the containers, labels and commercial documents.
b) – The use of two or more customary denominations on the same label is prohibited.
c) – Wines presented, under the terms of the present specification, under a customary denomination may not be declared after the harvest, offered to the public, dispatched, put up for sale or sold unless the said denomination is entered in the harvest declaration, in advertisements, on leaflets, labels, invoices and any containers whatsoever.
d) – Wines liable to benefit from the terms “vendanges tardives” and “sélection de grains nobles” must be presented:
- with the indication of the vintage;
- and with one of the following customary denominations: Gewurztraminer, Muscat, Muscat Ottonel, Pinot gris, Riesling.
e) – The varieties of interest for adaptation purposes may not be mentioned on the labelling.
# CHAPTER II
## I. – Declaration obligations
#### 1. Prior declaration of parcel assignment for vines liable to benefit from a complementary geographical denomination or the name of a lieu-dit
The prior declaration of parcel assignment is lodged with the defence and management body before 1 March of the year of harvest. This declaration specifies:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the identity of the operator receiving the grapes, where applicable;
- for each parcel: the cadastral reference, the area, the grape variety.
#### 2. Prior declaration of parcel assignment for vines liable to produce red wines
The prior declaration of parcel assignment is lodged with the defence and management body before 1 March of the year of harvest. This declaration specifies:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the identity of the operator receiving the grapes, where applicable;
- for each parcel: the cadastral reference, the area.
#### 3. Prior declaration of parcel assignment for vines liable to benefit from the term “vendanges tardives” or “sélection de grains nobles”
The prior declaration of parcel assignment is lodged with the defence and management body before 1 March of the year of harvest. This declaration specifies:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the identity of the operator receiving the grapes, where applicable;
- for each parcel: the cadastral reference, the area, the grape variety.
#### 4. Irrigation declaration
An irrigation declaration is sent to the defence and management body and to the control body at the latest 2 days before irrigation, in accordance with the provisions of Article D. 645-5 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code. This declaration is drawn up according to the model defined by the defence and management body.
#### 5. Declaration of renunciation to produce
Every operator declares to the defence and management body, before 15 August preceding the harvest, the parcels for which they renounce producing the controlled designation of origin. The defence and management body transmits this declaration to the approved control body as soon as possible.
#### 6. Declaration relating to vinification vat capacity
Every operator vinifying wines of the controlled designation of origin sends the defence and management body, at the latest on 31 August of the year of harvest, a declaration relating to the capacity of their vinification vats. The cellar plan annexed to the identification declaration may serve as the declaration relating to vinification vat capacity. This declaration is renewable by tacit renewal, unless changes are notified by the operator before 31 August of the year of harvest. In the event of a sale of grapes, this declaration specifies the destination of the harvest, in particular on the basis of the inter-trade contract entered into.
#### 7. Claim declaration
The claim declaration is lodged with the defence and management body at least 15 days before the first exit of products from the vinification cellar and at the latest 60 days after the date of placing on the market for the consumer.
It indicates:
- the designation claimed;
- the volume of the wine;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the name and address of the applicant;
- the place of storage of the wine.
It is accompanied by a copy of the harvest declaration and, as the case may be, a copy of the production declaration or an extract of the stock accounts for buyers of grapes and musts.
#### 8. Prior declaration of collections
Every operator wishing to sell in bulk a wine benefiting from the controlled designation of origin sends the approved control body a transaction declaration for the lot concerned between the day the transaction is contracted and at least eight days before collection. If the volume actually collected differs by more or less than 10 % from the volume indicated when the contract was concluded, the operator makes a correction available to the approved control body.
#### 9. Downgrading declaration
Every operator carrying out a downgrading of wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin must declare it to the defence and management body and to the approved control body within a maximum period of 15 days after this downgrading.
