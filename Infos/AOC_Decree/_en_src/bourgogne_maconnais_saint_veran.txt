# CHAPTER I
## I. – Name of the designation
Only wines meeting the specific provisions set out below may claim the controlled designation of origin “Saint-Véran”, initially recognised by the decree of 6 January 1971.
## II. – Geographical denominations and complementary terms
The name of the controlled designation of origin may be followed by the name of a smaller geographical unit known locally as a “climat”, provided that:
- it is the name of a registered lieu-dit (cadastral locality);
- it appears on the harvest declaration;
- the wines meet the production conditions laid down for the indication of a smaller geographical unit in the present specification.
## III. – Colour and types of product
The controlled designation of origin “Saint-Véran” is reserved for still white wines.
## IV. – Areas and zones in which the various operations are carried out
### 1° – Geographical area
The harvesting of the grapes, the vinification, the elaboration and the ageing of the wines are carried out within the territory of the following communes of the Saône-et-Loire department, on the basis of the official geographic code of the year 2021: Chânes, Chasselas, Davayé, Leynes, Prissé, Saint-Vérand and Solutré-Pouilly.
The cartographic documents representing the geographical area may be consulted on the website of the National Institute of Origin and Quality.
### 2° – Delimited parcel area
The wines come exclusively from vines situated within the delimited parcel production area as approved by the National Institute of Origin and Quality at the session of the competent national committee of 3 June 2021. The National Institute of Origin and Quality lodges with the town halls of the communes referred to in 1° the graphic documents establishing the parcel boundaries of the production area thus approved.

## V. – Grape varieties
The wines come solely from the chardonnay B variety.
## VI. – Management of the vineyard
### 1° – Training methods
a) – Planting density
The vines have a minimum planting density of 8,000 plants per hectare, with a spacing between rows less than or equal to 1.30 metres and a spacing between plants within the same row greater than or equal to 0.80 metre.
b) – Pruning rules
The wines come from vines pruned according to the following provisions:

| Long pruning (vines pruned in Guyot or in “taille à queue du Mâconnais”) | Vines pruned in single or double Guyot are pruned with a maximum of 10 free eyes per plant. c) – Trellising and foliage height rules
- The vines must be trellised and the trellising is maintained;
- The height of trellised foliage is at least equal to 0.6 times the spacing between rows, the height of trellised foliage being measured between the lower limit of the foliage, set at least 0.30 metre above the ground, and the upper trimming limit.
d) – Average maximum load per parcel
The average maximum load per parcel is set at 10,500 kilograms per hectare.
e) – Threshold of missing vines
The percentage of dead or missing vine plants referred to in Article D. 645-4 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code is set at 20 %.
f) – Cultivation condition of the vine
The parcels are managed so as to ensure a good overall cultivation condition of the vine, in particular its sanitary condition and the upkeep of its soil.
### 2° – Other cultivation practices
a) – In order to preserve the characteristics of the physical and biological environment, which constitutes a fundamental element of the terroir:
- Permanent grass cover of the headlands is compulsory;
- Any substantial modification of the morphology, the subsoil, the arable layer or the elements that guarantee the integrity and sustainability of the soils of a parcel intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin is prohibited, with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works;
- The destruction, within the vines, of existing cadoles (dry-stone huts), dry-stone walls, hedges and murgers (heaps of cleared stones) is prohibited.
b) – Vine plantings and replacements are carried out with healthy plant material that has undergone hot-water treatment or any other method making it possible to combat flavescence dorée.
### 3° – Irrigation
Irrigation is prohibited.

The benefit of the controlled designation of origin may only be granted to wines coming from:
- parcels of young vines only from the 2nd year following that in the course of which planting was carried out in place before 31 July;
- parcels of young vines only from the 1st year following that in the course of which grafting in place was carried out before 31 July;

By derogation, in the year following that in the course of which the top-grafting was carried out before 31 July, the variety permitted for the appellation may represent only 80 % of the planting of each parcel concerned.
## IX. – Processing, elaboration, ageing, packaging, storage
### 1° – General provisions
The wines are vinified in accordance with local, fair and constant usages.
a) – Analytical standards
Finished wines, ready to be released for consumption within the meaning of Article D. 645-18-I of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code, have a maximum fermentable sugar content (glucose + fructose) of:
- 3 grams per litre;
- or 4 grams per litre if the total acidity is greater than or equal to 55.10 milliequivalents per litre, i.e. 4.13 grams per litre expressed as tartaric acid (or 2.7 grams per litre expressed as H2SO4).
b) – Oenological practices and physical treatments
- The use of pieces of wood is prohibited;
- After enrichment, the wines have a total alcoholic strength by volume less than or equal to 13.5 %.
c) – Prohibited equipment
Continuous presses are prohibited.
d) – Vat capacity
Every operator has an overall vat capacity (vinification and storage) equivalent to at least the volume of wine vinified for the previous year’s harvest, as entered either on the harvest declaration, pro rata to the change in the holding’s area in production, or on the production declaration.
e) – Upkeep of the cellar and equipment
The cellar and the vinification equipment are well maintained; this is reflected in particular by:
- general hygiene of the elaboration premises, with general cleanliness, well-maintained floors, adequate drainage and a surface avoiding stagnation;
- harmlessness of the equipment and products coming into contact with the wine;
- separation and specificity of the premises: premises without the same functions must be separated, such as storage areas for plant-protection products, cleaning products or hydrocarbons, from the premises for vinification, ageing and storage of dry goods (corks, cartons);
- management of wine effluents: effluents must be removed as quickly as possible from foodstuff premises and deposited in well-maintained containers that are easy to clean and can be closed; waste storage areas must be kept clean; waste must be disposed of hygienically and in an environmentally friendly manner; a waste storage and removal area must be provided;
- absence of hazardous or odorous substances in the vinification, ageing and storage premises (odour).

## X. – Link with the geographical area
### 1° – Information on the geographical area
a) – Description of the natural factors contributing to the link
The geographical area is situated in the natural region of the “Monts du Mâconnais”, in the southern part of the Burgundy region. The emblematic limestone rocks of Solutré, a famous prehistoric site, and Vergisson, as well as the Mont de Pouilly, dominate the undulating and rugged landscape. The geographical area crosses diagonally the ridges at the southern end of the Mâconnais. The soils, of great diversity and developed on varied substrata, are mainly clay-limestone.
The parcels delimited for the harvesting of the grapes preferentially occupy hillsides of varying steepness, broadly facing the Saône plain and the south, at altitudes of between 200 metres and 450 metres, on Lower and Middle Jurassic marly subsoils and locally on Upper Jurassic soft limestones. The geographical area thus extends over 7 communes of the Saône-et-Loire department, about 10 kilometres south-west of Mâcon.
The soils of the parcels delimited for the harvesting of the grapes are generally lean, stony and very calcareous. In piedmont situations they are enriched by thin weathering formations of silty-clay nature, sometimes decarbonated at the surface.
The oceanic climate is marked by clear southern influences. Rainfall is evenly distributed over the year and does not exceed 800 millimetres. It is moderate during the vine’s period of activity. The natural barrier of the “Monts du Charollais” to the west partly protects the vines from wet oceanic influences and lets the geographical area benefit from mild air currents coming from the south along the Rhône corridor from the first fine days. The average annual temperature is 11 °C. The vineyard benefits greatly from the summer heat, which often lasts into the autumn.
b) – Description of the human factors contributing to the link
The presence of the vine in the Mâconnais is ancient. A. PELLETIER, in “La Saône, axe de civilisation”, recalls that boatmen were already carrying on the Saône, for the market of Lugdunum, “wine from Burgundy, where the vineyard has existed since the 1st century”.
Vine growing and the wine trade developed from the Middle Ages thanks to the great monastic orders, notably under the influence of Cluny, close to the geographical area. The archives of Mâcon reveal the existence, as early as 1606, of a “terrier” (parcel survey) from the Domaine de la Bâtie at Saint-Vérand, belonging to the chapter of the cathedral of Saint-Vincent de Mâcon, whose productive parcels were spread over the commune of Saint-Vérand and its surroundings.
Until the 18th century, the vineyard of “Saint-Véran”, like the whole of the Mâconnais, was planted mainly with the gamay N variety. From the end of the phylloxera crisis it was supplanted by the chardonnay B variety, which now represents almost the entire planting.
Jules GUYOT, in 1876, noted in his “Etude des vignobles de France”: “Saint-Vérand, Leynes and Chasselas produce the most delicate wines of the Mâconnais”. These three communes still belong to the geographical area.
In 1947 the “Amicale des Producteurs de Vin Blanc” was created; on the initiative of three men, Louis DAILLY, Georges CHAGNY and Louis ORIZET, it pursued the application for recognition of the controlled designation of origin “Saint-Véran”, which led to the decree of 6 January 1971. The controlled designation of origin takes up the original name of “Saint Véran”, bishop of Cavaillon at the end of the 6th century, who sat at the Council of Mâcon. He gave his name to the village of “Saint-Véran”, which became “Saint-Vérand des vignes” and then “Saint-Vérand”.
The vineyard of “Saint-Véran” is trained according to the usages in force in the south of the Mâconnais. The “taille à queue”, characteristic of the region and particularly suited to the chardonnay B variety, is still practised. The wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin “Saint-Véran” are produced from the single variety chardonnay B, with low yields. Ageing is relatively short, at least until 1 February of the year following that of the harvest, in order to highlight the youthful character of the wine.
The diversity of soils and situations introduces nuances that are revealed in the wines benefiting from some forty “climat” names (name given locally to a lieu-dit), enriching the palette of wines with localised expressions.
In 2009 the vineyard covered about 700 hectares and was worked by several hundred producers cultivating very fragmented parcels. Two thirds of the wines are sold directly in bottle.
### 2° – Information on the quality and characteristics of the product
“Saint-Véran” generally displays a fine green-gold robe supporting a wine of great finesse, often showing delicate nuances of white flowers and white-fleshed fruit. It expresses the full range of its qualities after a few years of ageing, then frequently evolving towards notes of hazelnut or toasted almond, enhanced by delicate touches of honey, vanilla or gunflint. The structure on the palate is ample and complex, with a successful balance between power and elegance.
### 3° – Causal interactions
The chardonnay B variety has adapted perfectly to the soils of the geographical area, where the grapes ripen easily. The eastward exposure of the hillsides shelters the vines from westerly winds and allows them to warm up from dawn with the first rays of the sun. The uniformity of the limestone bedrock, on which varied soils have formed, favours a harmonious yet varied development of the aromas of the chardonnay B variety.
The expressions of the natural environment are varied but always give rise to rich wines of well-asserted originality, whose common denominator is fleshiness and fruit on the palate, asserting their southern accents within the palette of Burgundy white wines.
Producers’ efforts are constant, and know-how has been perpetuated to respect the character of the wines inherited from the nature of the soils, and the usages specific to the Mâconnais, both in the vineyard and in the cellars. The “taille à queue”, characteristic of the Mâconnais, contributes to the distinctiveness of the landscapes of the geographical area. The great fragmentation of the parcels, as well as the small size and large number of holdings, contribute to the expression of the wines in a particularly subtle range of shades, well highlighted by the customary addition of the names of the “climats” from which the cuvées come.
This small region of the Mâconnais was made famous by the annual pilgrimage of the President of the French Republic François MITTERRAND to the famous rock of Solutré, from which he admired the wine landscape. Producers are very attached to the rocks of Solutré and Vergisson, symbols of the geographical area that appear on many labels and promotional logos.
“Saint-Véran” is one of the flagship vineyards of the Mâconnais, whose international renown is attested in particular by shipments beyond national borders to northern Europe and by exports to Quebec, where it is particularly well known.
## XI. – Transitional measures
### 1° – Planting density
Vine parcels in place on 31 July 2009 with a planting density of between 6,000 and 8,000 plants per hectare continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up.
### 2° – Pruning rules
Vine parcels in place before 31 August 1975 and pruned in the so-called “taille à queue du Mâconnais” may be pruned in such a way that, at the phenological stage corresponding to 11 or 12 leaves, each plant bears a maximum of 28 fruit-bearing shoots of the year, including 2 canes each bearing a maximum of 12 fruit-bearing shoots of the year.
## XII. – Rules of presentation and labelling
### 1° – General provisions
Wines for which, under the terms of the present specification, the controlled designation of origin “Saint-Véran” is claimed and which are presented under the said appellation may not be declared after the harvest, offered to the public, dispatched, put up for sale or sold unless, in the harvest declaration, in advertisements, on leaflets, labels, invoices and any containers whatsoever, the aforesaid designation of origin is entered.
### 2° – Particular provisions
a) – The name of the smaller geographical unit is placed immediately after the name of the controlled designation of origin and printed in characters whose dimensions, both in height and in width, are not larger than those of the characters making up the name of the controlled designation of origin.
b) – The labelling of wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin may specify the larger geographical unit “Vin de Bourgogne” or “Grand Vin de Bourgogne”.
c) – Where the grape variety is indicated on the labelling, this indication does not appear in the same field of vision as the compulsory particulars and is printed in characters whose dimensions do not exceed 2 millimetres.
# CHAPTER II
## I. – Declaration obligations
#### 1. Claim declaration
The claim declaration is sent to the defence and management body at least fifteen days before circulation between authorised warehousekeepers and at the latest on 10 December of the year of harvest.
It indicates in particular:
- the designation claimed;
- the volume of the wine;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the name and address of the applicant;
- the place of storage of the wine.
It is accompanied in particular by a copy of the harvest declaration and, as the case may be, a copy of the production declaration or an extract of the stock accounts for buyers of grapes and musts.
#### 2. Declaration prior to transaction and collections
Every operator wishing to sell in bulk a wine benefiting from the controlled designation of origin makes a transaction declaration for the lot concerned to the approved control body within time limits set in the control plan of between six and fifteen working days before any collection.
This declaration, accompanied where applicable by a copy of the purchase contract, specifies in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the identification of the lot;
- the volume of the lot;
- the identification of the containers;
- the identity of the buyer.
If collections are made for volumes smaller than those set out in the transaction declaration, the operator informs the approved control body in writing.
#### 3. Declaration of release for consumption
Every operator declares to the approved control body each lot of wine intended to be released for consumption within the meaning of Article D. 645-18-I of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code.
This declaration may also be drawn up for lots already packaged. It is made within time limits set in the control plan of between six and fifteen working days before release for consumption or before the lots concerned are dispatched from the operator’s cellars.
It specifies in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the identification of the lot;
- the volume of the lot;
- the lot number for wines already packaged;
- the identification of the containers for non-packaged wines.
However, making available to the approved control body the register referred to in Article D. 645-18 II of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code serves as the declaration of release for consumption, under the conditions set out in the control plan.
#### 4. Declaration relating to the dispatch outside the national territory of a non-packaged wine
Every operator wishing to dispatch outside the national territory a non-packaged wine benefiting from the controlled designation of origin declares it to the approved control body within time limits set in the control plan of between six and fifteen working days before any dispatch.
#### 5. Fallback declaration
Every operator carrying out a fallback of a wine benefiting from the controlled designation of origin into a more general controlled designation of origin sends the defence and management body and the approved control body a monthly declaration at the latest on the 1st working day following the tenth day of each month.
This declaration indicates in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the more general controlled designation of origin of the fallback;
- the volume concerned by the fallback;
- the vintage;
- the state of the lot concerned (bulk, bottle, hermetically sealed vacuum containers…).
The approved control body transmits without delay a copy of the monthly declaration to the approved control body for the more general controlled designation of origin concerned.
The defence and management body transmits without delay to the defence and management body of the more general controlled designation of origin concerned the list of operators who have made a monthly fallback declaration, together with a summary statement indicating:
- the more general controlled designation of origin of the fallback;
- the volume concerned by the fallback;
- the vintage(s);
- the state of the lot(s) concerned (bulk, bottle, hermetically sealed vacuum containers…).
#### 6. Downgrading declaration
Every operator carrying out a downgrading of wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin sends the defence and management body and the approved control body a monthly declaration within time limits set in the control plan. This declaration indicates in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the volume downgraded;
- the vintage;
- the date of the downgrading.
#### 7. Reworking of parcels
Before any development or any works liable to modify the morphology, the subsoil, the arable layer (including any addition of exogenous soil) or the elements that guarantee the integrity and sustainability of the soils of a parcel intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin, with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works, the operator sends a declaration to the defence and management body at least four weeks before the planned start date of the works.
The defence and management body transmits, without delay, a copy of this declaration to the services of the National Institute of Origin and Quality.
## II. – Keeping of registers
#### General plan of the storage and vinification places
Every vinifying operator keeps up to date and available for the approved control body a general plan of the storage and vinification places, allowing in particular the number, designation and capacity of the containers to be identified.
