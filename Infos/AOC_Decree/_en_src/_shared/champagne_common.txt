@define CH_VARIETIES
### 1° – Grape varieties
The wines come exclusively from the following varieties:
- main varieties: Arbane B, Chardonnay B, Chardonnay rose Rs, Meunier N, Petit meslier B, Pinot blanc B, Pinot gris G, Pinot noir N;
- and the variety “of interest for adaptation purposes”: Voltis B, subject to the signature between the INAO, the ODG, the authorised operator concerned, the operator-vinifier and the producer (élaborateur) of an agreement conforming to the framework agreement approved by the competent national committee of 2 June 2021.
### 2° – Proportion rule on the holding
a) The proportion of the main varieties is greater than or equal to 95 % of the planting.
b) The proportion of the variety “of interest for adaptation purposes” is less than or equal to 5 % of the planting.
In order to encourage the reduction of plant-protection inputs in areas adjoining inhabited/urbanised zones, the areas planted with Voltis and situated less than 20 metres from the places mentioned in Article L253-7-1, in I of Article L253-7 and in III of Article L253-8 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code are not taken into account in calculating the areas of varieties “of interest for adaptation purposes” subject to the limit of 5 % of the area of the holding declared under the AOC.

@define CH_DENSITY
a) Planting density
*General provisions:*
The vines are planted with a spacing between rows that may not exceed 2.00 metres. The spacing between plants within the same row is between 0.70 metre and 1.50 metres.
The sum of the spacing between rows and the spacing between plants within the same row may not exceed 3.00 metres.
Any transformation of the parcel resulting in a change in planting density is prohibited until it is grubbed up.
*Particular provisions:*
In order to allow the passage of suitable machinery, parcels with:
- either a slope greater than 35 %,
- or a slope greater than 25 % combined with a cross-slope greater than 10 %,
may have alleys between 1.50 metres and 3 metres wide, at a maximum frequency of one row in six. In this case, the sum of the spacing between the other rows and the spacing between plants within the same row may not exceed 2.30 metres.
b) Pruning rules
A “free eye” means a bud separated from the base of the cane or “crown”, whatever the length of the internode.
Any overlapping between plants and any superposition of fruiting branches is prohibited.
The number of free eyes is less than or equal to 18 eyes per square metre.
Pruning is carried out at the latest before phenological stage (F) (Lorenz 12), i.e. four leaves unfolded.
The vines are pruned according to the following techniques:

c) Trellising rule
The trellising system is in place at the latest in the year of entry into production under the controlled designation of origin.
d) Tying
Tying is completed before phenological stage (I) (Lorenz 23), known as “flowering”, and is consistent with the pruning system adopted.
e) Lifting
Lifting the shoots held by wires is compulsory. It is carried out at the latest at phenological stage (L) (Lorenz stage 33), known as “bunch closure”.
f) Foliage height
The height of trellised foliage, after trimming, is at least equal to 0.6 times the spacing between rows. It is measured between the tying wire and the upper trimming limit.
g) Average maximum load per parcel
The average maximum load per parcel is set at 19,700 kilograms of grapes per hectare.
The number of bunches per square metre of vine in production is less than or equal to 17.
h) Threshold of missing vines
The percentage of dead or missing vine plants referred to in Article D. 645-4 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code is set at 20 %.
i) Cultivation condition of the vine
The parcels are managed so as to ensure a good overall cultivation condition of the vine, in particular its sanitary condition and the upkeep of its soil.
### 2° – Other cultivation practices
In order to preserve the characteristics of the soils, which constitute a fundamental element of the terroir:
a) Permanent grass cover of the headlands is compulsory;
b) The use of composts and organic household waste, and of sewage sludge other than that from wine-making installations, alone or mixed, is prohibited;
c) The maximum width of the strip that may be chemically weeded is 40 centimetres on either side of the vine row;
d) The inter-row must have a plant cover, spontaneous or sown, from 30 November to 31 January of the following year;
e) Vine plantings and replacements are carried out with plants all of whose components (scions and rootstocks) have been hot-water treated before grafting. Failing this, the plants are hot-water treated before planting. Hot-water treatment must be carried out in a station approved by FranceAgriMer;
f) Any substantial modification of the morphology, the subsoil or the elements that guarantee the integrity and sustainability of the soils of a parcel intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin is prohibited, with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works.
### 3° – Irrigation
Irrigation is prohibited.

@define CH_NATURAL
They form part of a landscape characterised by a hillside vineyard lying on the cuestas of the eastern Paris Basin, imposing geomorphological structures:
– the Côte d’Île-de-France in the Marne, together with the slopes of the associated valleys, grouping from north to south the Montagne de Reims, the Marne valley (which extends into the south of the Aisne and as far as Seine-et-Marne), the Côte des Blancs and the Côte du Sézannais for the most emblematic sectors;
– the Côte de Champagne, with the Marne Vitryat and the Aube sector of Montgueux;
– the Côte des Bar, cut by many valleys, bringing together the Bar-sur-Aubois to the east and the Barséquanais to the west, in the Aube and Haute-Marne.
This typical cuesta relief, with its adjacent valleys, presents hillsides facing east and south, sometimes north, as for the northern Montagne de Reims and the left bank of the Marne valley. The scarp crests are made up of hard layers of limestone or chalk. The slopes of the hillsides are chalky, marly or sandy, softer, cleared by erosion and then covered with colluvial deposits coming from the overlying scarp crests.
