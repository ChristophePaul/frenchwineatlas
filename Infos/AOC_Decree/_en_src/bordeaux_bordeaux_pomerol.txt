# CHAPTER I
## I. – Name of the designation
Only wines meeting the specific provisions set out below may claim the controlled designation of origin “Pomerol”, initially recognised by the decree of 8 December 1936.
## II. – Geographical denominations and complementary terms
No specific provision.
## III. – Colour and types of product
The controlled designation of origin “Pomerol” is reserved for still red wines.
## IV. – Areas and zones in which the various operations are carried out
### 1° – Geographical area
The geographical area, on the basis of the official geographic code dated 1 January 2021, is limited to the territory of the following communes and parts of communes of the Gironde department:
a) the commune of Pomerol,
b) the part of the territory of the commune of Libourne provided for by the judgment of the Bordeaux civil court of 29 December 1928, bounded to the north by the river la Barbanne, to the east by the boundary of the commune of Pomerol, to the south by the Tailhas stream, to the west by departmental road 910, the boulevard Beauséjour, the avenue Georges Clémenceau, the rue du Docteur-Nard, the avenue de l’Europe and the Libourne-to-Bergerac railway line,
c) the parcel situated in the commune of Lalande-de-Pomerol mentioned in annex 1-A, which produces wines of the controlled designation of origin “Pomerol” by virtue of the judgment of the Bordeaux civil court of 29 December 1928,
d) the parcels listed in annex 1-B for the commune of Saint-Emilion,
e) the parcels listed in annex 1-C for the following communes: Les Artigues-de-Lussac, Lalande-de-Pomerol, Libourne, Lussac, Montagne, Néac and Saint-Emilion.
The harvesting of the grapes is authorised on the territories referred to in the preceding paragraphs a), b), c) and d). The vinification, the elaboration and the ageing of the wines are authorised on the territories referred to in the preceding paragraphs a), b), c) and e).
### 2° – Delimited parcel area
The wines come exclusively from vines situated within the delimited parcel production area as approved by the National Institute of Origin and Quality at the sessions of the competent national committee of 3 September 1936, of 3 and 4 February 2000 and of 2 June 2021, as well as in the parcel of the commune of Lalande-de-Pomerol indicated in annex 1-A.
The National Institute of Origin and Quality lodges with the town halls of the communes referred to in 1° the graphic documents establishing the parcel boundaries of the production area thus approved.
## V. – Grape varieties
The wines come from the following varieties: cabernet franc N, cabernet-sauvignon N, cot N (or malbec or pressac), merlot N, petit verdot N.
## VI. – Management of the vineyard
### 1° – Training methods
a) – Planting density.
The vines present a minimum planting density of 5,500 plants per hectare. These vines may not present a spacing between rows greater than 2 metres nor a spacing between plants within the same row of less than 0.80 metre.
b) – Pruning rules.
Pruning is carried out at the latest by the spread-leaves stage (Lorenz stage 9) and according to the following techniques:
- single Guyot or double Guyot pruning;
- spur pruning (cots) in Royat cordon;
- long-wood (cane) pruning (astes).
Each plant bears a maximum of 10 free eyes.
c) – Trellising rules and foliage height.
The height of the trellised foliage is at least equal to 0.6 times the spacing between rows, the height of the trellised foliage being measured at its lower limit at 0.10 metre below the bending wire and at its upper limit at the topping height.
d) – Average maximum load per parcel.
The average maximum load per parcel is fixed at 8,000 kilograms per hectare. Where irrigation is authorised in accordance with the provisions of Article D. 645-5 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code, the average maximum load per parcel of the irrigated parcels is fixed at 5,500 kilograms per hectare.
e) – Threshold for missing vines.
The percentage of dead or missing vine plants referred to in Article D. 645-4 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code is fixed at 10 % until the 25th campaign following that of the planting of the vine. This percentage is raised to 20 % from the 26th campaign following that of the planting of the vine. Dead plants must be removed from the parcels; any storage of these dead plants on the parcels is prohibited.
f) – Cultivation condition of the vine.
The parcels are managed so as to ensure a good overall cultivation condition of the vine, in particular its sanitary condition, the upkeep of its soil and the sanitary condition of the harvest. In particular, no parcel is left abandoned.
### 2° – Other cultivation practices
In order to preserve the characteristics of the physical and biological environment that constitutes a fundamental element of the terroir:
- any substantial modification of the morphology of the relief and of the natural pedological sequence of the parcels intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin is prohibited, with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works;
- the addition of exogenous soil onto parcels of the delimited parcel area is prohibited. Exogenous soil means soil that does not come from the delimited parcel area of the appellation;
- the banks and headlands are grassed;
- chemical weeding is prohibited. The control of the grass cover of the parcels is carried out solely by mechanical or physical means;
- every operator calculates and records its Treatment Frequency Index (IFT).
### 3° – Irrigation
Irrigation during the vine’s growing period may be authorised, in accordance with the provisions of Article D. 645-5 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code, only in the event of persistent drought and when this disturbs the good physiological development of the vine and the good ripening of the grapes.
## VII. – Harvest, transport and ripeness of the grapes
### 1° – Harvest
The wines come from grapes harvested at proper ripeness and presenting a good sanitary condition.
### 2° – Ripeness of the grapes
a) – Sugar content of the grapes. Grapes with a sugar content lower than the following may not be considered to be at proper ripeness:
- 194 grams per litre of must for merlot;
- 180 grams per litre of must for the other varieties.
b) – Minimum natural alcoholic strength by volume. The wines present a minimum natural alcoholic strength by volume of 11 %.
## VIII. – Yields. – Entry into production
### 1° – Yield
The yield referred to in Article D. 645-7 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code is fixed at 49 hectolitres per hectare.
### 2° – Limit yield
The limit yield referred to in Article D. 645-7 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code is fixed at 60 hectolitres per hectare.
### 3° – Entry into production of young vines
The benefit of the controlled designation of origin may be granted to wines coming from:
- parcels of young vines only from the second year following that in the course of which planting was carried out in place before 31 July;
- parcels of young vines only from the first year following that in the course of which grafting in place was carried out before 31 July;
- parcels of vines that have been top-grafted, at the earliest in the first year following that in the course of which the top-grafting was carried out before 31 July, and once the parcels contain only varieties permitted for the appellation. By way of derogation, in the year following that in the course of which the top-grafting was carried out before 31 July, the varieties permitted for the appellation may represent only 80 % of the grape varieties of each parcel concerned.
## IX. – Processing, elaboration, ageing, packaging, storage
### 1° – General provisions
The wines are vinified in accordance with local, fair and constant usages.
a) – Malolactic fermentation. Every lot of wine marketed (in bulk or packaged) presents a malic acid content less than or equal to 0.30 gram per litre.
b) – Analytical standards. Every lot of wine marketed (in bulk or packaged) presents a content of fermentable sugars (glucose + fructose) less than or equal to 2 grams per litre. Every lot of wine marketed in bulk presents a volatile acidity content less than or equal to 13.26 milliequivalents per litre, i.e. 0.79 gram per litre expressed as acetic acid (0.65 gram per litre expressed as H2SO4) until 31 July of the year following that of the harvest, and less than or equal to 16.30 milliequivalents per litre, i.e. 0.97 gram per litre expressed as acetic acid (0.80 gram per litre expressed as H2SO4) after that date. Every lot of wine marketed in bulk presents a total sulphur dioxide content less than or equal to 140 milligrams per litre.
c) – Oenological practices and physical treatments. Subtractive enrichment techniques (SET) are authorised within the limit of a concentration rate of 15 %. After enrichment, the wines do not exceed a total alcoholic strength by volume of 13.5 %.
d) – Prohibited equipment. The use of the foulo-benne (self-emptying skip fitted with a so-called centrifugal paddle pump) is prohibited. The use of a continuous-type press fitted with an endless screw of a diameter of less than 400 millimetres is prohibited.
e) – Vat capacity. Every operator has a vinification vat capacity at least equal to 1.5 times the average ten-year claimed production of the holding. The vinification capacity available at the time of harvest corresponds to the vinification containers.
f) – Overall upkeep of the cellar and equipment. The cellar (floors and walls) and the vinification equipment present a good general condition of upkeep.
### 2° – Provisions relating to ageing
The wines are aged at least until 1 November of the year following that of the harvest.
### 3° – Provisions relating to packaging
a) – The wines are packaged from 1 November of the year following that of the harvest.
b) – For each packaged lot, the operator keeps available to the defence and management body and the approved control body:
- the information appearing in the register of handling operations referred to in Article D. 645-18 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code;
- an analysis carried out before or after packaging.
The analysis reports are kept for a period of six months from the date of packaging.
### 4° – Provisions relating to storage
The operator demonstrates a place suitable for the storage of the packaged products. A suitable place for the storage of packaged products means any place sheltered from bad weather (wind, rain) and protected from any contamination.
### 5° – Provisions relating to placing on the market for the consumer
At the end of the ageing period, the wines are placed on the market for the consumer from 15 November of the year following that of the harvest.
## X. – Link with the geographical area
### 1° – Information on the geographical area
#### a) – Description of the natural factors contributing to the link
The geographical area of the controlled designation of origin “Pomerol” comprises the territories of the commune of Pomerol and of the northern part of that of Libourne, in the north-east of the Gironde department. This geographical area adjoins those of “Saint-Emilion” to the south-east and of “Lalande de Pomerol” to the north, from which it is separated by the La Barbanne stream.
The relatively flat relief of the appellation, sloping little by little towards the valley of the Isle at its confluence with the Dordogne, is the result of the total destruction of the Tertiary carbonate formations by the river Isle, whose course today lies further to the west. There followed the deposit of various alluvial sheets during the Quaternary. These detrital materials, coming from the Massif Central, formed terraces of north-south orientation parallel to the course of the Isle. From east to west, between 30 and 40 metres in altitude, the high terrace of the Günz is composed of reddened sands, gravels and pebbles resting, at variable depths, on the Tertiary clay formations rich in smectites; then, between 20 and 25 metres, the middle terraces of the Riss rise in tiers, of sands, gravels and pebbles in a sandy-clay matrix. On the slopes and lower slopes spread the colluvial sandy-clay and gravelly formations on sands, gravels and pebbles, then on sandy clays.
The climate, like that of the whole of the Gironde, on the edge of the Atlantic Ocean, is temperate oceanic, with moderate temperature ranges favouring the cultivation of the vine. The situation of the geographical area at the confluence of the courses of the Isle and the Dordogne also explains the regulation of temperatures. Its situation in the north-east of the Gironde gives it continental nuances, perceptible through higher temperatures in summer and autumn, favouring the ripening of the grapes.
The landscape of the “Pomerol” appellation is characterised by a great territorial homogeneity, very sober, entirely covered with vineyards of gentle relief, dotted with elegant wine châteaux or more humble dwellings of white limestone and dominated by the spire of the church of its village.
#### b) – Description of the human factors contributing to the link
The Pomerol plateau was crossed by two ancient roads in Roman times. This period marks the beginnings of wine consumption in Gaul and of the establishment of the vine. But the development of the vineyard only took place from the 12th century, with the installation of the first commandery of the Order of the Hospitallers of Saint John of Jerusalem in the Pomerol region. The hospice of Pomerol was a stage for the pilgrims of Santiago de Compostela, where the wines comforted them and gave them courage for the stages to come.
Destroyed during the Hundred Years’ War, the vineyard was rebuilt, then renewed in the middle of the 18th century. A record, drawn up on 7 May 1761 at the request of a bourgeois merchant in Libourne, Louis Léonard FONTEMOING, establishes that his Pomerol vineyard, based on white varieties, had been replanted in 8 years with red varieties: noir de pressac (cot N), bouchet (cabernet franc N) and merlot N. For professor Henri ENJALBERT this is undoubtedly the first document concerning the modernisation of viticulture, and he adds: “No text, so old, so precise, exists in the Bordeaux area on this subject. Pomerol is a nobility that I had not dared to imagine.” (ENJALBERT, H. Les Grands Vins de Saint-Emilion, Pomerol et Fronsac. 1983).
At the end of the 19th century, after the phylloxera crisis, the replantings profoundly transformed the vineyard; merlot N then became predominant in the region, because grafting regularises its production and it proves particularly suited to the soils of Pomerol. The immigration of people from the Corrèze, leaving the poverty of their region to settle as wine merchants in the Libourne area, and the construction of the Libourne-Paris railway line, favoured the sale of these wines in the North of France, then in Belgium. They bought numerous estates and rapidly made them known throughout the world. From 1900 the wine-growers of Pomerol grouped together to work together and to carry out joint actions and purchases, creating a “Wine-growing and Agricultural Union” with a double role: the pooling of techniques for the improvement of quality and the defence of the name “Pomerol”. They wished in particular to prevent the wine-growers of the neighbouring communes from “stamping their barrels with the name of Pomerol”. A judgment of the Gironde court dated 29 December 1928 made it possible to fix the geographical limits of the use of the name “Pomerol”. It is this production area that was taken up in 1936 when the “Pomerol” appellation was recognised by decree. The vineyard developed until the vine covered the whole agricultural space of the appellation. Production there remains most often family-run, alongside a few large estates, as in all the appellations of the Libourne area. In 2009, the 140 holdings had an average area of six hectares and the vineyard of the “Pomerol” appellation covered 800 hectares, for an average production of 35,000 hectolitres per year.
### 2° – Information on the quality and characteristics of the product
The wines of the controlled designation of origin “Pomerol” are still red wines. The merlot N variety, very largely predominant, gives the wines intense colours and powerful noses with a wide aromatic palette, evoking black fruits, sometimes with notes of violet and truffle. These wines are ample and of great length on the palate, without hardness, and can withstand long ageing in bottle. The wines of the appellation are elegant and balanced. The varieties cabernet franc N, cabernet-sauvignon N and cot N (or malbec) are often present, bringing structure and complexity to the wines. The petit verdot N variety, an old Bordeaux variety, is also authorised but infrequent.
### 3° – Causal interactions
The parcel production area delimits the parcels that are healthy or easy to drain, either because of the texture of the soil or because of their topographic position (knoll or slope) allowing a normal run-off of water. Excluded are parcels situated on hydromorphic land and on pseudogley soils, which present an impermeable layer at shallow depth, and parcels situated in valley bottoms presenting signs of hydromorphy and subject to an unfavourable microclimate (frost-prone, damp…). Also excluded are sandy, flat parcels resting on a clay subsoil. Moreover, in order to preserve the essential characteristics of the physical and biological environment, any substantial modification of the morphology of the relief and of the natural pedological sequence of the parcels intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin, as well as any addition of exogenous soil, are prohibited, and the grassing of the banks and “headlands” (tournières), the vegetated strip around the parcel, is compulsory in order to limit soil erosion.
These carefully selected parcels allow the optimal expression of the local varieties, chosen over the course of history for their aptitude for keeping and ageing, linked to the need to transport these products over long distances. It is the Tertiary clay substrate, a cool horizon inducing a slow ripening of the grapes and a very regulated water supply to the vine; the more or less compact or sandy surface gravels, draining and reflecting light towards the foliage; and the subsoil containing iron oxides or “crasse de fer”, bringing specific mineral elements, which, with the benefit of a favourable climate, give the wines of Pomerol their irreplaceable personality.
The red wines must present a sufficient structure, obtained through the optimal ripeness of merlot N, the dominant variety, in particular on the gravelly soils with a clay matrix where it best expresses the characteristics of the wines. The varieties cabernet franc N and cabernet-sauvignon N find their best expression on the most gravelly soils. The generalisation of trellising and a sufficiently severe pruning method make it possible to guarantee a good distribution of the harvest and a leaf surface sufficient for photosynthesis, ensuring optimal ripeness. In order to ensure a satisfactory harvest, without overloading the vine plants, a guarantee of concentration of the fruit, the planting density is high. These rigorous production conditions must lead to wines expressing all the qualities of the terroir from which they come. They are aged at least until 1 November of the year following that of the harvest, this long ageing period being necessary for their stabilisation, their refinement and their better expression before being placed on the market.
The renown of the wines of Pomerol remained confidential until the second half of the 19th century. Today, it is the dynamism of people who, overcoming difficult periods through the improvement of their practices, have been able to find the ideal match of soil and variety at the origin of wines whose fame goes around the planet. The landscapes of this appellation are entirely devoted, without ostentation, to this single production, and all local activity is closely attached to it.
## XI. – Transitional measures
### 1° – Delimited parcel area
The parcels mentioned in annex 2, planted with vines before 3 February 2000 and excluded from the delimited parcel area as defined in IV (2°) above, continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin “Pomerol” until they are grubbed up and at the latest until the 2025 harvest inclusive, subject to meeting the other provisions of the present specification.
### 2° – Planting density
Parcels of vines in place at the date of 31 July 2009 and presenting a planting density between 5,000 plants per hectare and 5,500 plants per hectare continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up, subject to compliance with the trellising and foliage-height rules laid down in the present specification.
## XII. – Rules of presentation and labelling
a) – Wines for which, under the terms of the present specification, the controlled designation of origin “Pomerol” is claimed and which are presented under the said appellation may not be declared after the harvest, offered to the public, dispatched, put up for sale or sold unless the aforesaid controlled designation of origin is entered in the harvest declaration, in advertisements, on leaflets, labels, invoices and any containers whatsoever.
b) – The labelling of the wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin may specify the larger geographical unit “Vin de Bordeaux” or “Grand Vin de Bordeaux”. The dimensions of the characters of the larger geographical unit are not greater, either in height or in width, than two thirds of those of the characters composing the name of the controlled designation of origin.
# CHAPTER II
## I. – Declaration obligations
### 1. Claim declaration
The claim declaration is sent to the defence and management body before 15 January of the year following the harvest year.
It indicates:
- the designation claimed;
- the volume of the wine;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the name and address of the applicant;
- the place of storage of the wine.
It is accompanied by:
- a copy of the harvest declaration and, as the case may be, a copy of the production declaration or an extract of the stock accounts for buyers of grapes and musts;
- and the cellar plan (place of vinification and storage), allowing in particular the number, designation and capacity of the containers to be identified.
### 2. Prior declaration of removals or packaging
Every operator wishing to move or package wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin declares to the approved control body any bulk removal or packaging operation at least five working days before the operation.
Any operator who packages at least once a week for more than nine consecutive months in the year is considered a continuous packager. This operator is exempt from the prior declaration for each operation but sends a copy of the handling register to the approved control body every six months.
Any operator who packages at least once a week for more than three months in the year and less than nine successive months in the year is considered a semi-continuous packager. This operator is exempt from the prior declaration for each operation but declares the packaging period(s) and sends a copy of the handling register to the approved control body at the end of the indicated period.
### 3. Fallback (repli) declaration
Every operator marketing a wine benefiting from the controlled designation of origin under a more general appellation declares it to the defence and management body and to the approved control body within a maximum period of one month after this fallback.
### 4. Downgrading declaration
Every operator downgrading wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin declares it to the defence and management body and to the approved control body within a maximum period of one month after this downgrading.
### 5. Reworking of parcels
Before any addition of soil or amendment, any development or any works modifying the soil profile or the morphology of the relief, or any modification of the landscape, and with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works, a declaration is sent by the operator to the defence and management body at least two months before the start of the envisaged works.
The defence and management body transmits, without delay, a copy of this declaration to the services of the National Institute of Origin and Quality.
## II. – Keeping of registers
Tasting register:
All packaged lots undergo an organoleptic examination before and after packaging in accordance with the arrangements provided for in the inspection plan, the results of which are recorded in a tasting register.
