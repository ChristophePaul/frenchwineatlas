# CHAPTER I
## I. – Name of the designation
Only wines meeting the specific provisions set out below may claim the controlled designation of origin “Beaujolais”, initially recognised by the decree of 12 September 1937.
## II. – Geographical denominations and complementary terms
1° – The name of the controlled designation of origin may be followed by the mention “supérieur” for wines meeting the production conditions laid down for this mention in the present specification.
2° – The name of the controlled designation of origin may be followed by the mention “Villages” for wines meeting the production conditions laid down for this mention in the present specification.
3° – The name of the controlled designation of origin may be followed by the name of the commune of origin of the grapes for wines meeting the production conditions laid down in the present specification.
4° – The name of the controlled designation of origin, whether or not followed by the mention “Villages” or by the name of the commune of origin of the grapes, may be supplemented by the mention “primeur” or “nouveau” for wines meeting the conditions laid down for this mention in the present specification.
## III. – Colour and types of product
The controlled designation of origin “Beaujolais”, whether or not followed by the mention “Villages” or by the name of the commune of origin of the grapes, is reserved for still white, rosé and red wines.
The mention “supérieur” is reserved for red wines.
The mention “primeur” or “nouveau” is reserved for rosé and red wines.

## V. – Grape varieties
### 1° – Grape varieties
a) – White wines are produced from the chardonnay B variety alone.
b) – Red and rosé wines are produced from the following varieties:
- main variety: gamay N;
- accessory varieties: aligoté B, chardonnay B, gamay de Bouze N, gamay de Chaudenay N, melon B, pinot gris G and pinot noir N.
### 2° – Proportion rules on the holding
Compliance of the varietal composition is assessed, for the colour concerned, over all the parcels of the holding producing the wine of the appellation.
- The proportion of the accessory varieties gamay de Bouze N and gamay de Chaudenay N, together or separately, is less than or equal to 10 % of the planting.
- The other accessory varieties are authorised only as mixed plantings within the vines, and their total proportion is limited to 15 % within each parcel.
## VI. – Management of the vineyard
### 1° – Training methods
a) – Planting density
The vines have a minimum planting density of 5,000 plants per hectare. The spacing between rows may not exceed 2.50 metres and the spacing between plants within the same row may not be less than 0.80 metre. From the date of approval of the present specification, the grubbing-up of rows within a parcel may not lead to a spacing between the widest rows of more than 3 metres.
b) – Pruning rules
Pruning is completed by 15 May.
The wines come from vines pruned according to the following provisions:
| COLOUR OF THE WINES | PRUNING RULES |
| White wines | – either simple Guyot pruning with a maximum of 8 free eyes on the cane and one spur with a maximum of 2 free eyes; – or the so-called “taille à queue du Mâconnais” pruning: each plant bears one cane with a maximum of 12 free eyes and one spur with a maximum of 2 free eyes. |
| Red and rosé wines | With a maximum of 10 free eyes per plant: – either short pruning (trained in gobelet, fan, or single, double or “charmet” Royat cordon) with 3 to 5 spurs with a maximum of 2 free eyes each. With a view to rejuvenation, each plant may also bear a spur with a maximum of 2 free eyes pruned on a water shoot growing from old wood; – or simple Guyot pruning, with a maximum of 6 free eyes on the cane and one spur with a maximum of 2 free eyes; – or with 2 short canes with a maximum of 3 free eyes each. During formative pruning or a change of pruning method, the vines are pruned with a maximum of 12 free eyes per plant. |
c) – Trellising and foliage height rules
| COLOUR OF THE WINES | TRELLISING AND FOLIAGE HEIGHT RULES |
| White wines | The vines are trained on a fixed trellis. The trellising is careful, solid and inert, with at least one carrying wire and one pair of lifting wires. The height of trellised foliage must be at least equal to 0.6 times the average spacing between rows, the height of trellised foliage being measured between the lower limit of the foliage, set at least 0.30 metre above the ground, and the upper trimming limit, set at least 0.20 metre above the upper trellising wire(s). |
| Red and rosé wines | a) – Where the average spacing between rows is greater than or equal to 1.50 metres, the vines are trained on a fixed trellis. b) – The trellising is careful, solid and inert, with at least one carrying wire and one pair of lifting wires. c) – The height of trellised foliage must be at least equal to 0.6 times the average spacing between rows, the height of trellised foliage being measured between the lower limit of the foliage, set at least 0.30 metre above the ground, and the upper trimming limit, set at least 0.20 metre above the upper trellising wire(s). d) – For untrellised vines, the height of foliage must also be at least equal to 0.6 times the average spacing between rows, the height of foliage being measured from at least 0.10 metre above the ground. |
d) – Average maximum load per parcel
The average maximum load per parcel is set at:
- 11,000 kilograms per hectare for white wines;
- 10,000 kilograms per hectare for red and rosé wines.
e) – Threshold of missing vines
The percentage of dead or missing vine plants referred to in Article D. 644-22 of the Rural Code is set at 20 %.
f) – Cultivation condition of the vine
The parcels are managed so as to ensure a good overall cultivation condition of the vine, in particular its sanitary condition and the upkeep of its soil.
g) – Establishment and planting of the vineyard
Before each new planting, every operator must carry out a physico-chemical analysis of the soil of the parcel in order to have all the information needed to know the wine-growing situation and its potential.
### 2° – Other cultivation practices
a) – In order to preserve the characteristics of the soils, which constitute a fundamental element of the terroir:
- Permanent grass cover of the headlands is compulsory.
- Where the spacing between rows is greater than 1.50 metres, the inter-row must have a controlled plant cover, spontaneous or sown, at least during winter and early spring.
- Any substantial modification of the morphology, the subsoil, the arable layer or the structuring elements of the landscape of a parcel intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin is prohibited, with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works.
b) – Vine plantings and replacements may only be made with healthy plant material that has undergone hot-water treatment or any other method making it possible to combat flavescence dorée.
### 3° – Irrigation
Irrigation is prohibited.
## VII. – Harvest, transport and ripeness of the grapes
### 1° – Harvest
a) – The wines come from grapes harvested at good ripeness. The date on which the harvest begins is set in accordance with the provisions of Article D. 644-24 of the Rural Code.
b) – Particular harvesting provisions
No particular provision.
c) – Particular provisions for transport of the harvest
In the case of mechanical harvesting of parcels intended to produce red or rosé wines:
- the height of the harvest in the containers used to transport it from the parcel to the vinification cellar does not exceed 0.50 metre;
- the containers are made of inert, food-grade material;
- the harvesting and harvest-transport equipment must have an appropriate water-drainage or protection system;
- sorting of the harvest is compulsory. It may be carried out either in the vineyard or at the winery, provided suitable equipment can be shown. Sorting is defined as the removal or separation of bunches of grapes or berries affected by disease (botrytis, powdery mildew, etc.) or of insufficiently ripe bunches and berries.
For parcels intended to produce wines eligible for the mention “primeur” or “nouveau”, the harvest is transported under conditions that protect the grapes from oxidation and maintain their quality potential. These transport conditions are the subject of a classification of operators according to the procedures defined in the control or inspection plan.

### 2° – Entry into production of young vines
The benefit of the controlled designation of origin may only be granted to wines coming from:
- parcels of young vines only from the 2nd year following that in the course of which planting was carried out in place before 31 July;
- parcels of young vines only from the 1st year following that in the course of which grafting in place was carried out before 31 July;
- parcels of vines that have been top-grafted, at the earliest in the 1st year following that in the course of which the top-grafting was carried out before 31 July, and once the parcels no longer contain any varieties other than those permitted for the appellation. By derogation, in the year following that in the course of which the top-grafting was carried out before 31 July, the varieties permitted for the appellation may represent only 80 % of the planting of each parcel concerned.
## IX. – Processing, elaboration, packaging and storage
### 1° – General provisions
The wines are vinified in accordance with local, fair and constant usages.
a) – Reception and pressing
No particular provision.
b) – Blending of varieties
- Where wines are produced from parcels planted with accessory varieties, the wines are vinified by blending the grapes concerned.
- The proportion of the gamay de Bouze N and gamay de Chaudenay N varieties, together or separately, is less than or equal to 10 % of the blend.
c) – Malolactic fermentation
At the packaging stage, red wines have a maximum malic acid content of 0.4 gram per litre.

### 2° – Provisions by type of product
Wines eligible for the mention “primeur” or “nouveau” come exclusively from grapes harvested in the same year.
### 3° – Provisions relating to packaging
a) – For every packaged lot, the operator keeps available for the approved control body:
- the information appearing in the register of handling operations referred to in Article D. 644-36 of the Rural Code;
- the reports of analyses carried out before packaging. The reports are kept for a period of six months from the date of packaging.
b) – Containers for volumes of 1.5 litres or less are made of glass.
### 4° – Provisions relating to storage
a) – The operator provides evidence of a specific place for storing packaged products. The temperature of the storage premises is controlled by suitable insulation and must be less than or equal to 25 °C. The premises are equipped with temperature-measuring equipment.
b) – The temperature of the containers during the keeping of the wines must be controlled and less than or equal to 25 °C.
### 5° – Provisions relating to the circulation of products and placing on the market for the consumer
a) – Date of placing on the market for the consumer
The wines are placed on the market for the consumer in accordance with the provisions of Article D. 644-35 of the Rural Code.
b) – Period during which the wines may not circulate between authorised warehousekeepers
Wines eligible for the mention “primeur” or “nouveau” may only circulate between authorised warehousekeepers from the thirty-eighth day preceding the third Thursday of November of the year of harvest.

## XI. – Transitional measures
### 1° – Delimited parcel area
In the following communes of the Saône-et-Loire department: Chaintré, Chânes, La Chapelle-de-Guinchay, Chasselas, Crêches-sur-Saône, Leynes, Romanèche-Thorins and Saint-Symphorien-d’Ancelles, the parcels planted with vines that are excluded from the delimited parcel area of the controlled designation of origin continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up and at the latest up to and including the 2013 harvest, provided that they meet the conditions laid down by the present specification. These parcels are identified by their cadastral references, their area and their varieties on a list approved by the competent national committee of the National Institute of Origin and Quality at its session of 9 and 10 September 1999.
### 2° – Grape varieties, proportion rules on the holding and blending of varieties
a) – Vine parcels in place on 28 November 2004 planted with the aligoté B variety continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin (white wines) up to and including the 2024 harvest.
b) – Up to and including the 2015 harvest, the proportion of the pinot noir N variety, as an accessory variety, is less than or equal to 15 % of the planting of the holding, without necessarily being present as mixed plantings within the vines. Wines made from this variety must be blended with the main variety, in a proportion of less than or equal to 15 % of the blend of the wines.
### 3° – Planting density
For the production of red and rosé wines, except for the controlled designation of origin “Beaujolais” followed by the mention “Villages” or by the name of the commune of origin of the grapes, and up to and including the 2013 harvest, the partial grubbing-up of vine parcels in place on 28 November 2004 is authorised, which may lead, after adaptation, to a minimum planting density of 4,000 plants per hectare. These vine parcels continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up and up to and including the 2034 harvest. The spacing between rows may not exceed 2.80 metres and the spacing between plants within the same row may not exceed 1.10 metres. These vines are permanently trellised; the height of foliage must be at least equal to 0.5 times the average spacing between rows.
### 4° – Pruning rules
For vine parcels in place before 31 August 1975 and pruned according to the so-called “taille à queue du Mâconnais”, each plant may bear 2 canes of 12 developed eyes each, each plant then being unable to bear more than 28 developed eyes.
### 5° – Trellising and foliage height rules
a) – Vine parcels in place on the date of approval of the present specification, intended for the production of rosé and red wines, and with an average spacing between rows of between 2.30 metres and 2.80 metres, have a height of trellised foliage which must be at least equal to 0.5 times the average spacing between rows, the height of trellised foliage being measured from the lower trellising wire, set at least 0.30 metre above the ground, to the upper trimming limit, set at least 0.20 metre above the upper trellising wire(s).
b) – The provision relating to the obligation of a carrying wire does not apply to vine parcels in place on the date of approval of the present specification and pruned with spurs.
### 6° – Average maximum load per parcel and volume eligible for the right to the controlled designation of origin
The average maximum load per parcel of parcels intended for the production of rosé and red wines and with a planting density of between 4,000 and 5,000 plants per hectare after adaptation by partial grubbing-up is set at 8,000 kilograms per hectare. The volume that may benefit from the right to the controlled designation of origin is established on the basis of the yield authorised for the controlled designation of origin, for the harvest concerned, multiplied by a coefficient of 0.9.
## XII. – Rules of presentation and labelling
### 1° – General provisions
Wines for which, under the terms of the present specification, the controlled designation of origin “Beaujolais”, “Beaujolais” followed by the mention “supérieur”, or “Beaujolais” followed by the mention “Villages” or by the name of the commune of origin of the grapes is claimed and which are presented under the said appellation may not be declared after the harvest, offered to the public, dispatched, put up for sale or sold unless, in the harvest declaration, in advertisements, on leaflets, labels, invoices and any containers whatsoever, the aforesaid controlled designation of origin is entered, accompanied by the words “Appellation d’origine contrôlée”, the whole in very conspicuous characters.
### 2° – Particular provisions
a) – Wines eligible for the mention “primeur” or “nouveau” must be presented with the vintage.
b) – The name of the commune of origin of the grapes is entered immediately after the name of the controlled designation of origin.
# CHAPTER II
## I. – Declaration obligations
#### 1. Declaration of intention to produce (wines eligible for the mention “primeur” or “nouveau”)
Every operator lodges with the defence and management body a declaration of intention to produce before (1 June) of the year of harvest. This declaration specifies in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- for the parcel(s) concerned: the cadastral reference and the area.
#### 2. Claim declaration
The claim declaration must be sent to the approved control body within a time limit set in the control or inspection plan, which may not be later than 31 December of the year of harvest. The approved control body transmits the information to the defence and management body without delay.
For operators producing wines with the mention “primeur” or “nouveau”, a partial claim declaration covering all the wines with the mention “primeur” or “nouveau” may be lodged once only, before a date set in the control or inspection plan and at the latest on 31 December of the year of harvest.
It indicates in particular:
- the designation claimed;
- the mention “primeur” or “nouveau”, or “supérieur”, or “Villages”, or the name of the commune of origin of the grapes, where applicable;
- the volume of the wine;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the name and address of the applicant;
- the place of storage of the wine.
It is accompanied by a copy of the harvest declaration and, as the case may be, a copy of the production declaration or an extract of the stock accounts for buyers of grapes and musts.
#### 3. Declaration prior to transaction and collection
Every operator must declare each bulk transaction to the approved control body within time limits set in the control or inspection plan of between five and fifteen working days before any collection of the product. It specifies in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the identification of the lot;
- the volume of the lot;
- the identification of the containers for non-packaged wines;
- the identity of the buyer;
- the number of the interprofessional purchase contract, where applicable.
In the case of collections of volumes smaller than those set out in the transaction declaration, the operator must inform the approved control body in writing.
#### 4. Declaration of release for consumption within the meaning of Article D. 644-36-I of the Rural Code
Each lot of wine intended for release for consumption within the meaning of Article D. 644-36-I of the Rural Code must be the subject of a declaration to the approved control body within minimum time limits set in the control or inspection plan of between five and fifteen working days before release for consumption. This declaration may also be made for lots already packaged, within minimum time limits set in the control or inspection plan of between five and fifteen working days before the lots concerned are dispatched from the operator’s cellars. It specifies in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the identification of the lot;
- the volume of the lot;
- the lot number for wines already packaged;
- the identification of the containers for non-packaged wines;
- the date of packaging or the planned date of packaging.
#### 5. Declaration relating to the dispatch outside the national territory of a non-packaged wine
Every operator wishing to dispatch outside the national territory a non-packaged wine benefiting from the controlled designation of origin must declare it to the approved control body within time limits set in the control or inspection plan of between five and fifteen working days before any dispatch.
#### 6. Fallback declaration
Every operator marketing a wine benefiting from the designation of origin under a more general appellation must declare it to the defence and management body and to the approved control body:
- at the same time as the declaration prior to transaction, if the wine is the subject of a bulk transaction after the fallback;
- at the same time as the packaging declaration, if the wine is packaged after the fallback;
- within time limits set in the control or inspection plan of between five and fifteen working days following the recording of the fallback in the wine register, if the wine is the subject of a fallback after packaging.
The defence and management body and the approved control body inform, respectively and without delay, the defence and management body of the more general appellation concerned and the approved control body for the more general appellation concerned.
#### 7. Downgrading declaration
Every operator carrying out a downgrading of wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin sends the defence and management body and the approved control body a quarterly summary.
#### 8. Declaration of renunciation of marketing with the mention “primeur” or “nouveau”
Every operator wishing to market without the mention “primeur” or “nouveau” a volume claimed with that mention in the claim declaration must declare it to the approved control body within time limits set in the control or inspection plan of between five and fifteen working days before any marketing.
#### 9. Derogatory system
Operators whose number of preparations for release for consumption within the meaning of Article D. 644-36-I of the Rural Code exceeds a threshold set in the control or inspection plan, in number of preparations, number of containers or volume, per year, may opt, by derogation, for a quarterly summary declaration of the declarations referred to in points 3 and 5 above.
Operators whose number of preparations for release for consumption within the meaning of Article D. 644-36-I of the Rural Code of wines with the mention “primeur” or “nouveau” exceeds a threshold set in the control or inspection plan, in number of preparations, number of containers or volume, per year, may opt, by derogation, for a summary declaration of releases for consumption.
#### 10. Declaration of equipment for TSE
Every operator holding a concentration apparatus must declare it upon purchase to the defence and management body, specifying its specifications. The defence and management body keeps an up-to-date list of operators holding such equipment and sends it each year to the National Institute of Origin and Quality at the latest on 1 September.
Every operator using a service provider declares it to the defence and management body, which draws up the list of these operators and sends it each year to the National Institute of Origin and Quality at the latest on 1 September.
#### 11. Reworking of parcels
Before any development or any works liable to modify the morphology, the subsoil, the arable layer (including any addition of exogenous soil) or the structuring elements of the landscape of a parcel intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin, with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works, a declaration must be sent by the operator to the defence and management body at least 4 weeks before the planned start date of the works. The defence and management body transmits, without delay, a copy of this declaration to the services of the National Institute of Origin and Quality.
#### 12. Declaration of adaptation of vine density by partial grubbing-up
Every operator adapting the density of vines by partial grubbing-up sends a declaration to the defence and management body at the latest on 31 July of the wine-growing year in which this operation is carried out. For the parcel(s) or parts of parcels concerned, the declaration specifies:
- the cadastral reference;
- the area;
- the new planting density.
## II. – Keeping of registers
#### 1. General plan of the storage places
Every vinifying operator must keep up to date and available for the approved control body a general plan of the storage places, allowing in particular the number, designation and capacity of the containers to be identified.
#### 2. TSE register
Every operator carrying out partial concentration of musts must keep an up-to-date TSE register including in particular:
- the identification of the initial lot (volume and potential alcoholic strength);
- the volume of water evaporated;
- the identification of the lot after concentration (volume and potential alcoholic strength).
