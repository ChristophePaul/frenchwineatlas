# PART I – TECHNICAL FILE
## 1. – Name of the designation
“Marc de Bourgogne” or “Eau-de-vie de marc de Bourgogne”
## 2. – Description of the spirit drink
The controlled designation of origin “Marc de Bourgogne” or “Eau-de-vie de marc de Bourgogne” is entered in the register of Geographical Indications defined in Article 33 of Regulation (EU) No 2019/787 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 17 April 2019, in the spirit drink category “grape marc spirit or grape marc”, Annex I, point 6 of the aforesaid regulation.
### 2.1. Physical, chemical and/or organoleptic characteristics
The spirits have a minimum content of volatile substances other than ethyl and methyl alcohol of 500 g/hl of pure alcohol. At the time of sale to the consumer, the spirits produced must have a minimum alcoholic strength by volume of 40 %.
“Marc de Bourgogne” is characterised by a yellow to amber colour and slightly fruity aromas with notes of caramel. While it shows a good balance of flavours, it retains a slight roughness that prolongs its length on the palate.
### 2.2. Specific characteristics (compared with other spirit drinks of the same category)
Marcs de Bourgogne are produced from marcs resulting from the vinification of wines claimed under one of the controlled designations of origin of wine-growing Burgundy. They are distilled by the discontinuous multi-stage cascade distillation process using stills made up of concentration columns and copper distillation vessels, to an alcoholic strength by volume of 72 % or less. Except for the quantities intended for industrial uses and for the production of compound products, the spirit is aged in wood for at least 2 years in containers with a maximum unit capacity of 60 hl.

## 4. – Description of the method of production
All the production conditions contributing to obtaining the controlled designation of origin “Marc de Bourgogne” are detailed below.
### 4.1. Raw material
The grape marcs come from the vinification of wines claimed under one of the controlled designations of origin situated in the communes listed in C of the present chapter. The marcs come from white grapes, red grapes, or both processed simultaneously, from the following varieties: aligoté B, césar N, chardonnay B, gamay N, gamay de Bouze N, gamay de Chaudenay N, melon B, pinot noir N, pinot gris G, pinot blanc B, sacy B, sauvignon B, tressot R.
### 4.2. Packaging of the marcs
Until their distillation, the marcs are packed away from the air in closed containers with a capacity of between 100 litres and 200 hectolitres. Marcs from white vinification are packed at the latest 18 hours after the end of pressing. Marcs from red vinification are packed at the latest 24 hours after the end of pressing. Enrichment of the marcs and the addition of preservatives are prohibited.
### 4.3. Distillation
The maximum quantity of marcs processed must be less than or equal to 35 kilograms gross weight per hectolitre of wine claimed under one of the controlled designations of origin of wine-growing Burgundy as defined in point C of the present chapter.
Distillation period: the grape marcs are distilled before 31 March of the year following the harvest, unless an earlier date is proposed by the competent national committee after consulting the defence and management body. Marcs from non-destemmed grapes must be distilled at the latest 60 days after the date of their packing.
Conduct of the distillation: after the container is opened for distillation, the stored marc is kept away from the air in order to avoid microbial or fungal spoilage. The grape marcs are distilled by the discontinuous multi-stage distillation process using stills made up of distillation vessels and concentration columns. The stills used consist of at most three successively loaded vessels and at most three concentration columns mounted in series. The distillation vessels are made of copper. Each has a maximum volume of 630 litres and is heated by direct fire or by steam injection. The columns are made of copper. Each column has a height of 1.50 metres or less. The total number of plates for all the columns is at most 7.
Every distillation must be preceded by cleaning of the still with steam or boiling water.
On leaving the still, at a temperature of 20 °C, the spirits produced must have an alcoholic strength by volume of 72 % or less.
The quantity of alcohol obtained after distillation per 100 kilograms of marcs processed must be:
– greater than or equal to 5 litres and less than or equal to 8 litres of pure alcohol for marcs from red vinification;
– greater than or equal to 4 litres and less than or equal to 7 litres of pure alcohol for marcs from white vinification.
### 4.4. Ageing
Spirits claimed under the designation of origin “Marc de Bourgogne” must be aged in ageing cellars whose humidity and temperature are regulated naturally, without any installation other than the insulation and ventilation of the premises.
Spirits claimed under the designation of origin “Marc de Bourgogne” must be placed, after distillation, in oak containers for a minimum period of two years without interruption from the date of placing in wood. During this period, the capacity of the containers is less than or equal to 60 hl.
### 4.5. Finishing
Adjustment of the colour with caramel and sweetening to round off the final taste are authorised, provided that their effect on the obscuration of the spirit is less than 4 %. Obscuration, expressed in %, is obtained from the difference between the actual alcoholic strength by volume and the gross alcoholic strength by volume.
## 5. – Elements justifying the link with the geographical environment
### 5.1. Specificity of the geographical area
a) Natural factors
The geographical area of the spirits of Burgundy corresponds to that of wine-growing Burgundy, spread over the Yonne (89), Côte-d’Or (21), Saône-et-Loire (71) and Rhône (69) departments. The main vineyard extends along the Burgundian côte, west of the Saône valley, from the north of Lyon to Dijon. It forms a continuous wine-growing strip 200 kilometres long and a few kilometres wide (only 800 metres on the Côte de Nuits). The Beaujolais region, at the southern end, is an integral part of this whole.
This well-defined geographical entity enjoys a continental climate with cold, harsh winters and sometimes late frosts, but hot and sunny summers. The late season, generally very mild, favours perfect ripening of the grapes and allows them to be harvested in good weather conditions.
Rainfall over the Burgundian vineyard is fairly regular throughout the year but remains relatively low compared with regional rainfall, with annual totals of around 800–850 mm, sometimes less. This lower rainfall on the wine-growing côte is due to a foehn effect generated by the reliefs of the upper Beaujolais, the upper Mâconnais and the Morvan to the west, which dry out the oceanic air masses and reduce the effect of rainfall on the eastern piedmonts. The overall eastward exposure of the hillsides allows them to benefit from the first morning warmth of the sun and to dry out the residual humidity of the night quickly.
The geology is dominated by Secondary-era limestone rocks, except in the north of the Beaujolais, which consists of crystalline rocks. The periglacial past of the Burgundian côte led to intense frost-shattering of the constituent rocks, which allowed the formation of stony soils on the slopes, very favourable to vine growing.
b) Human factors
The professional fabric of marc de Bourgogne is made up of roughly the same operators as that of fine de Bourgogne. A small proportion of the winegrowers of the area (about 200, i.e. 5 % of the winegrowers of the area), attached to this specific production, pack marcs for distillation. Some of them (about fifty) have their marc distilled and age their spirit themselves, while most sell the marc to négociants from the spirits sector (liqueurs, marc spirits, liqueur wines…) who have it distilled.
Distillation is carried out by 12 businesses (8 mobile distillers and 4 fixed distillers), each with a still, working either on behalf of winegrowers or of négociants.
In Burgundy, the marcs come from a great diversity of grapes and vinification methods. On the Côtes de Nuits, de Beaune and Chalonnaise, the harvest is generally destemmed, whereas in the Beaujolais the marcs most often come from non-destemmed harvests.
The marc is kept packed and stored away from the air in vats closed with a plastic film and sand, or in large sealed bags that are only opened when the still is loaded. In the Beaujolais, to avoid spoilage, non-destemmed marcs are distilled within a short time.
The stills operating in the region are designed on the same model: a steam-injection boiler, generally three vessels in which the marc is placed, a small concentration column and a condenser. The vessels are made of copper and of small capacity. The succession of three vessels, very widespread in the north of the appellation, allows the first to be preheating while the second is distilling and the third is being exhausted. Most of these stills are a hundred years old and have been handed down from generation to generation. The stills are generally mobile north of Mâcon and often fixed and installed in a workshop in the Beaujolais. Gathering around the still during distillation is still an important moment of conviviality in the life of Burgundian villages.
The cellars house containers of varying capacity: from the “pièce”, which in Burgundy corresponds to a 228-litre cask, commonly used by winegrowers, to large casks (foudres) of several tens of hectolitres, found more among négociants. The proportion of new casks is always relatively low, because the aim is not to extract tannins but to favour oxidation phenomena.
Ageing is managed by the winegrower or, on large estates, by the estate manager. Among the large négociant-éleveurs, it is managed by the owner or a cellar master, who directs the selection of the spirits intended for ageing as well as the maintenance and renewal of the casks. The way ageing is conducted depends on the intrinsic qualities of the various lots and of the ageing equipment, as well as on the know-how of the cellar master. In any event, the first years of ageing take place in foudres of limited capacity (60 hl or less), not topped up, in order to ensure a sufficient exchange surface with the air.
c) Historical elements concerning the factors of the link with the terroir
The vineyard dates from Gallo-Roman times, but it was abandoned for many years, and it was the monks of the abbey of Cîteaux (Cistercians) who, in the Middle Ages, cleared and developed this vineyard. In Burgundy, as in other wine regions, the residues of vinification were reused. This was the case of the marc, called “genne” in Burgundy, over which a little water was passed to make a refreshing, weak-tasting drink, the “râpé”.
Although the distillation process has been known in France since the 13th century, distillation spread in Burgundy and was applied to marc only later. According to Legrand d’Aussy (Histoire de la vie privée du Français, 1782), “it was only towards the end of the 17th century that the idea of drawing a spirit from grape marc was conceived”. Burgundy took this up from that time, since in 1698 the intendant of Lorraine provided the Duke of Burgundy with a “Mémoire sur l’état de sa généralité” in which he mentioned this practice, by which “a fairly good product is drawn from a thing that was only good for burning”.
From the 18th century, the quality of the wine spirits of Burgundy created a demand that could hardly be met owing to the lack of wines for distillation, Burgundian wines being even more highly prized than the spirits. Thus P. Poncelet, a grower, reported in 1740 in his “Chimie du goût et de l’odorat” that the wines of Burgundy were too good and of too modest a yield to make wine spirit. As a result, the distillation of marcs developed.
### 5.2. – Qualities and characteristics of the product
a) Typicity linked to the terroir
As marc de Bourgogne is obtained by distilling the pulp and skins of the grapes after devatting and pressing, it is imbued with the scents of the must. Marc de Bourgogne is characterised by an amber-yellow colour and slightly fruity aromas with notes of caramel. While it shows a good balance of flavours, it retains a slight roughness that prolongs its length on the palate.
Marc de Bourgogne is used mainly as a digestif, but also as an aperitif mixed with crème de cassis (“mêlé-cass”), as an ingredient in cooking and for washing the rind of the designation-of-origin cheese “Epoisses”, to which it imparts its characteristic aromas.
Every year since 1991, the national competition for the wine and marc spirits of Burgundy has been held at the Dijon International Gastronomic Fair, at which a jury of some twenty growers, producers, distillers, négociants and representatives of the administrations judges the best marcs de Bourgogne. Marcs de Bourgogne are also presented at the Metz spirits competition and at the Concours général agricole in Paris.
b) Historical elements linked to reputation
The various surveys carried out from the beginning of the 19th century show that significant quantities were produced, part of which was exported. In 1827, M. Cavoleau, in his “Œnologie française ou statistique de tous les vignobles de France”, mentioned that “in Côte-d’Or some owners and a fairly large number of distillers produce 1,476 hectolitres of pure alcohol of spirit, which is consumed in taverns or exported to Switzerland”; in Saône-et-Loire, 738 hl of pure alcohol, part of which is consumed locally, the rest being shipped to Switzerland, where it is used to make absinthe liqueur; in the Yonne, 393.60 hl of pure alcohol.
At the 1867 Universal Exhibition in Paris, Michel Chevalier mentioned that the production of marc spirits of Burgundy was located in three departments, Côte-d’Or, Saône-et-Loire and Yonne, and varied for the three departments between 8,000 and 10,000 hectolitres of pure alcohol. At the 1899 regional agricultural show, R. Danguy noted that “the marc spirits industry has for some years undergone very great development…”. In 1901, Paul Taquet, in “La Distillerie dans le monde entier, souvenir de l’exposition de 1900”, mentioned that “Marc spirits are much sought after. Some consumers prefer them to ordinary wine spirits. The main marc spirits are those of Burgundy. Burgundy consumes almost exclusively marc spirit. It enjoys, in this region and the surrounding areas, a vogue due to its bouquet. The renown of the marc spirit of Burgundy is universal. In Paris, such spirits at 50° are paid at the same rate as high-value cognacs…”.
In the first half of the 20th century, marc de Bourgogne was associated with several renowned works of art, such as Pablo Picasso’s 1913 canvas “Bouteille de marc de Bourgogne, verre, journal”, and several novels that mention marc de Bourgogne, such as “La chaussée des géants” by Pierre Benoît (1922) or “Un homme à la côte” by Henri Queffélec (1946).
From 1941, a vast campaign of requisitioning of spirits was launched by the German authorities. With the exception of the spirits of the Hospices de Beaune, exceptionally exempted, all the products of Burgundian distilling were affected. Professionals pressed constantly to have “Marc de Bourgogne” recognised as a regulated designation of origin (AOR) and thus remove it from requisition. This was obtained by the decree of 23 February 1942, which conferred the regulated designation of origin on “Marc de Bourgogne”.
In the “marc” entry of the “Encyclopédie des vins et des alcools” (1993), Alexis Lichine mentions that while most wine regions produce marc, that of Burgundy is the best known… Finally, in the 2009 edition of the Petit Robert dictionary, under “marc”, only marc de Bourgogne is cited.
### 5.3. – Causal link between the geographical area and the quality or characteristics of the product
The marcs, or rather the Burgundian grapes, which benefit from the influence of all the factors of the terroir (physical, biological and human environment), act on the specificity of the spirits, because the finesse of the tannins, the aromatic complexity and the freshness of Burgundian wines are found in the distillates. The care taken to preserve the marcs, away from air and light, helps to preserve their quality potential.
Distillation know-how, adapted to equipment whose design has been fixed for more than a century, is passed down from generation to generation. It rests on knowledge of the raw materials processed and on mastery of the operation, in winter, of traditional copper vessel stills. Because of their simplicity, these stills require a raw material of high quality but, notably because of their small capacity, allow very precise monitoring of the quality of the distillates and corresponding adjustment of temperature and flow parameters. This interaction between the quality of the wines, the equipment and the know-how is decisive for the quality of the distillates.
Finally, ageing contributes unquestionably to the final quality of marc de Bourgogne, notably thanks to the continental climate of Burgundy, which favours a relatively small reduction in alcoholic strength, to the development of oxidation reactions owing to the large exchange surfaces between the spirit and the air in the casks, and to the limited use of new wood, which preserves part of the fruity and floral aromas of the new spirits.

## 7. – Rules of presentation and labelling
Spirits for which, under the terms of the present specification, the controlled designation of origin “Marc de Bourgogne” or “Eau-de-vie de marc de Bourgogne” is claimed may not be declared for manufacture, offered to consumers, dispatched, put up for sale or sold unless, on the movement documents, the declaration documents, in advertisements, on leaflets, labels, invoices and any containers whatsoever, the designation is entered, accompanied by the words “appellation d’origine contrôlée”, the whole in very conspicuous characters.
The optional term “vieux” designates spirits aged for at least three years that meet the production conditions of the present specification.
The optional term “très vieux” designates spirits aged for at least six years that meet the production conditions of the present specification.
The optional term “hors d’âge” designates spirits aged for at least ten years that meet the production conditions of the present specification.
In the labelling, these optional terms must be written before or after the name of the controlled designation of origin, in characters of equal or smaller size.
The name of the controlled designation of origin “Marc de Bourgogne” or “Eau-de-vie de marc de Bourgogne” may not be supplemented by any additional term referring to a grape variety or to a geographical indication.
# PART II – DECLARATION OBLIGATIONS AND REGISTERS
## 1.1 – Declaration obligations
#### Declaration of opening and closing of distillation work
The opening of distillation work must be declared to the approved control body before any distillation of spirits intended to be claimed under the AOC “Marc de Bourgogne”. The declaration indicates the name of the AOC, the opening date of the work and the references of the distillation equipment concerned.
The closing of distillation work must be declared to the approved control body at the end of the distillation of the spirits intended to be claimed under the AOC “Marc de Bourgogne”. The declaration indicates the name of the AOC, the closing date of the work and the references of the distillation equipment concerned.
Where the operator distils no spirits other than one of the Burgundy AOCs, the copy of the declarations made to the DGDDI (customs) serves as the declaration and is sent to the approved control body.
#### Claim declaration
The claim declaration must be sent to the defence and management body at the latest one month after the closing of distillation work. It specifies in particular:
– the designation claimed;
– the quantities distilled (volume and alcoholic strength).
#### Declaration of placing in wood
This declaration is sent to the defence and management body at the latest thirty days after the operation. It may be combined with the claim declaration. It includes in particular the dates and place of distillation of the spirits as well as the address of the cellar, the capacity of the containers used, the date of placing in wood and the volume and alcoholic strength by volume of the spirits when placed in wood.
#### Stock declaration
Every operator with an ageing cellar must send the defence and management body each year, at the latest on 10 April, the result of the physical inventory of all the stocks held in each of its identified cellars, broken down by ageing account as defined in point B.3.
## 1.2 – Registers
#### Packaging of the marc
The designation of the product on the document accompanying the marcs during their transport between the winegrower and the distiller specifies the AOC claimed. Every operator packing marcs keeps an up-to-date marc-packing register containing in particular the following information:
– the contact details and identification of the winegrower(s) who produced the marc;
– the characteristics of the grapes from which the marc comes: colour, destemmed or not;
– the date and time of the end of the pressing operations;
– the date and time of packing of the marc.
#### Distillation
Every distiller keeps an up-to-date distillation register containing in particular the following information:
– the AOC claimed;
– the contact details and identification of the operator who packed the marc;
– the reference of the movement document accompanying the marc, where applicable;
– the characteristics of the grapes from which the marc comes: colour, destemmed or not;
– the start and end times of the cleaning procedure;
– the date and time of distillation;
– the quantity and alcoholic strength of the spirit obtained;
– the destination of the spirit obtained (placing in ageing or other).
#### Ageing
Marcs de Bourgogne are entered in the operator’s stock accounts under one of the following ageing accounts: 0 for spirits at 1 April of the year following placing in wood; 1 for spirits with more than one year of ageing; 2 for more than two years; 3 for more than three years; 4 for more than four years; 5 for more than five years; 6 for more than six years; 7 for more than seven years; 8 for more than eight years; 9 for more than nine years; 10 for spirits with more than ten years of ageing. On 1 April each year, the balances of each of accounts 0 to 9 are transferred to the next higher account.
Every operator keeps the following information up to date:
– the identification of the containers in the cellar and the description of their capacity;
– the dates of placing in wood of the distilled spirits;
– the quantities of distilled spirits placed in wood, by container;
– other entries of spirits, by container and by ageing account;
– removals of spirits, by container and by ageing account.
