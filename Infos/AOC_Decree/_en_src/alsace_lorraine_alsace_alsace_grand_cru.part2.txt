### 4° – Entry into production of young vines
The benefit of the controlled designation of origin may only be granted to wines coming from:
- parcels of young vines only from the 3rd year following that in the course of which planting was carried out in place before 31 July;
- parcels of young vines only from the 2nd year following that in the course of which grafting in place was carried out before 31 July;
- parcels of vines that have been top-grafted, at the earliest in the 1st year following that in the course of which the top-grafting was carried out before 31 July, and once the parcels no longer contain any varieties other than those permitted for the appellation. ## IX. – Processing, elaboration, ageing, packaging, storage
### 1° – General provisions
The wines are vinified in accordance with local, fair and constant usages.
a) – Reception and pressing
The installation for receiving and pressing the harvest is sheltered from all kinds of bad weather, as is the press while it is in operation.
The floor of the reception and pressing premises must be easy to clean (nature of the floor, water point, drainage…).
Daily washing of the press is compulsory.
b) – Blending of varieties
The presence of grapes from the varieties gewurztraminer Rs, pinot gris G and riesling B is compulsory in blended wines liable to benefit from the controlled designation of origin “Alsace grand cru Altenberg de Bergheim”. The blending of the grapes is carried out at the pressing stage.
c) – Malolactic fermentation
Malolactic fermentation is completed for red wines.
At the packaging stage, red wines have a malic acid content less than or equal to 0.4 gram per litre.
d) – Analytical standards
Red wines have, after fermentation, a fermentable sugar content (glucose and fructose) less than or equal to 2 grams per litre.
e) – Oenological practices and physical treatments
- The use of pieces of wood is prohibited.
- Wines liable to benefit from the terms “vendanges tardives” or “sélection de grains nobles” are not enriched in any way.
- The increase in the minimum average natural alcoholic strength by volume may not exceed 0.5 % vol. for wines from the gewurztraminer and pinot gris G varieties, and 1.5 % vol. for wines from the other varieties and for wines from a blend of varieties for the appellation “Alsace grand cru Kaefferkopf”.
- For the controlled designation of origin “Alsace grand cru Altenberg de Bergheim”, the wines are not enriched in any way.
- Red wines are not enriched in any way.

## X. – Link with the geographical area
### “Alsace grand cru Altenberg de Bergheim”
#### 1° – Information on the geographical area
a) – Description of the natural factors contributing to the link
The controlled designation of origin “Alsace grand cru Altenberg de Bergheim” forms an integral part of the Alsace vineyard, its geographical area being set in the heart of it. It is delimited within the area of the controlled designation of origin “Alsace”.
Located entirely in the commune of Bergheim, at the outlet of the small closed valley of the Bergenbach, the Altenberg de Bergheim grand cru is a majestic, warm and sunny terroir, facing mainly south yet never short of water, with a moderate to steep slope.
The Vosges massif offering protection against the humid, cool north-westerly winds, the vineyard finds its ideal conditions of establishment on the well-exposed (east to south-west), steeply sloping hillsides of the Grasberg hill. The landscape is completely open, without screening.
This terroir, detached from the Vosges massif and marked by a certain surface diversity of soils, is very particular. The clay-limestone-sandstone geological nature of the terroir gives red, calcareous, fine and stony soils, rich in marls and highly prone to erosion. There is a tendency to chlorosis, and humus is not a central issue.
In climatic terms, this appellation is protected from north winds by the relief of the Altenberg and from west winds by the Taennchel massif. Rainfall is low. The mesoclimate is calm and still; there is no wind. Consequently, the difference between day and night temperatures is small; this absence of jolts means that physiological ripening advances gently: the grapes are harvested late, often at a time when noble rot is beginning to develop.
The parcels are fairly large, planted in the direction of the slope, at high planting density and of rather advanced age. The vineyard shows good vigour and low but regular productivity. A terroir that is regular in terms of yield, physiological ripeness and degree, regularly capable of great ripeness, the Altenberg de Bergheim is marked by a complex mix of varieties, ripened and harvested at the same time.

#### 2° – Information on the quality and characteristics of the products
White wines for laying down par excellence, the wines of the appellation “Alsace grand cru Altenberg de Bergheim” are characterised by a marked freshness based on a dominant tartaric acidity linked to excellent ripeness of the grapes. They show great substance, much complexity and strong aromatic power. Of great length on the palate, they gain in complexity over time. Young wines are discreet on the attack, voluminous on the palate and with a fruity acidity. Originality is expressed very well in the blended wines.
The Altenberg de Bergheim produces wines powerfully marked by the terroir because the grapes harvested are thin-skinned, physiologically ripe, sensitive to the weather and of high degree: the musts are never enriched. These wines show a seasonal evolution and very high ageing potential, and reach their peak after 15 to 20 years. These original characteristics are linked to the natural factors of the terroir and to the practices of the wine-growers detailed here.
At their peak these wines are very powerful, discreet on the attack, voluminous on the palate, with a fruity, vanilla-tinged and gentle acidity, often marked by noble rot yet remaining very mouth-watering.
Four types of product can be distinguished:
- the identity group, by far the largest, made up of aromatic, rich, fruity, full and complex wines from the authorised varieties, planted alone or mixed as permitted by the specification, wines that are never technically dry;
- a group from the riesling variety alone, characterised by drier, mineral, deep wines, sometimes marked by fossil aromas.
These two types of wine show a deep colour intensity ranging up to golden yellow.
- wines benefiting from the term “vendanges tardives” often display very exotic aromas of candied fruit, with a fresh finish;
- wines benefiting from the term “sélection de grains nobles” are more concentrated, powerful wines, often showing aromas of fruit paste.
These two types of wine show a deep colour intensity ranging up to amber yellow. Wines benefiting from these last two terms are sweet wines of very long keeping. They are of remarkable concentration and great aromatic persistence. The terms “vendanges tardives” and “sélection de grains nobles” are systematically followed by the denominations Gewurztraminer, Pinot gris and Riesling, and the indication of the vintage is compulsory.
#### 3° – Causal interactions
Drawing on the favourable climatic conditions that characterise the Alsace vineyard, the controlled designation of origin “Alsace grand cru Altenberg de Bergheim” expresses the quintessence of a very great terroir. Nestled within the picturesque Alsace landscape, this vineyard allows the production of wines of high expression, with a marked character and a unique personality.
The location, the general full-south orientation, the slope, the geology and particularly the highly fractured limestone subsoils, the strength of the shallow clay-limestone soils, the warm climate and the regular water supply give the vine the possibility of regular growth, balanced vigour and production of extreme quality.
Rather late-ripening, the terroir leads to high levels of ripeness, often amplified by the excellent late-season climatic conditions favourable to concentration on the vine and to the development of noble rot, allowing the production of wines from overripe harvests. The ageing provided for in the specification allows the wines to improve.
By adopting rigorous production rules, such as maintaining a large leaf area and manual harvesting, Alsace wine-growers preserve the assertive character of wines recognised for their complexity and their aptitude for long keeping. They constitute the top of the range of this region. The “Altenberg de Bergheim” wines include some of the most highly valued wines produced in Alsace.

### “Alsace grand cru Eichberg”
#### 1° – Information on the geographical area
a) – Description of the natural factors contributing to the link
@@NAT Eichberg
The substratum of this grand cru is essentially made up of calcareous conglomerates and marls, overlain at the foot of the slope by colluvium of the same nature, which may however incorporate sandstone scree and pebbles. The scree does not rest directly on the coastal conglomerate but is separated from it by a layer of yellow clay of variable thickness. The soil is thus sometimes siliceous, fairly clayey and rich in gravel.
