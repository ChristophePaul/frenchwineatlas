# CHAPTER I
## I. – Name of the designation
Only wines meeting the specific provisions set out below may claim the controlled designation of origin “Mazis-Chambertin”, initially recognised by the decree of 31 July 1937.
## II. – Geographical denominations and complementary terms
No complementary provisions.
## III. – Colour and types of product
The controlled designation of origin “Mazis-Chambertin” is reserved for still red wines.
## IV. – Areas and zones in which the various operations are carried out
### 1° – Geographical area
The harvesting of the grapes, the vinification, the elaboration and the ageing of the wines are carried out within the territory of the commune of Gevrey-Chambertin in the Côte-d’Or department.
### 2° – Delimited parcel area
The wines come exclusively from vines situated within the delimited parcel production area as approved by the National Institute of Origin and Quality at the session of the competent national committee of 17 and 18 May 1984.
The National Institute of Origin and Quality lodges with the town hall of the commune referred to in 1° the graphic documents establishing the parcel boundaries of the production area thus approved.

## V. – Grape varieties
### 1° – Grape varieties
The wines come from the following varieties:
- main variety: pinot noir N;
- accessory varieties: chardonnay B, pinot blanc B, pinot gris G.
### 2° – Proportion rules on the holding
The accessory varieties are authorised only as mixed plantings within the vines. Their total proportion is limited to 15 % within each parcel.
## VI. – Management of the vineyard
### 1° – Training methods
a) – Planting density
The vines have a minimum planting density of 9,000 plants per hectare, with a spacing between rows that may not exceed 1.25 metres and a spacing between plants within the same row that may not be less than 0.50 metre.
The vines may be planted “en foule” (randomly, without rows), provided that the minimum planting density is respected and the spacing between plants is greater than 0.50 metre.
b) – Pruning rules
The wines come from vines pruned according to the following techniques:
- either short pruning (vines trained as Royat cordon, bilateral cordon, gobelet and fan), with a number of free eyes per plant less than or equal to 8;
- or single Guyot long pruning, with a number of free eyes per plant less than or equal to 8.
Whatever the pruning method, the vines may be pruned with additional free eyes provided that, at the phenological stage corresponding to 11 or 12 leaves, the number of fruit-bearing shoots of the year per plant is less than or equal to the number of free eyes defined in the pruning rules.
c) – Trellising and foliage height rules
The height of trellised foliage must be at least equal to 0.6 times the spacing between rows, the height of trellised foliage being measured between the lower limit of the foliage, set at least 0.30 metre above the ground, and the upper trimming limit.
When the vines are not pruned in gobelet, they must be raised on a stake or trellised; the trellising must be maintained.
When the vines are planted “en foule”, they are trained on stakes.
d) – Average maximum load per parcel
The average maximum load per parcel is set at 8,000 kilograms per hectare.
e) – Threshold of missing vines
The percentage of dead or missing vine plants referred to in Article D. 644-22 of the Rural Code is set at 20 %.
f) – Cultivation condition of the vine
The parcels must be managed so as to ensure a good overall cultivation condition of the vine, reflected in particular by:
- control of a good sanitary condition, making it possible to obtain healthy foliage and healthy berries;
- upkeep of the soil, namely control of grass cover by a grass height of less than half the trellising height (height between the ground and the upper trellising wire), and control of erosion through the absence of visible roots.
### 2° – Other cultivation practices
a) – In order to preserve the characteristics of the soils, which constitute a fundamental element of the terroir:
- Permanent grass cover of the headlands is compulsory.
- Any substantial modification of the morphology, the subsoil, the arable layer or the structuring elements of the landscape of a parcel intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin is prohibited, with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works.
b) – Vine plantings and replacements may only be made with healthy plant material that has undergone hot-water treatment or any other method making it possible to combat flavescence dorée.
### 3° – Irrigation
Irrigation is prohibited.
## VII. – Harvest, transport and ripeness of the grapes
### 1° – Harvest
a) – The wines come from grapes harvested at good ripeness.
b) – Particular harvesting provisions
No particular provision.
c) – Particular provisions for transport of the harvest
The harvest is protected from rain during its transport and on its reception.
### 2° – Ripeness of the grapes
a) – Sugar content of the grapes
Grapes with a sugar content of less than 198 grams per litre of must may not be considered to be at good ripeness.
b) – Minimum natural alcoholic strength by volume
The wines have a minimum natural alcoholic strength by volume of 11.5 %.
## VIII. – Yields – Entry into production
### 1° – Yield
The yield referred to in Article D. 644-25 of the Rural Code is set at 45 hectolitres per hectare.
### 2° – Limit yield
The limit yield referred to in Article D. 644-25 of the Rural Code is set at 53 hectolitres per hectare.
### 3° – Maximum production yield
No particular provision.
### 4° – Entry into production of young vines
The benefit of the controlled designation of origin may only be granted to wines coming from:
- parcels of young vines only from the 2nd year following that in the course of which planting was carried out in place before 31 July;
- parcels of young vines only from the 1st year following that in the course of which grafting in place was carried out before 31 July;
- parcels of vines that have been top-grafted, at the earliest in the 1st year following that in the course of which the top-grafting was carried out before 31 July, and once the parcels no longer contain any varieties other than those permitted for the appellation. By derogation, in the year following that in the course of which the top-grafting was carried out before 31 July, the varieties permitted for the appellation may represent only 80 % of the planting of each parcel concerned.
### 5° – Particular provisions
No particular provision.
## IX. – Processing, elaboration, ageing, packaging, storage
### 1° – General provisions
The wines are vinified in accordance with local, fair and constant usages.
a) – Reception and pressing
No particular provision.
b) – Blending of varieties
Wines produced from parcels with mixed plantings are vinified by blending the grapes.
c) – Malolactic fermentation
At the packaging stage, the wines have a maximum malic acid content of 0.4 gram per litre.
d) – Analytical standards
Finished wines ready to be released for consumption within the meaning of Article D. 644-36-1 of the Rural Code have a maximum fermentable sugar content (glucose + fructose) of 2 grams per litre.
e) – Oenological practices and physical treatments
- Subtractive enrichment techniques (TSE) are authorised within the limit of a maximum concentration rate of 10 %. The equipment used is fitted with a meter allowing the volume of water removed to be declared.
- The use of pieces of wood is prohibited.
- After enrichment, the wines do not exceed a total alcoholic strength by volume of 14.5 %.
f) – Prohibited equipment
Continuous presses are prohibited.
g) – Overall vat capacity
Every operator must have an overall vat capacity (vinification and storage) equivalent to at least 150 % of the volume of wine vinified for the previous year’s harvest, as entered either on the harvest declaration, pro rata to the change in the holding’s area in production, or on the production declaration.
h) – Good overall state of upkeep of the cellar (floor and walls) and of the equipment (hygiene)
The cellar and the equipment are well maintained; this is reflected in particular by:
- general hygiene of the elaboration premises, with general cleanliness, well-maintained floors, adequate drainage and a surface avoiding stagnation;
- harmlessness of the equipment and products coming into contact with the wine;
- separation and specificity of the premises: premises without the same functions must be separated, such as storage areas for plant-protection products, cleaning products or hydrocarbons, from the premises for vinification, ageing and storage of dry goods (corks, cartons);
- management of wine effluents: effluents must be removed as quickly as possible from foodstuff premises and deposited in well-maintained containers that are easy to clean and can be closed. Waste storage areas must be kept clean; waste must be disposed of hygienically and in an environmentally friendly manner; a waste storage and removal area must be provided;
- absence of hazardous or odorous substances in the vinification, ageing and storage premises (odour).
i) – Ageing
- The wines are aged at least until 15 June of the year following that of the harvest.
- The temperature of the containers during the ageing phase must be controlled and less than or equal to 25 °C.
### 2° – Provisions by type of product
No particular provision.
### 3° – Provisions relating to packaging
For every packaged lot, the operator keeps available for the approved control body:
- the information appearing in the register of handling operations referred to in Article D. 644-36 of the Rural Code;
- the reports of analyses carried out before packaging allowing analytical monitoring of the packaged lots. These reports are kept for a period of six months from the date of packaging.
### 4° – Provisions relating to storage
The operator provides evidence of a protected storage place for products packaged in unlabelled bottles, meeting the following conditions:
- temperature: between 5 °C and 22 °C.
### 5° – Provisions relating to the circulation of products and placing on the market for the consumer
a) – Date of placing on the market for the consumer
At the end of the ageing period, the wines are only placed on the market for the consumer after 30 June of the year following that of the harvest.
b) – Period during which the wines may not circulate between authorised warehousekeepers
No particular provision.

## XI. – Transitional measures
No particular provision.
## XII. – Rules of presentation and labelling
### 1° – General provisions
Wines for which, under the terms of the present specification, the controlled designation of origin “Mazis-Chambertin” is claimed and which are presented under the said appellation may not be declared after the harvest, offered to the public, dispatched, put up for sale or sold unless, in the harvest declaration, in advertisements, on leaflets, labels, invoices and any containers whatsoever, the aforesaid controlled designation of origin is entered, accompanied by the words “Appellation d’origine contrôlée”, the whole in very conspicuous characters.
### 2° – Particular provisions
The term “grand cru” must appear on the label. This term is placed immediately below the name of the appellation in characters at most equal, both in height and in width, to two-thirds of those used for the name of the appellation.
# CHAPTER II
## I. – Declaration obligations
#### 1. Claim declaration
The claim declaration must be sent to the defence and management body at least fifteen days before circulation between authorised warehousekeepers and at the latest on 1 December of the year of harvest.
It indicates in particular:
- the designation claimed;
- the volume of the wine;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the name and address of the applicant;
- the place of storage of the wine.
It is accompanied in particular by a copy of the harvest declaration and, as the case may be, a copy of the production declaration or an extract of the stock accounts for buyers of grapes and musts.
#### 2. Declaration prior to transaction and collections
Every operator wishing to sell in bulk a wine benefiting from the controlled designation of origin must make a transaction declaration for the lot concerned to the approved control body within time limits set in the control or inspection plan of between six and fifteen working days before any collection.
This declaration, accompanied where applicable by a copy of the purchase contract, specifies in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the identification of the lot;
- the volume of the lot;
- the identification of the containers;
- the identity of the buyer.
If collections are made for volumes smaller than those set out in the transaction declaration, the operator must inform the control body in writing.
#### 3. Declaration of release for consumption
Each lot of wine intended to be released for consumption within the meaning of Article D. 644-36-I of the Rural Code must be the subject of a declaration of release for consumption to the approved control body within minimum time limits set in the control or inspection plan of between six and fifteen working days before release for consumption.
This declaration may also be drawn up for lots already packaged, within minimum time limits set in the control or inspection plan of between six and fifteen working days before the lots concerned are dispatched from the operator’s cellars.
It specifies in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the identification of the lot;
- the volume of the lot;
- the lot number for wines already packaged;
- the identification of the containers for non-packaged wines.
#### 4. Declaration relating to the dispatch outside the national territory of a non-packaged wine
Every operator wishing to dispatch outside the national territory a non-packaged wine benefiting from the controlled designation of origin must declare it to the approved control body within time limits set in the control or inspection plan of between six and fifteen working days before any dispatch.
#### 5. Fallback declaration
Every operator marketing a wine benefiting from the controlled designation of origin under a more general appellation must declare it to the defence and management body and to the approved control body:
- at the same time as the declaration prior to transaction, if the wine is the subject of a bulk transaction after the fallback;
- at the same time as the packaging declaration, if the wine is packaged after the fallback;
- within time limits set in the control or inspection plan of between six and fifteen working days following the recording of the fallback in the wine register, if the wine is the subject of a fallback after packaging.
The defence and management body and the approved control body inform, respectively and without delay, the defence and management body of the more general appellation concerned and the approved control body for the more general appellation concerned.
#### 6. Downgrading declaration
Every operator carrying out a downgrading of wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin sends the defence and management body and the approved control body a quarterly summary.
#### 7. Declaration of equipment for TSE
Every operator holding a concentration apparatus must declare it upon purchase to the defence and management body, specifying its specifications. The defence and management body keeps an up-to-date list of operators holding such equipment and sends it each year to the National Institute of Origin and Quality at the latest on 1 September.
Every operator using a service provider declares it to the defence and management body, which draws up the list of these operators and sends it each year to the National Institute of Origin and Quality at the latest on 1 September.
#### 8. Reworking of parcels
Before any development or any works liable to modify the morphology, the subsoil, the arable layer (including any addition of exogenous soil) or the structuring elements of the landscape of a parcel intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin, with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works, a declaration must be sent by the operator to the defence and management body at least 4 weeks before the planned start date of the works. The defence and management body transmits, without delay, a copy of this declaration to the services of the National Institute of Origin and Quality.
#### 9. Derogatory system
Operators carrying out at least five transactions per week and/or at least forty-two preparations for release for consumption within the meaning of Article D. 644-36-I of the Rural Code per year may group their transaction, release-for-consumption and fallback declarations. In this case, they must provide the approved control body with their forecast of transactions, releases for consumption and fallbacks, then send the quarterly summary of all the declarations referred to in points 2, 3 and 5 above.
## II. – Keeping of registers
#### 1. General plan of the storage and vinification places
Every vinifying operator must keep up to date and available for the approved control body a general plan of the storage and vinification places, allowing in particular the number, designation and capacity of the containers to be identified.
#### 2. TSE register
Every operator carrying out partial concentration of musts must keep an up-to-date TSE register including in particular:
- the initial volume;
- the volume of water evaporated;
- the identification of the lot after concentration (volume and potential alcoholic strength).
