# CHAPTER I
## I. – Name of the designation
Only wines meeting the specific provisions set out below may claim the controlled designation of origin “Crémant de Bourgogne”, initially recognised by the decree of 17 October 1975.
## II. – Geographical denominations and complementary terms
No particular provision.
## III. – Colour and types of product
The controlled designation of origin “Crémant de Bourgogne” is reserved for white or rosé sparkling wines.

## V. – Grape varieties
The wines come from the following varieties:
- red varieties: gamay N, pinot gris G, pinot noir N;
- white varieties: aligoté B, chardonnay B, melon B, pinot blanc B, sacy B.
## VI. – Management of the vineyard
### 1° – Training methods
a) – Planting density
- The vines have a minimum planting density of 5,000 plants per hectare, with a spacing between rows that may not exceed 2.20 metres.
- In the delimited vineyard of the controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne” followed by the geographical denominations “Hautes Côtes de Beaune” and “Hautes Côtes de Nuits”, the vines have a minimum planting density of 4,000 plants per hectare, with a spacing between rows that may not exceed 3 metres.
- Where the planting density is greater than 8,000 plants per hectare, the vines may not have a spacing between plants within the same row of less than 0.50 metre.
- Where the planting density is less than or equal to 8,000 plants per hectare, the vines may not have a spacing between plants within the same row of less than 0.80 metre.
Vines planted “en foule” (randomly, without rows) have a minimum planting density of 9,000 plants per hectare and a spacing between plants within the same row greater than 0.50 metre. Vines trained according to the so-called “lyre” system have a minimum planting density of 3,000 plants per hectare, with a spacing between rows of less than 3.50 metres and a spacing between plants within the same row of between 0.80 metre and 1 metre.
b) – Pruning rules
The vines are pruned according to the following rules:

Vines trained according to the so-called “lyre” system are pruned in double Guyot or double Royat cordon. Overlapping of canes or cordons is prohibited. Each plant bears a maximum of 26 free eyes and the maximum number of free eyes per square metre of ground surface is 8.

c) – Trellising and foliage height rules
| SPACING BETWEEN ROWS, LOCATION OF THE VINES | TRELLISING AND FOLIAGE HEIGHT RULES |

| Vines with a spacing of 2.50 metres or more, in the delimited vineyard of the controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne” followed by the geographical denominations “Hautes Côtes de Beaune” and “Hautes Côtes de Nuits” (so-called “wide” vines) | The height of trellised foliage must be at least equal to 1.50 metres, the height of trellised foliage being measured between the lower limit of the foliage, set at least 0.30 metre above the ground, and the upper trimming limit. |
- The vines must be raised on a stake or trellised; the trellising must be maintained.
- When the vines are planted “en foule”, they are trained on stakes.
- For vines trained according to the so-called “lyre” system, the height of trellised foliage is at least 1.2 metres. It is measured between the lower trellising wire and the upper trimming limit. The trellising consists of two planes that diverge towards the top. The distance between the trellising planes is at least 0.5 metre at the base and between 1.0 metre and 1.4 metres at the top.
d) – Average maximum load per parcel
The average maximum load per parcel is set at 14,500 kilograms per hectare.
e) – Threshold of missing vines
The percentage of dead or missing vine plants referred to in Article D. 644-22 of the Rural Code is set at 20 %.
f) – Cultivation condition of the vine
The parcels must be managed so as to ensure a good overall cultivation condition of the vine, reflected in particular by:
- control of a good sanitary condition, making it possible to obtain healthy foliage and healthy berries;
- upkeep of the soil, namely control of grass cover by a grass height of less than half the trellising height (height between the ground and the upper trellising wire), and control of erosion through the absence of visible roots.
### 2° – Other cultivation practices
a) – In order to preserve the characteristics of the soils, which constitute a fundamental element of the terroir:
- Permanent grass cover of the headlands is compulsory.
- Any substantial modification of the morphology, the subsoil, the arable layer or the structuring elements of the landscape of a parcel intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin is prohibited, with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works.
b) – Vine plantings and replacements may only be made with healthy plant material that has undergone hot-water treatment or any other method making it possible to combat flavescence dorée.
### 3° – Irrigation
Irrigation is prohibited.
## VII. – Harvest, transport and ripeness of the grapes
### 1° – Harvest
a) – The wines come from grapes harvested at good ripeness.
b) – Particular harvesting provisions
The wines come from grapes harvested by hand.
c) – Particular provisions for transport of the harvest
The harvest is protected from rain during its transport. The harvest may only be transported from the place of picking to the pressing installation in containers:
- not exceeding a depth of 0.50 metre, in order to avoid any compression of the harvest;
- not watertight, allowing the rapid and complete draining of the juice during transport and while awaiting pressing.
The time elapsing between the picking of the grapes and pressing must be as short as possible. In no case may it exceed twenty-four hours.
### 2° – Ripeness of the grapes
a) – Sugar content of the grapes
Grapes with a sugar content of less than 144 grams per litre of must may not be considered to be at good ripeness.
b) – Minimum natural alcoholic strength by volume
The wines have a minimum natural alcoholic strength by volume of 9 %.
## VIII. – Yields – Entry into production
### 1° – Yield
a) – The yield referred to in Article D. 644-25 of the Rural Code is set at 74 hectolitres per hectare.
b) – In the absence of a prior declaration of parcel assignment, the volume that may benefit from the right to the controlled designation of origin is established on the basis of the yield authorised for the controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne” (white wines) for the harvest concerned, if this is lower than the yield authorised for the controlled designation of origin.
### 2° – Limit yield
The limit yield referred to in Article D. 644-25 of the Rural Code is set at 80 hectolitres per hectare.
### 3° – Entry into production of young vines
The benefit of the controlled designation of origin may only be granted to wines coming from:
- parcels of young vines only from the 2nd year following that in the course of which planting was carried out in place before 31 July;
- parcels of young vines only from the 1st year following that in the course of which grafting in place was carried out before 31 July;
- parcels of vines that have been top-grafted, at the earliest in the 1st year following that in the course of which the top-grafting was carried out before 31 July, and once the parcels no longer contain any varieties other than those permitted for the appellation. By derogation, in the year following that in the course of which the top-grafting was carried out before 31 July, the varieties permitted for the appellation may represent only 80 % of the planting of each parcel concerned.
### 5° – Particular provisions
a) – Base wines intended for the production of wines eligible for the controlled designation of origin are obtained within the limit of 100 litres of must per 150 kilograms of grapes processed.
b) – The rate of “rebêches” referred to in Article D. 644-34 of the Rural Code is a minimum extraction set at between 0 % and 10 % of the quantity of settled musts eligible for the controlled designation of origin.

- the pressing installations must comply with the provisions below. The opening, extension or modification of a pressing installation must be authorised before the installation comes into operation.

b) – Blending of varieties
In the cuvée:
- The proportion of the chardonnay B, pinot blanc B, pinot gris G and pinot noir N varieties is greater than or equal to 30 %;
- The proportion of the gamay N variety is less than or equal to 20 %.
“Cuvée” means all the volumes of wine intended directly for bottling for the prise de mousse. It consists of one base wine or a blend of base wines.
c) – Analytical standards
Before tirage, base wines have a total sulphur dioxide content less than or equal to 100 milligrams per litre.
After tirage and before disgorging, the sparkling wines have:
- a total sulphur dioxide content less than or equal to 150 milligrams per litre;
- a carbon dioxide overpressure of at least 4 bars, measured at a temperature of 20 °C.
d) – Oenological practices and physical treatments
- For the production of rosé wines, the use of oenological charcoal, alone or mixed in preparations, is prohibited.
- The use of pieces of wood is prohibited.
- After enrichment, base wines do not exceed a total alcoholic strength by volume of 11.5 %.
- After enrichment, the wines do not exceed, before the addition of the dosage liqueur (liqueur d’expédition), a total alcoholic strength by volume of 13 %.
e) – Overall vat capacity
Every operator must have an overall vat capacity (vinification and storage) equivalent to at least the volume of wine vinified for the previous year’s harvest, as entered either on the harvest declaration, pro rata to the change in the holding’s area in production, or on the production declaration.
f) – Good overall state of upkeep of the cellar (floor and walls) and of the equipment (hygiene)
The cellar and the equipment are well maintained; this is reflected in particular by:
- general hygiene of the elaboration premises, with general cleanliness, well-maintained floors, adequate drainage and a surface avoiding stagnation;
- harmlessness of the equipment and products coming into contact with the wine;
- separation and specificity of the premises: premises without the same functions must be separated, such as storage areas for plant-protection products, cleaning products or hydrocarbons, from the premises for vinification, ageing and storage of dry goods (corks, cartons);
- management of wine effluents: effluents must be removed as quickly as possible from foodstuff premises and deposited in well-maintained containers that are easy to clean and can be closed. Waste storage areas must be kept clean; waste must be disposed of hygienically and in an environmentally friendly manner; a waste storage and removal area must be provided;
- absence of hazardous or odorous substances in the vinification, ageing and storage premises (odour).
g) – Other provisions
The temperature of the containers during the vinification of the base wines must be controlled and less than or equal to 25 °C.
### 2° – Provisions by type of product
a) – Base wines intended for the production of rosé wines may come from maceration or saignée.
b) – The wines are produced exclusively by second fermentation in glass bottles.
c) – Bottling (tirage) in the bottles in which the prise de mousse takes place is carried out from 1 December following the harvest.
d) – The period of keeping in bottle on lees may not be less than nine months.
### 3° – Provisions relating to packaging
a) – For every packaged lot, the operator keeps available for the approved control body the information appearing in the register of handling operations referred to in Article D. 644-36 of the Rural Code. Analysis reports must be kept for a period of 6 months from the date of tirage.
b) – The wines are produced and sold in the bottles in which the prise de mousse took place, except for wines sold in bottles with a volume of 37.5 centilitres or less, or of more than 150 centilitres.
### 4° – Provisions relating to storage
The operator provides evidence of a specific storage place for packaged wines, suitable and temperature-controlled. The temperature of the premises where bottles are stored “sur lattes” is less than or equal to 25 °C.
### 5° – Provisions relating to the circulation of products and placing on the market for the consumer
a) – Date of placing on the market for the consumer
The wines may only be placed on the market for the consumer after an ageing period of at least 12 months from the date of tirage.
b) – Period during which the wines may not circulate between authorised warehousekeepers
The wines may only circulate between authorised warehousekeepers after a period of at least nine months from the date of tirage.

## XI. – Transitional measures
### 1° – Training methods
a) – Vine parcels in place on 31 July 2009 trained in gobelet continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up.
b) – Vine parcels in place on 31 July 2009, situated in the communes of the Rhône and Saône-et-Loire departments belonging to the delimited vineyard of the controlled designation of origin “Beaujolais”, with a density of between 4,000 and 5,000 plants per hectare, continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up.
c) – Vine parcels in place on 31 July 2009, situated in the Yonne department, with a density of less than 5,000 plants per hectare continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up.
d) – Vine parcels in place before 1980, situated outside the delimited vineyard of the controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne” supplemented by the geographical denominations “Hautes Côtes de Beaune” and “Hautes Côtes de Nuits”, which do not comply with the provisions relating to planting density set out in the present specification and are trained on a “single-plane” trellis, continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up, provided that they:
- have a planting density greater than 3,000 plants per hectare;
- are pruned with a maximum of 6 free eyes per square metre;
- have a trellised foliage height at least equal to 1.50 metres, the height of trellised foliage being measured between the lower limit of the foliage, set at least 0.30 metre above the ground, and the upper trimming limit.
The volume that may be claimed under the controlled designation of origin is calculated on the basis of the yield authorised for the controlled designation of origin multiplied by a coefficient of 0.80.
e) – Vine parcels in place before 1980, situated in the delimited vineyard of the controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne” followed by the geographical denominations “Hautes Côtes de Beaune” and “Hautes Côtes de Nuits”, which do not comply with the provisions relating to planting density set out in the present specification and are trained on a “single-plane” trellis, continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up, provided that they:
- have a planting density greater than 3,000 plants per hectare;
- are pruned with a maximum of 6 free eyes per square metre;
- have a trellised foliage height at least equal to 1.50 metres, the height of trellised foliage being measured between the lower limit of the foliage, set at least 0.30 metre above the ground, and the upper trimming limit.
f) – Vine parcels in place before 31 August 1970 and pruned in the so-called “taille à queue du Mâconnais” may be pruned in such a way that, at the phenological stage corresponding to 11 or 12 leaves, each plant bears a maximum of 28 fruit-bearing shoots of the year, including 2 canes each bearing a maximum of 12 fruit-bearing shoots of the year.
### 2° – Reception and pressing
Conveying of the grapes and grape belts: for any reception and pressing site installed before 31 July 2009, and provided that this installation is not subject to a major modification of the structure or of the constituent elements of the site, a maximum of 4 belts is allowed between the first drop and the press, up to and including the 2015 harvest.
### 3° – Date of placing on the market for the consumer
- For tirages carried out between 1 December 2010 and 30 November 2011, the wines may be placed on the market for the consumer after a minimum ageing period of 10 months from the date of tirage.
- For tirages carried out between 1 December 2011 and 30 November 2013, the wines may be placed on the market for the consumer after a minimum ageing period of 11 months from the date of tirage.
## XII. – Rules of presentation and labelling
### 1° – General provisions
Wines for which, under the terms of the present specification, the controlled appellation “Crémant de Bourgogne” is claimed and which are presented under this appellation may not be declared after the harvest, offered to the public, dispatched, put up for sale or sold unless, in the harvest declaration, in advertisements, on leaflets, labels, invoices and any containers whatsoever, the aforesaid controlled designation of origin is entered, accompanied by the words “Appellation d’origine contrôlée”, the whole in very conspicuous characters.
### 2° – Particular provisions
a) – On the labels, the name of the controlled designation of origin is written in very conspicuous characters. The dimensions of the largest characters appearing on the labels are not greater, both in height and in width, than twice those of the characters of “Crémant” and “Bourgogne”. The characters of “Crémant” and “Bourgogne” are of equal size.
b) – Bottles must be closed with a cork bearing the name of the controlled designation of origin on the part contained in the neck of the bottle.
# CHAPTER II
## I. – Declaration obligations
#### 1. Prior declaration of parcel assignment
Each operator declares to the defence and management body the list of its parcels assigned to the production of the controlled designation of origin before 31 March preceding the harvest. This declaration specifies in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- for the parcel(s) concerned: the cadastral reference, the area, the planting, the grape variety and the lieu-dit.
#### 2. Declaration of intention to produce
In the absence of a prior declaration of parcel assignment, the operator must submit to the defence and management body a declaration of intention to produce at the latest 72 hours before the start of the harvest. This declaration specifies in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- for the parcel(s) concerned: the cadastral reference, the area, the planting.
The prior declaration of parcel assignment serves as a declaration of intention to produce.
#### 3. Claim declaration for base wines
The claim declaration must be sent to the defence and management body at least fifteen days before circulation of the wines between authorised warehousekeepers and at the latest on 1 December of the year of harvest. It indicates in particular:
- the designation claimed;
- the grape variety(ies);
- the volume of the base wine;
- by grape variety, the volume of wine placed in reserve;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the name and address of the applicant;
- the place of storage of the base wine.
#### 4. Declaration prior to transaction and collections (base wines)
Every operator wishing to move base wines sends the defence and management body a transaction declaration for the lot concerned between the day the transaction is contracted and at least eight days before collection. If the volume actually collected differs by more or less than 10 % from the volume indicated at the time of contracting, the operator makes a correction available to the approved control body.
#### 5. Claim declaration
The claim declaration must be sent to the defence and management body at the latest at the end of the month in which the tirage was carried out. It indicates in particular:
- the name and address of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the designation claimed;
- the volume of wine, expressed in number of bottles;
- the colour of the wine;
- the tirage number;
- the place of storage of the wine.
#### 6. Downgrading declaration
Every operator carrying out a downgrading of wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin sends the defence and management body and the approved control body a quarterly summary.
#### 7. Reworking of parcels
Before any development or any works liable to modify the morphology, the subsoil, the arable layer (including any addition of exogenous soil) or the structuring elements of the landscape of a parcel intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin, with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works, a declaration must be sent by the operator to the defence and management body at least 4 weeks before the planned start date of the works. The defence and management body transmits, without delay, a copy of this declaration to the services of the National Institute of Origin and Quality.
## II. – Keeping of registers
The registers provided for in the present specification are kept and held available to the agents responsible for control, and can be communicated to them on prior request. They may be kept in any form (paper or electronic).
#### 1. Press book
Keeping a press book is compulsory. It is filled in as operations proceed. For each press load (marc), this book specifies:
- the date and time of the start of each operation;
- the weight of grapes processed, by grape variety;
- the commune of origin of the grapes;
- the name of the operator supplying the grapes;
- the volumes of musts obtained;
- the potential alcoholic strength by volume;
- the volumes of rebêches.
#### 2. Tirage register
Keeping a tirage register is compulsory. This register specifies:
- the start date of tirage;
- the end date of tirage;
- the volume used;
- the composition of the cuvée;
- the tirage number.
#### 3. Disgorging register
Every operator keeps an up-to-date register for each disgorging operation. This register indicates in particular:
- the start date of the operation;
- the tirage number(s) of the lot(s) concerned with the corresponding volume;
- the tirage date(s);
- the volume of wine, expressed in number of bottles, after disgorging;
- the lot number after disgorging.
