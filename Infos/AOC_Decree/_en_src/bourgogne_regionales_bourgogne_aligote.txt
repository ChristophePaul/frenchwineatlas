# CHAPTER I
## I. – Name of the designation
Only wines meeting the specific provisions set out below may claim the controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne aligoté”, initially recognised by the decree of 31 July 1937.
## II. – Geographical denominations and complementary terms
The name of the controlled designation of origin may be followed by, or supplemented with, the term “primeur” or “nouveau” for wines meeting the production conditions laid down for this term in the present specification.
## III. – Colour and types of product
The controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne aligoté” is reserved for still white wines.

### 2° – Other cultivation practices
a) – In order to preserve the characteristics of the physical and biological environment, which constitutes a fundamental element of the terroir:
- Permanent grass cover of the headlands is compulsory;
- Any substantial modification of the morphology, the subsoil, the arable layer or the elements that guarantee the integrity and sustainability of the soils of a parcel intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin is prohibited, with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works.
b) – Vine plantings and replacements are carried out with healthy plant material that has undergone hot-water treatment.
### 3° – Irrigation
Irrigation is prohibited.
## VII. – Harvest, transport and ripeness of the grapes
### 1° – Harvest
a) – The wines come from grapes harvested at good ripeness.
b) – Particular provisions for transport of the harvest
The harvest is protected from rain during its transport and on its reception.
### 2° – Ripeness of the grapes
a) – Sugar content of the grapes
Grapes with a sugar content of less than 161 grams per litre of must may not be considered to be at good ripeness.
b) – Minimum natural alcoholic strength by volume
The wines have a minimum natural alcoholic strength by volume of 10 %.
## VIII. – Yields – Entry into production
### 1° – Yield
The yield referred to in Article D. 645-7 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code is set at 72 hectolitres per hectare.
### 2° – Limit yield
The limit yield referred to in Article D. 645-7 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code is set at 77 hectolitres per hectare.
### 3° – Entry into production of young vines
The benefit of the controlled designation of origin may only be granted to wines coming from:
- parcels of young vines only from the 2nd year following that in the course of which planting was carried out in place before 31 July;
- parcels of young vines only from the 1st year following that in the course of which grafting in place was carried out before 31 July;
- parcels of vines that have been top-grafted, at the earliest in the 1st year following that in the course of which the top-grafting was carried out before 31 July, and once the parcels no longer contain any variety other than that permitted for the appellation. By derogation, in the year following that in the course of which the top-grafting was carried out before 31 July, the variety permitted for the appellation may represent only 80 % of the planting of each parcel concerned.
## IX. – Processing, elaboration, ageing, packaging, storage
### 1° – General provisions
The wines are vinified in accordance with local, fair and constant usages.
a) – Analytical standards
Finished wines, ready to be released for consumption within the meaning of Article D. 645-18-I of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code, have a maximum fermentable sugar content (glucose + fructose) of:
- 3 grams per litre;
- or 4 grams per litre if the total acidity is greater than or equal to 55.10 milliequivalents per litre, i.e. 4.13 grams per litre expressed as tartaric acid (or 2.7 grams per litre expressed as H2SO4).
b) – Oenological practices and physical treatments
- The use of pieces of wood is prohibited;
- After enrichment, the wines do not exceed a total alcoholic strength by volume of 12.5 %.
c) – Prohibited equipment
Continuous presses are prohibited.
d) – Vat capacity
Every operator has an overall vat capacity (vinification and storage) equivalent to at least the volume of wine vinified for the previous year’s harvest, as entered either on the harvest declaration, pro rata to the change in the holding’s area in production, or on the production declaration.
e) – Upkeep of the cellar and equipment
The cellar and the equipment are well maintained; this is reflected in particular by:
- general hygiene of the elaboration premises, with general cleanliness, well-maintained floors, adequate drainage and a surface avoiding stagnation;
- harmlessness of the equipment and products coming into contact with the wine;
- separation and specificity of the premises: premises without the same functions must be separated, such as storage areas for plant-protection products, cleaning products or hydrocarbons, from the premises for vinification, ageing and storage of dry goods (corks, cartons);
- management of wine effluents: effluents must be removed as quickly as possible from foodstuff premises and deposited in well-maintained containers that are easy to clean and can be closed; waste storage areas must be kept clean; waste must be disposed of hygienically and in an environmentally friendly manner; a waste storage and removal area must be provided;
- absence of hazardous or odorous substances in the vinification, ageing and storage premises (odour).
f) – Ageing
The temperature of the containers during the ageing phase is controlled and less than or equal to 25 °C.
### 2° – Provisions relating to packaging
For every packaged lot, the operator keeps available for the approved control body:
- the information appearing in the register of handling operations referred to in Article D. 645-18 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code;
- the reports of analyses carried out before or after packaging.
These reports are kept for a period of 6 months from the date of packaging.
### 3° – Provisions relating to storage
The operator provides evidence of a protected storage place for wines packaged in unlabelled bottles, with a temperature of between 5 °C and 22 °C.
### 4° – Provisions relating to the circulation of products and placing on the market for the consumer
a) – Date of placing on the market for the consumer
The wines are placed on the market for the consumer in accordance with the provisions of Article D. 645-17 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code.
b) – Period during which the wines may not circulate between authorised warehousekeepers
Wines eligible for the term “primeur” or “nouveau” may only circulate between authorised warehousekeepers from the 38th day preceding the 3rd Thursday of November of the year of harvest.
## X. – Link with the geographical area
### 1° – Information on the geographical area
a) – Description of the natural factors contributing to the link
The geographical area rests on the reliefs traditionally devoted to viticulture in the Yonne, Côte d’Or, Saône-et-Loire and Rhône departments. It groups together a set of more or less separate vineyards stretching for about 250 kilometres from north to south. It thus extends over the territory of more than 300 communes. Each of the vineyards is characterised by its own landscapes, geology and climate.
The vineyards of the Yonne and of the “Châtillonnais”, north of the “Côte d’Or”, are planted on the cuestas of the Paris Basin, on sediments dated to the Upper Jurassic, with the exception of the “Vézelien”, planted on Lower and Middle Jurassic formations, and the “Jovinien”, planted on Upper Cretaceous formations. The substrata are mainly marly (clayey limestones), locally calcareous. The vineyard is confined to the best-exposed reliefs of the cuestas, on the flanks of the main valleys draining the region, the Yonne and its tributaries, the Seine and its tributaries. It lies at altitudes of between 150 metres and 300 metres.
From Dijon to the outskirts of Lyon, the vineyards occupy an almost continuous series of linear reliefs. This is the western edge of the Bresse rift, a major tectonic structure that subsided during the Alpine uplift. The substrata are mainly sedimentary, limestones or marls, generally dated to the Jurassic, but also locally to the Triassic. Locally, mainly in Saône-et-Loire, outcrops of Primary-era metamorphic and granitic basement, generating acid soils, bear vines. Although the reliefs of the hinterland are sometimes high (650 metres in Côte-d’Or, 1,000 metres in the “Beaujolais”), the vines are confined to altitudes of between 250 metres and 400 metres.
Each of the vineyards is characterised by its own landscapes, geology and morphological style:
- broad valleys cutting wedge-like into the limestone plateaux of the Paris Basin;
- a thin continuous strip lying on the boundary between plain and plateau for “the Côte”;
- a series of parallel reliefs stretching on a north–south axis, the “chaînons” (ridges), in the “Mâconnais”.
“Wine-growing Burgundy” enjoys a rather cool oceanic climate. This climate is characterised by a moderate and regular rainfall regime, with no marked summer drought. Temperatures reflect its coolness, with an annual average of 11 °C. Well marked in the Yonne department, it is somewhat attenuated in the south-east of the geographical area by the screening role played by the relief of the Morvan and Charolais hills, which generates a foehn effect expressed in lower humidity and higher temperatures than the regional reference. The eastern part of the geographical area is affected by continental influences, expressed in relatively low winter temperatures and periods of frost that can be long and intense, but also in autumns that are sometimes very dry and sunny. Southern influences, perceptible above all in the southern part of the geographical area, can momentarily generate high summer temperatures and rising warm sea air, responsible for summer storms.
The parcels delimited for the harvesting of the grapes correspond to all the sectors traditionally recognised for their aptitude for viticulture. They thus preferentially occupy the well-exposed hillsides of the main reliefs, as well as the piedmonts, the cool plateaux and the back slopes facing north and west, all of these situations ensuring optimal drainage and good soil-warming capacity.
b) – Description of the human factors contributing to the link
The cultivation of the vine is attested from the Gallo-Roman period. In the Middle Ages, the vineyards of “Burgundy” had already acquired economic importance and were known beyond its borders. Auxerre produced the so-called “Basse Bourgogne” wines, while the “Côte d’Or” and part of the “Côte châlonnaise” were known for supplying the “Vins de Beaune”. The role of ecclesiastical structures (abbeys, bishoprics) and of the nobility in building the renown of the wines of “Burgundy” is well known. Cluny, Cîteaux, Pontigny, the Chapters of Langres and Autun and the Dukes of Burgundy each contributed in their own way to building a prestigious vineyard and to the influence of these wines.
In the 18th century, the significant development of the wine trade generated a new economic power. The wines of “Burgundy” were widely sold to the north of France and of Europe, and even to other continents. “Wine-growing Burgundy” became clearly individualised during the 19th century. Old (provinces) or recent (departments) administrative divisions combined with the economic dimension. Urban centres such as Auxerre, Dijon, Beaune, Chalon-sur-Saône or Mâcon ensured the distribution of the region’s wines. The north of the “Beaujolais” identified itself at that time with the wines of Mâcon. The “Tonnerrois” and the “Châtillonnais”, at the gates of Champagne, also turned towards Burgundy.
“Bourgogne aligoté” originates from the production of so-called “grands ordinaires” wines. These wines held an important place in the regional wine economy, supplying local consumption with wines of quality intermediate between “fine wines” and everyday production. In the 19th century and at the beginning of the 20th, wines from the aligoté B variety were gradually distinguished from the other so-called “grands ordinaires” wines. Between 1919 and 1930, various judgments gradually regulated production, before the controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne aligoté” was recognised by the decree of 31 July 1937.
The wines are produced from the aligoté B variety, an old Burgundian variety, probably originating from the “Hautes Côtes”. In 2008 the vineyard covered an area of about 1,600 hectares, for an annual production of nearly 100,000 hectolitres.
### 2° – Information on the quality and characteristics of the product
The wines are characterised by a robe with clear green highlights and an aromatic expression dominated by tangy notes. These characteristics are revealed by a short ageing, which most often gives wines to be drunk young. Wines from parcels situated on hillside slopes, with low yields, are wines of character with good longevity.
### 3° – Causal interactions
“Bourgogne” is a northern vineyard where the vine is subject to strong climatic constraint. Planting is therefore confined to the most favourable situations, concentrated on the main reliefs and enjoying both mesoclimates that are warmer and drier than the regional climate and free-draining soils able to remove excess water while ensuring good fertility and rapid warming.
The vineyard intended for the production of wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne aligoté” favours situations conducive to preserving a certain freshness, necessary for the aromatic expression of a fruity wine to be drunk young. It is thus planted on parcels with relatively heavy piedmont soils, particularly in the “Côte d’Or”, where it gives light wines characterised by good liveliness, archetypes of the refreshing “Bourgogne aligoté”. It is also planted on hillside parcels, where it makes the most of less fertile soils, giving more complex wines that can withstand ageing of 2 to 3 years or more. It is thus present in the “Hautes Côtes” de Nuits and de Beaune, in the “Côte châlonnaise” and in the Yonne.
Historically, wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne aligoté” found an intermediate place between the so-called “fine” wines, intended for a wealthy clientele and for export, and the “ordinary wines” supplying daily local consumption. Consumed mainly in France, they have an image of quality wine. They still keep a flattering and ultimately rather complex image. Generally light and tangy aperitif wines, some can sometimes find their place during a meal, alongside richer and more complex wines.
Wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne aligoté” remain an emblematic component of the wines of “Burgundy”. Their renown extends beyond regional borders, where they convey an image of original regional tradition. They have also found a place in the image of the region as an essential ingredient of the famous “Kir”, a blackcurrant aperitif made famous by Canon KIR, deputy-mayor of Dijon from 1945 to 1967 and a colourful figure. Their aromatic qualities allow them to be appreciated as fully-fledged quality dry white wines.
## XI. – Transitional measures
### 1° – Geographical area
Vine parcels in place on 29 May 2008, situated in the communes excluded from the geographical area by decision of the competent national committee of the National Institute of Origin and Quality at its session of 28 September 2011, continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up and at the latest up to and including the 2035 harvest, provided that:
- by 31 July 2010, at least one harvest has been declared under the controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne aligoté” in the harvest declaration;
- they comply with the other provisions of the present specification.
The list of parcels, giving for each of them its cadastral references and area, may be consulted at the services of the National Institute of Origin and Quality and at the defence and management body.
### 2° – Training methods
a) – Vine parcels in place on 31 July 2009, situated in the Yonne department, with a planting density of less than 5,000 plants per hectare continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up.
b) – Vine parcels in place before 1980, situated outside the delimited vineyard of the controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne” followed by the complementary geographical denominations “Hautes Côtes de Beaune” and “Hautes Côtes de Nuits”, which do not comply with the provisions relating to planting density set out in the present specification and are trained on a “single-plane” trellis, continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up, provided that they:
- have a planting density greater than 3,000 plants per hectare;
- are pruned with a maximum of 6 free eyes per square metre;
- have an external leaf-canopy surface of at least 1.30 square metres for the production of one kilogram of grapes.
The volume that may be claimed under the controlled designation of origin is calculated on the basis of the yield authorised for the controlled designation of origin multiplied by a coefficient of 0.80.
c) – Vine parcels in place on the date of approval of the present specification, situated in the delimited vineyard of the controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne” followed by the complementary geographical denominations “Hautes Côtes de Beaune” and “Hautes Côtes de Nuits”, which do not comply with the provisions relating to planting density set out in the present specification and are trained on a “single-plane” trellis, continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up, provided that they:
- have a planting density greater than 3,000 plants per hectare;
- are pruned with a maximum of 6 free eyes per square metre;
- have an external leaf-canopy surface of at least 1.30 square metres for the production of one kilogram of grapes.
d) – Vine parcels in place on 31 July 2009 trained according to the so-called “lyre” system continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up, provided that they:
- have a minimum planting density of 3,000 plants per hectare, with a spacing between rows of less than 3.5 metres and a spacing between plants within the same row of between 0.80 metre and 1 metre;
- are pruned in double Guyot or trained as double Royat cordon; overlapping of canes or cordons is prohibited; each plant bears a maximum of 26 free eyes and the maximum number of free eyes per square metre is 8;
- have a height of trellised foliage of at least 1.20 metres, measured between the lower trellising wire and the upper trimming limit; the trellising consists of two planes that diverge towards the top; the distance between the trellising planes is at least 0.50 metre at the base and between 1 metre and 1.40 metres at the top.
## XII. – Rules of presentation and labelling
### 1° – General provisions
Wines for which, under the terms of the present specification, the controlled designation of origin “Bourgogne aligoté” is claimed and which are presented under the said appellation may not be declared after the harvest, offered to the public, dispatched, put up for sale or sold unless, in the harvest declaration, in advertisements, on leaflets, labels, invoices and any containers whatsoever, the aforesaid controlled designation of origin is entered.
### 2° – Particular provisions
a) – Wines benefiting from the term “primeur” or “nouveau” must be presented with the indication of the vintage, which appears on the label bearing all the compulsory particulars.
b) – The labelling of wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin may specify the name of a smaller geographical unit, provided that:
- it is the name of a registered lieu-dit (cadastral locality);
- it appears on the harvest declaration.
The name of the registered lieu-dit is printed in characters whose dimensions, both in height and in width, are not larger than half those of the characters making up the name of the controlled designation of origin.
# CHAPTER II
## I. – Declaration obligations
#### 1. Claim declaration
The claim declaration is sent to the defence and management body at least fifteen days before circulation between authorised warehousekeepers and at the latest on 10 December of the year of harvest.
It indicates in particular:
- the designation claimed;
- the volume of the wine;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the name and address of the applicant;
- the place of storage of the wine.
It is accompanied in particular by a copy of the harvest declaration and, as the case may be, a copy of the production declaration or an extract of the stock accounts for buyers of grapes and musts.
#### 2. Declaration prior to transaction and collections
Every operator wishing to sell in bulk a wine benefiting from the controlled designation of origin makes a transaction declaration for the lot concerned to the approved control body within time limits set in the control plan of between six and fifteen working days before any collection.
This declaration, accompanied where applicable by a copy of the purchase contract, specifies in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the identification of the lot;
- the volume of the lot;
- the identification of the containers;
- the identity of the buyer.
If collections are made for volumes smaller than those set out in the transaction declaration, the operator informs the approved control body in writing.
#### 3. Declaration of release for consumption
Every operator declares to the approved control body each lot of wine intended to be released for consumption within the meaning of Article D. 645-18-I of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code.
This declaration may also be drawn up for lots already packaged. It is made within time limits set in the control plan of between six and fifteen working days before release for consumption or before the lots concerned are dispatched from the operator’s cellars.
It specifies in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the identification of the lot;
- the volume of the lot;
- the lot number for wines already packaged;
- the identification of the containers for non-packaged wines.
However, making available to the approved control body the register referred to in Article D. 645-18 II of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code serves as the declaration of release for consumption, under the conditions set out in the control plan.
#### 4. Declaration relating to the dispatch outside the national territory of a non-packaged wine
Every operator wishing to dispatch outside the national territory a non-packaged wine benefiting from the controlled designation of origin declares it to the approved control body within time limits set in the control plan of between six and fifteen working days before any dispatch.
#### 5. Fallback declaration
Every operator carrying out a fallback of a wine benefiting from the controlled designation of origin into a more general controlled designation of origin sends the defence and management body and the approved control body a monthly declaration at the latest on the 1st working day following the tenth day of each month.
This declaration indicates in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the controlled designation of origin under which the wine was claimed;
- the more general controlled designation of origin of the fallback;
- the volume concerned by the fallback;
- the vintage;
- the state of the lot concerned (bulk, bottle, hermetically sealed vacuum containers…).
The approved control body transmits without delay a copy of the monthly declaration to the approved control body for the more general controlled designation of origin concerned.
The defence and management body transmits without delay to the defence and management body of the more general controlled designation of origin concerned the list of operators who have made a monthly fallback declaration, together with a summary statement indicating:
- the more general controlled designation of origin of the fallback;
- the volume concerned by the fallback;
- the vintage(s);
- the state of the lot(s) concerned (bulk, bottle, hermetically sealed vacuum containers…).
#### 6. Downgrading declaration
Every operator carrying out a downgrading of wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin sends the defence and management body and the approved control body a monthly declaration within time limits set in the control plan. This declaration indicates in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the volume downgraded;
- the vintage;
- the date of the downgrading.
#### 7. Reworking of parcels
Before any development or any works liable to modify the morphology, the subsoil, the arable layer (including any addition of exogenous soil) or the elements that guarantee the integrity and sustainability of the soils of a parcel intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin, with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works, the operator sends a declaration to the defence and management body at least four weeks before the planned start date of the works.
The defence and management body transmits, without delay, a copy of this declaration to the services of the National Institute of Origin and Quality.
## II. – Keeping of registers
#### General plan of the storage and vinification places
Every vinifying operator keeps up to date and available for the approved control body a general plan of the storage and vinification places, allowing in particular the number, designation and capacity of the containers to be identified.
