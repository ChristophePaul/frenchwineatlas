# CHAPTER I
## I. – Name of the designation
Only wines meeting the specific provisions set out below may claim the controlled designation of origin “Brouilly”, initially recognised by the decree of 19 October 1938.
## II. – Geographical denominations and complementary terms
No particular provision.
## III. – Colour and types of product
The controlled designation of origin “Brouilly” is reserved for still red wines.
## IV. – Areas and zones in which the various operations are carried out
### 1° – Geographical area
The harvesting of the grapes, the vinification, the elaboration and the ageing of the wines take place within the territory of the following communes of the Rhône department, on the basis of the 2022 Official Geographic Code: Cercié, Charentay, Odenas, Quincié-en-Beaujolais, Saint-Etienne-la-Varenne and Saint-Lager.
The cartographic documents representing the geographical area may be consulted on the INAO website.
### 2° – Delimited parcel area
The wines come exclusively from vines situated within the delimited parcel production area as approved by the National Institute of Origin and Quality at the sessions of the competent national committee of 5 November 1985 and 17 September 1986.
The National Institute of Origin and Quality lodges with the town halls of the communes referred to in 1° the graphic documents establishing the parcel boundaries of the production area thus approved.

## V. – Grape varieties
### 1° – Grape varieties
The wines are produced from the following varieties:
- main variety: gamay N;
- accessory varieties: aligoté B, chardonnay B and melon B.
### 2° – Proportion rules on the holding
The accessory varieties are authorised only as mixed plantings within the vines. Their total proportion is limited to 15 % within each parcel.
## VI. – Management of the vineyard
### 1° – Training methods
a) – Planting density
**GENERAL PROVISIONS**
The vines have a minimum planting density of 6,000 plants per hectare. These vines have a spacing between rows of less than or equal to 2.10 metres and a spacing between plants within the same row of greater than or equal to 0.80 metre.
**PARTICULAR PROVISIONS**
Subject to compliance with the minimum density of 6,000 plants per hectare, and for the purposes of mechanisation, the vines may have inter-rows with a spacing of less than or equal to 3 metres.
These provisions do not apply to parcels planted on terraces. A vine parcel planted on terraces means a parcel benefiting from a particular development linked to the existing slope, carried out before the vines were planted, entailing a discontinuity in the usual planting spacing and making it impossible for machinery to pass between two successive levels. For vine parcels planted on terraces, the spacing between plants within the same row is greater than or equal to 0.80 metre.
b) – Pruning rules
- Pruning is completed by 15 May;
- The wines come from vines with short pruning (trained in gobelet, fan, or single, double or “charmet” Royat cordon) with a maximum of 10 free eyes per plant;
- Each plant bears 3 to 5 spurs with a maximum of 2 free eyes each; with a view to rejuvenation, each plant may also bear a spur with a maximum of 2 free eyes pruned on a water shoot growing from old wood;
- During formative pruning or a change of pruning method, the vines are pruned with a maximum of 12 free eyes per plant.
c) – Trellising and foliage height rules
- Where the average spacing between rows is greater than or equal to 1.50 metres, the vines are trained on a fixed trellis;
- The trellising is careful, solid and inert, with at least one carrying wire and one pair of lifting wires;
- The height of trellised foliage is at least equal to 0.6 times the average spacing between rows, the height of trellised foliage being measured between the lower limit of the foliage, set at least 0.30 metre above the ground, and the upper trimming limit, set at least 0.20 metre above the upper trellising wire(s);
- For untrellised vines, the height of foliage is also at least equal to 0.6 times the average spacing between rows;
- For vine parcels planted on terraces, the height of trellised foliage provides 1.4 square metres of external leaf-canopy surface for the production of one kilogram of grapes.
d) – Average maximum load per parcel
The average maximum load per parcel is set at 10,000 kilograms per hectare.
e) – Threshold of missing vines
- The percentage of dead or missing vine plants referred to in Article D. 645-4 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code is set at 20 %;
- For vines with an initial planting density greater than 8,000 plants per hectare, the percentage of dead or missing vine plants referred to in Article D. 645-4 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code is set at 30 %.
f) – Cultivation condition of the vine
The parcels are managed so as to ensure a good overall cultivation condition of the vine, in particular its sanitary condition and the upkeep of its soil.
g) – Establishment and planting of the vineyard
Before each new planting, every operator carries out a physico-chemical analysis of the soil of the parcel in order to have all the information needed to know the wine-growing situation and its potential.
### 2° – Other cultivation practices
a) – The addition of exogenous soil to parcels of the delimited parcel area is prohibited. Exogenous soil means soil that does not come from the delimited parcel area of the controlled designation of origin “Brouilly”.
b) – In order to preserve the characteristics of the physical and biological environment, which constitutes a fundamental element of the terroir:
- Permanent grass cover of the headlands is compulsory;
- Where the spacing between rows is greater than 1.50 metres, the inter-row has a controlled plant cover, spontaneous or sown, at least during winter and early spring;
- Any substantial modification of the morphology, the subsoil, the arable layer or the elements guaranteeing the integrity and durability of the soils of a parcel intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin is prohibited, with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works; the elements guaranteeing the integrity and durability of the soils are maintained in accordance with usage.
c) – Hot-water treatment of standard plants and of plants from private nurseries is compulsory.
### 3° – Irrigation
Irrigation is prohibited.
## VII. – Harvest, transport and ripeness of the grapes
### 1° – Harvest
a) – The wines come from grapes harvested at good ripeness.
b) – Particular provisions for harvesting and transport of the harvest
**GENERAL PROVISIONS**
Sorting of the harvest is compulsory. Sorting is carried out either in the vineyard or at the winery, in the latter case provided suitable equipment can be shown. Sorting is defined as the removal or separation of bunches of grapes or berries affected by disease (botrytis, powdery mildew, etc.) or of insufficiently ripe bunches and berries.
**PROVISIONS RELATING TO MECHANICAL HARVESTING**
a) – The height of the harvest in the containers used to transport it from the parcel to the vinification cellar does not exceed 0.50 metre;
b) – The containers are made of inert, food-grade material;
c) – The harvesting and harvest-transport equipment has an appropriate water-drainage or protection system.
### 2° – Ripeness of the grapes

b) – For vines planted on terraces, the volume that may benefit from the controlled designation of origin is the product of the area equal to the number of plants actually planted on the parcel concerned [N] multiplied by an area of 2 square metres, and the yield of the controlled designation of origin (R, in hectolitres per hectare), i.e. the formula (N × 2) × (R / 10,000). The area equal to the number of plants actually planted on the parcel concerned multiplied by an area of 2 square metres per plant may not exceed the cadastral area of the parcel.
### 2° – Entry into production of young vines
The benefit of the controlled designation of origin may only be granted to wines coming from:
- parcels of young vines only from the 2nd year following that in the course of which planting was carried out in place before 31 July;
- parcels of young vines only from the 1st year following that in the course of which grafting in place was carried out before 31 July;
- parcels of vines that have been top-grafted, at the earliest in the 1st year following that in the course of which the top-grafting was carried out before 31 July, and once the parcels no longer contain any varieties other than those permitted for the appellation. By derogation, in the year following that in the course of which the top-grafting was carried out before 31 July, the varieties permitted for the appellation may represent only 80 % of the planting of each parcel concerned.
## IX. – Processing, elaboration, ageing, packaging, storage
### 1° – General provisions
The wines are vinified in accordance with local, fair and constant usages.
a) – Blending of varieties
Wines produced from parcels planted with accessory varieties are vinified by blending the grapes concerned.
b) – Malolactic fermentation
At the packaging stage, the wines have a maximum malic acid content of 0.4 gram per litre.
c) – Analytical standards
Finished wines ready to be released for consumption within the meaning of Article D. 645-18-I of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code meet the following analytical standards:
| ANALYTICAL PARAMETER | CONTENT |
| Maximum fermentable sugar content (glucose and fructose) (grams per litre) | 3 |
| Maximum volatile acidity (milliequivalents per litre) | 14.17 |
d) – Oenological practices and physical treatments
- The use of pieces of wood is prohibited;
- Subtractive enrichment techniques (TSE) are authorised within the limit of a concentration rate of 10 %;
- After enrichment, the wines have a total alcoholic strength by volume less than or equal to 13 %.
e) – Vat capacity
The overall vat capacity is at least equivalent to the product of the area in production under the controlled designation of origin and the yield set in 1° of point VIII.
f) – Temperature control
- The vinification cellar offers sufficient conditions for controlling the temperature of the vinification vats;
- The vats used to vinify grapes from mechanical harvesting are equipped with fermentation temperature regulation systems.
g) – Upkeep of the cellar and equipment
The cellar (floors and walls) and the vinification equipment are in a good general state of upkeep.
### 2° – Provisions by type of product
The wines are aged at least until 15 January of the year following that of the harvest.
### 3° – Provisions relating to packaging
For every packaged lot, the operator keeps available for the approved control body:
- the information appearing in the register of handling operations referred to in Article D. 645-18 of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code;
- the reports of analyses carried out before or after packaging. These analysis reports are kept for a period of 6 months from the date of packaging.
### 4° – Provisions relating to storage
a) – The operator provides evidence of a specific place for storing packaged products. The temperature of the storage premises is controlled by suitable insulation and is less than or equal to 25 °C. The premises are equipped with temperature-measuring equipment.
b) – The temperature of the containers during the keeping of the wines is controlled and less than or equal to 25 °C.
### 5° – Provisions relating to placing on the market for the consumer
At the end of the ageing period, the wines are placed on the market for the consumer from 1 February of the year following that of the harvest.
## X. – Link with the geographical area
### 1° – Information on the geographical area
a) – Description of the natural factors contributing to the link
The geographical area lies at the heart of the Beaujolais vineyard, 40 kilometres north of Lyon, between the towns of Villefranche-sur-Saône and Mâcon. It encircles the “Mont Brouilly”, a small mountain detached from the eastern edge of the “Monts du Beaujolais”. It thus extends over the territory of 6 communes of the Rhône department.
The subsoil is mainly made up of acid Palaeozoic formations:
- granites, forming slopes with sandy, poor soils, to the west of the “Mont Brouilly”;
- porphyries (massive metamorphic rocks) giving more clayey and stonier soils to the south of the “Mont Brouilly”.
To the east and north, recent spreads mask the substratum. Debris from the mountain (fragments of porphyry, granitic arènes) mixes with a clay-siliceous matrix (“plateau de Briante”, “côte de Pisse Vieille”). Locally, on the eastern boundary of the geographical area, clay-limestone formations appear.
The climate is a temperate, modified oceanic climate, subject to clear continental influences (summer storms, freezing fog in winter) and above all southern influences (summer heat, rainfall peaks in autumn and spring). Sheltered from westerly winds by the “Monts du Beaujolais”, the vineyard, generally east-facing, enjoys optimal sunshine. From dawn, the first rays of the sun warm and light up the slope. The vineyard planted on the slope, at an altitude of between 250 metres and 400 metres, most often escapes the spring frosts and morning mists of the Saône plain (175 metres above sea level), and its soils rapidly drain any excess rainwater.
b) – Description of the human factors contributing to the link
The name “Brouilly” seems to come from the name of a Roman lieutenant, BRULIUS, who in the 4th century is said to have settled on the slopes of the “mount” and planted a few vines there. The hill still bears this name, dominating the landscape from its height of 485 metres. No village bears the name “Brouilly”.
Various writings attest to the presence of vines on the hill at very different periods. In the 4th and 5th centuries, the lower parts of the “Brouilly” mountain, to the south, were planted with small vine parcels. When the Lords of BEAUJEU founded the abbey of Belleville, they gave it, in 1179, vines situated in the “clos de Brouilly”. Until the 17th century, the production of “Brouilly” was drunk by connoisseurs of the region and as far as Lyon, where many vineyard owners lived. Here, as everywhere in “Beaujolais”, holdings are family-sized (6 to 8 hectares on average). They often belong to large estates and châteaux where part of the production is vinified in a shared vat house.
All the existing documents tell us that, until the 1910s, the name “Brouilly” was used only for wines whose grapes were harvested exclusively on the slopes of the hill. Gradually, the surrounding vineyards claimed this name. During the First World War in particular, some producers, in order to escape requisition by the Army Commissariat, marketed their wine under this name. At that time, the “Brouilly” vineyard covered an area of 400 hectares. This extension was ratified by the joint commission of representatives of the growers and the wine trade of Burgundy in 1919. In 1934, the producers set up their defence syndicate and, in 1938, the controlled designation of origin “Brouilly” was recognised.
The controlled designation of origin “Brouilly” is characterised by the combination of traditions specific to the region and modern techniques. The vineyard is devoted exclusively to the production of red wines, made essentially from gamay N. This variety is sensitive to late frost and fears sunscald. Producers seeking a quality wine have learned to control its growth, in particular through high planting density and short spur pruning, with “gobelet” training.
In order best to preserve the fruity character of the wines, producers practise a typically Beaujolais vinification inspired by “semi-carbonic maceration”. To ensure optimal extraction of the aromatic and polyphenolic compounds of the grapes, many producers use techniques that immerse the grapes in the juice during fermentation, such as the “grillage”, punching down or pumping over. Maceration frequently lasts more than 10 days. Packaging takes place after a few months of ageing.
To ensure good ripeness of the grapes, the producer makes sure that the exposed leaf area is sufficient. The vines may thus be trained on a fixed trellis, which also facilitates mechanisation.
In 2010, the “Brouilly” vineyard covered an area of 1,260 hectares, with an annual production of about 70,000 hectolitres.
### 2° – Information on the quality and characteristics of the products
“Brouilly” is a still red wine. It generally has a ruby colour, more purplish when the wine comes from vines planted on granitic formations, darker if the wine comes from the eastern part of the vineyard. On the nose, it gives off fruity aromas dominated by red fruit. On the palate, it combines suppleness, flesh and finesse. Among the “crus du Beaujolais”, it is reputed to be the most tender of all.
### 3° – Causal interactions
The barrier formed by the “Monts du Beaujolais”, rising to almost 1,000 metres west of the geographical area, gives the vines a favourable mesoclimate, sheltering them from winds and rain coming from the west. The morphology of the slopes, at an altitude of between 250 metres and 400 metres, provides protection against spring frosts and the morning mists of the Saône plain (175 m above sea level), and ensures optimal sunshine and rapid drainage of any excess rainwater. The opening onto the vast Saône plain favours luminosity conducive to the chlorophyll activity of the plant. The elevated situation and the south- and sunrise-facing exposure allow optimal and regular ripening of the grapes. The parcels precisely delimited for the harvesting of the grapes are planted on slopes, at altitudes of between 250 metres and 400 metres, and have acid, well-drained soils.
In these particular geographical conditions, the producers of “Brouilly” have developed, over the generations, techniques for getting the best out of the gamay N variety, a variety of low vigour but fertile and well adapted, which they have selected and adopted. The lessons of history have led producers to tame this variety and to prefer, for example, short pruning so as not to exhaust the vines and to prepare a quality harvest.
The climatic and soil conditions give the gamay N variety an earliness that allows the wines to be enjoyed from the spring following the harvest, justifying the saying “les crus du Beaujolais ont fait leurs Pâques” (the Beaujolais crus have done their Easter). Despite a relative diversity, the wines are characterised by a certain homogeneity and a good compromise between fruit and structure. Cuvées from parcels delimited for the harvesting of the grapes and planted on granite have more pronounced fruit and are enjoyed young. Those from parcels planted on schistose soils in the central part of the vineyard have a deeper colour and real assets for good keeping.
The “Mont Brouilly” is a remarkable landmark. Its sides are carpeted with vines and its wooded summit, rising to 484 metres, is crowned by a small chapel. The site strongly marks the identity of its inhabitants, to the point of giving its name to their most renowned products. “Côte de Brouilly”, produced on the hill itself, and “Brouilly”, in its immediate vicinity, are presented together at the “Cuvage des Brouilly”, the producers’ cellar located in Saint-Lager, “international village of the vine and wine”, at the heart of the geographical area.
## XI. – Transitional measures
### 1° – Planting density, average maximum load per parcel and volume eligible for the right to the controlled designation of origin
a) – Up to and including the 2031 harvest, the partial grubbing-up of vine parcels in place on 28 November 2004 is authorised, which may lead, after adaptation, to a minimum planting density of 5,000 plants per hectare. These vine parcels continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up and at the latest up to and including the 2034 harvest. For these vine parcels:
- The spacing between rows may not exceed 2.80 metres and the spacing between plants within the same row may not exceed 1.10 metres;
- The average maximum load per parcel is set at 9,000 kilograms per hectare;
- The volume that may benefit from the right to the controlled designation of origin is established on the basis of the yield authorised for the controlled designation of origin, for the harvest concerned, and:
> – multiplied by a coefficient of 0.9 for vine parcels with a planting density of between 5,000 and 6,000 plants/ha whose partial grubbing-up took place up to and including the 2015 harvest;
> – multiplied by a reduction coefficient equal to the ratio of the planting density of the parcel after partial grubbing-up, between 5,000 and 6,000 plants/ha, divided by the minimum planting density defined in 1° a) of VI of Chapter I of the present specification, for vine parcels whose partial grubbing-up takes place from the date of approval of the present specification.
b) – Vine parcels in place on 31 July 2009 with a spacing between plants within the same row of less than 0.80 metre continue to benefit, for their harvest, from the right to the controlled designation of origin until they are grubbed up.
### 2° – Trellising rules
The provision relating to the obligation of a carrying wire does not apply to vine parcels in place on 31 July 2009 and pruned with spurs.
## XII. – Rules of presentation and labelling
### 1° – General provisions
Wines for which, under the terms of the present specification, the controlled designation of origin “Brouilly” is claimed and which are presented under the said appellation may not be declared after the harvest, offered to the public, dispatched, put up for sale or sold unless, in the harvest declaration, in advertisements, on leaflets, labels, invoices and any containers whatsoever, the aforesaid controlled designation of origin is entered.
### 2° – Particular provisions
a) – The labelling of wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin may specify the name of a smaller geographical unit, provided that:
- it is the name of a registered cadastral locality (lieu-dit cadastré);
- it appears on the harvest declaration.
The name of the registered cadastral locality is entered immediately after the name of the controlled designation of origin and printed in characters whose dimensions, both in height and in width, are not greater than those of the characters making up the name of the controlled designation of origin.
b) – The labelling of wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin may specify the larger geographical unit “Vin du Beaujolais”, “Grand Vin du Beaujolais” or “Cru du Beaujolais”. The dimensions of the characters of the larger geographical unit are not greater, both in height and in width, than two-thirds of those of the characters making up the name of the controlled designation of origin.
# CHAPTER II
## I. – Declaration obligations
#### 1. Claim declaration
The claim declaration is sent to the approved control body within a time limit set in the control plan, which may not be later than 31 December of the year of harvest. The approved control body transmits the information to the defence and management body without delay.
It indicates in particular:
- the controlled designation of origin under which the wine was claimed;
- the volume of the wine;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the name and address of the applicant;
- the place of storage of the wine.
It is accompanied by a copy of the harvest declaration and, as the case may be, a copy of the production declaration or an extract of the stock accounts for buyers of grapes and musts.
#### 2. Declaration prior to transaction and collection
Every operator declares each bulk transaction to the approved control body within time limits set in the control plan. It specifies in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the controlled designation of origin under which the wine was claimed;
- the identification of the lot;
- the volume of the lot;
- the identification of the containers;
- the identity of the buyer;
- the number of the interprofessional purchase contract, where applicable.
Collection may only take place once the follow-up given to this declaration by the approved control body is known.
In the case of collections of volumes smaller than those set out in the transaction declaration, the operator informs the approved control body in writing.
#### 3. Declaration of release for consumption within the meaning of Article D. 645-18-I of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code
Each lot of wine intended for release for consumption within the meaning of Article D. 645-18-I of the Rural and Maritime Fishing Code is the subject of a declaration to the approved control body, in accordance with the procedures set in the control plan, before release for consumption or before the lots concerned are dispatched from the operator’s cellars. It specifies in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the controlled designation of origin under which the wine was claimed;
- the identification of the lot;
- the volume of the lot;
- the lot number for wines already packaged;
- the identification of the containers for non-packaged wines;
- the date of packaging, where applicable.
#### 4. Derogatory system
Operators may opt for a quarterly summary declaration of the declarations referred to in points 3 and 6. Any quarterly summary declaration must be sent to the approved control body no later than 10 working days after the end of the quarter concerned.
#### 5. Declaration relating to the dispatch outside the national territory of a non-packaged wine
Every operator wishing to dispatch outside the national territory a non-packaged wine benefiting from the controlled designation of origin declares it to the approved control body before any dispatch, taking into account the time needed by the control body to process the declaration, which is set in the control plan. Collection may only take place once the follow-up given to this declaration by the approved control body is known.
#### 6. Fallback declaration
Every operator carrying out a fallback of a wine benefiting from the controlled designation of origin to a more general controlled designation of origin sends the defence and management body and the approved control body a declaration:
- at the same time as the declaration prior to transaction, if the wine is the subject of a bulk transaction after the fallback;
- at the same time as the declaration of release for consumption, if the wine is released for consumption after the fallback;
- within time limits set in the control plan following the recording of the fallback in the wine register, if the wine is the subject of a fallback after the declaration of release for consumption.
This declaration indicates in particular:
- the identity of the operator;
- the EVV or SIRET number;
- the more general controlled designation of origin of the fallback;
- the volume subject to the fallback;
- the vintage;
- the state of the lot subject to the fallback (bulk, bottle, airtight vacuum containers, …);
- the date of the fallback.
The defence and management body and the approved control body send, respectively and without delay, a copy of the declaration to the defence and management body of the more general appellation concerned and to the approved control body for the more general appellation concerned.
#### 7. Downgrading declaration
Every operator carrying out a downgrading of wines benefiting from the controlled designation of origin sends the defence and management body and the approved control body a quarterly summary.
#### 8. Declaration of equipment for TSE
Every operator holding a concentration apparatus declares it upon purchase to the defence and management body, specifying its specifications. The defence and management body keeps an up-to-date list of operators holding such equipment and sends it each year to the services of the National Institute of Origin and Quality at the latest on 1 September.
Every operator using a service provider declares it to the defence and management body, which draws up the list of these operators and sends it each year to the services of the National Institute of Origin and Quality at the latest on 1 September.
#### 9. Reworking of parcels
Before any addition of soil, any development or any works liable to modify the morphology, the subsoil, the arable layer or the elements guaranteeing the integrity and durability of the soils of a parcel intended for the production of the controlled designation of origin, with the exception of classic deep-ploughing works, the operator sends a declaration to the defence and management body at least four weeks before the planned start date of the works. The defence and management body transmits, without delay, a copy of this declaration to the services of the National Institute of Origin and Quality.
#### 10. Declaration of adaptation of vine density by partial grubbing-up
Every operator adapting the density of vines by partial grubbing-up sends a declaration to the defence and management body at the latest on 31 July of the wine-growing year in which this operation is carried out.
For the parcel(s) or parts of parcels concerned, the declaration specifies:
- the cadastral reference;
- the area;
- the new planting density.
## II. – Keeping of registers
#### 1. General plan of the storage places
Every vinifying operator keeps up to date and available for the approved control body a general plan of the storage places, allowing in particular the number, designation and capacity of the containers to be identified.
#### 2. TSE register
Every operator carrying out partial concentration of musts keeps an up-to-date TSE register including in particular:
- the initial volume;
- the volume of water evaporated;
- the identification of the lot after concentration (volume and potential alcoholic strength).
