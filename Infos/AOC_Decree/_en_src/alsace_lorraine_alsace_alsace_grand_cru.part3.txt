@@H1 Froehn
@@NATA Froehn
The Vosges massif offering protection against the humid, cool north-westerly winds, the vineyard finds its ideal conditions of establishment here. The Froehn is a terroir unique of its kind, since it spreads over 180°, from south-west to east, at the foot of the small medieval village of Zellenberg (“cell on the mountain”). The delimitation therefore refers to this typical landscape.
@@NATC Froehn
The grand cru is well ventilated, with significant temperature variations in spring and autumn. Situated right in a fault zone, its geology is predominantly calcareous (marls, sandy limestone, carbonated sandstone), giving rich, deep soils that do not suffer from drought. These types of soil, combined with the climate, allow early and even ripening of the grapes.

#### 2° – Information on the quality and characteristics of the products
White wines for laying down par excellence, they are characterised by a marked freshness based on a dominant tartaric acidity linked to good ripeness of the grapes. The name of the appellation may be supplemented by customary denominations, provided that the wines come exclusively from the varieties that may be designated under the denomination concerned. They show great substance, much complexity and strong aromatic power with various nuances. Of great length on the palate, they gain in complexity over time.
Four types of wine can be distinguished:
- dry, mineral wines: often golden yellow with green highlights, they have a charming fruitiness while provoking strong salivation, marked by a long and complex acidity;
- aromatic, fruity, rich, full-bodied wines: often of high ripeness and golden in colour, they frequently prove rich in sugar while retaining an airy, digestible character;
- wines benefiting from the term “vendanges tardives” often display very exotic aromas of candied fruit, with a fresh finish;
- wines benefiting from the term “sélection de grains nobles” are more concentrated, powerful wines, often showing aromas of fruit paste.
These two types of wine show a deep colour intensity ranging up to amber yellow. Wines benefiting from these last two terms are sweet wines of very long keeping. They are of remarkable concentration and great aromatic persistence. The terms “vendanges tardives” and “sélection de grains nobles” are systematically followed by the denominations Gewurztraminer, Muscat, Muscat Ottonel, Pinot gris and Riesling, and the indication of the vintage is compulsory.
#### 3° – Causal interactions
@@CAU1 Froehn
The calcareous dominance and the depth of the soils give aromatic, fruity, rich and complex wines with the same expression of acidity followed by a significant minerality that increases with ageing, whatever the level of sweetness. The Liassic marls over ferruginous sandy limestones, which are the particular feature of the Froehn grand cru, give breadth and generosity to the wines in their youth and minerality as they age. The attack is tight and centred, then opens up with amplitude, its substance enveloping the acidity. The powerful character of the marls gives a powerful, enveloping structure to the rich wines, accompanied by a slight bitterness that supports the length of the wine. Wines of great ripeness take a long time to open up. Full of subtlety, their palates are often marked by a fine salinity.

@@H1 Geisberg
@@NATA Geisberg
The Vosges massif offering protection against the humid, cool north-westerly winds, the vineyard finds its ideal conditions of establishment on the well-exposed, steeply sloping hillsides of the sub-Vosgian hills. The delimitation therefore refers to this typical landscape.
@@NATC Geisberg
Perfectly homogeneous geologically, the grand cru stems from a substratum formed essentially of dolomites and dolomitic marls, overlain by variegated sandy marls with gypsum at the top. The soils are clayey and stony. The vineyard is situated on steep slopes, magnificently exposed to the sun (full south) and marked by the presence of numerous low walls. The cool night winds (“Tahlwendala”) coming out of the valley bring cool summer temperatures that allow slow ripening of the harvest.

@@H1 Hengst
@@NAT Hengst
The grand cru is established on a slender ridge in the commune of Wintzenheim. The deep, stony soil consists essentially of Oligocene conglomerates and calcareous marls.
@@HB
@@HUM1
As a translation of this usage, the controlled designation of origin “Alsace grand cru Hengst” was recognised in 1983 for white wines and then in 2022 for red wines. The wines come from parcels that have been the subject of a rigorous and precise delimitation inspired by the historical cores of production. For white wines, the only varieties selected are the following: riesling B, gewurztraminer Rs, pinot gris G, muscat à petits grains blancs B, muscat à petits grains roses Rs, muscat ottonel B. They may be vinified and marketed under their respective denomination. For red wines, only pinot noir N is authorised.
Since the law of 5 July 1972, the wines must be bottled within the Bas-Rhin and Haut-Rhin departments, traditionally in the “Vin du Rhin”-type flute described in the decree of 1955.
The vineyard, trained on high trellises in order to benefit from an optimal leaf area, is subject to more restrictive production conditions than the controlled designation of origin “Alsace”. The minimum planting density is 4,500 plants per hectare for the production of white wine and 5,500 plants per hectare for the production of red wine. The grapes are harvested by hand. Wines benefiting from the terms “vendanges tardives” and “sélection de grains nobles” come from overripe and fragile grapes, also harvested by hand. These wines must undergo a minimum ageing period of 18 months. The red wines, for their part, are not enriched in any way. They must undergo a minimum ageing period of 11 months.

@@H1 Kaefferkopf
@@NATA Kaefferkopf
The Vosges massif offering protection against the humid, cool north-westerly winds, the vineyard finds its ideal conditions of establishment on the well-exposed (east to south-west), sloping hillsides of the sub-Vosgian hills. The delimitation therefore refers to this typical landscape.
The controlled designation of origin “Alsace grand cru Kaefferkopf” is characterised by soils associated with a most favourable mesoclimate. Facing south-east and established on the six hills (“Kopf”) overlooking the commune of Ammerschwihr, the lieu-dit is cool, with slow ripening of the grapes. The brown soils, derived from various parent rocks, are mostly deep and saturated in calcium and magnesium.
@@HB
@@HUM1
The name Kaefferkopf appears in 1338 in the land register (urbarium) of the abbey of Pairis. Several works and theses cite it during the 19th and 20th centuries. In 1932, the Kaefferkopf was the first lieu-dit to be recognised by a judicial delimitation. The Confrérie des Amis d’Ammerschwihr et du Kaefferkopf, which holds a large wine library, has ensured its renown since 1984.
As a translation of this usage, the controlled designation of origin “Alsace grand cru Kaefferkopf” was recognised in 2007. The varieties selected are the following: riesling B, gewurztraminer Rs, pinot gris G and, as accessory varieties, muscat à petits grains blancs B, muscat à petits grains roses Rs, muscat ottonel B. The historical usage of the Kaefferkopf is a harmonious blend of the different varieties selected. They may also be vinified and marketed under their respective denomination.

@@H1 Kanzlerberg
@@NAT Kanzlerberg
The grand cru is situated at the edge of the closed valley of the Bergenbach and protected to the west by the Taennchel massif. Rainfall is low. The fairly steep slopes of the Kanzlerberg, at the foot of which flows the Bergenbach stream, face south and south-west. They become gentler in the upper part, forming a small plateau on the southern side at an average altitude of 250 metres. The grey marl-limestone soil is strongly marked by its particular composition of grey and black Keuper marls with gypsum, over a rock of silicified limestone made up of crystals of fluorite and baryte, the finest in Alsace. These characteristics make it a late-ripening lieu-dit with a long ripening period.
@@HB
@@HUM1
Historically, the cultivation of the vine is already mentioned here in 1312, the date of the suppression of the Order of the Templars, which at this spot owned the commandery of the Tempelhof. This estate was taken over by the Knights of St John of Jerusalem of the Order of Malta, whence the name of the lieu-dit, “Mont St Jean”. The work by Médard Barth, “Der Rebbau des Elsass und die Absatzgebieten seiner Weine”, written in 1958, already praises this lieu-dit, recognised today.
As a translation of this usage, the controlled designation of origin “Alsace grand cru Kanzlerberg” was recognised in 1983. The Kanzlerberg grand cru has the smallest area (3.40 ha) of the Alsace grands crus. The wines come from parcels that have been the subject of a rigorous and precise delimitation inspired by the historical cores of production. This recognition was built around the white varieties. The only varieties selected are the following: riesling B, gewurztraminer Rs, pinot gris G, muscat à petits grains blancs B, muscat à petits grains roses Rs, muscat ottonel B. They may be vinified and marketed under their respective denomination.

#### 3° – Causal interactions
@@CAU1 Kanzlerberg
The vines of the Kanzlerberg follow the incline of the land, exposing their fruit to the summer heat, tempered by the coolness of the Bergenbach stream and the light breeze from the valley floor. The yield is regular. Physiological ripeness is reached at a minimum degree, and the site is regularly capable of great ripeness. This late situation of the Kanzlerberg grand cru and the exceptional geology of the site – gypsum-bearing marl at the surface over a base of sedimentary rocks with multiple crystals in the subsoil – make the wines very powerful, with a foundational, broad, complex and mineral acidity. They remain discreet and elegant in their youth, and reveal all their complexity and freshness after a few years of ageing.
@@CAU2A
By adopting rigorous production rules, such as maintaining a large leaf area and manual harvesting, Alsace wine-growers preserve the assertive character of wines recognised for their complexity and their aptitude for long keeping. These wines, with a unique and very high ageing potential, develop qualitatively over several decades while retaining fine vitality, the energy of the terroir. They constitute the top of the range of this region. The wines of the Kanzlerberg are among the most highly valued wines of the Alsace vineyard.
@@H1 Kessler
@@NAT Kessler
The “Kessler”, situated on the east-facing flank of the “Unterlinger” hill, forms at its centre an amphitheatre that shelters it from north winds and from the cold air currents brought down by the Guebwiller valley. At its base appears a linear outcrop of shelly limestone (Muschelkalk) covered by sandstone colluvium. The landscape is marked by the cultivation of vines on terraces and a succession of stone walls.

@@H1 Mambourg
@@NAT Mambourg
Situated at the outlet of the Kaysersberg valley, on one of the hillsides projecting furthest into the plain, this lieu-dit thus benefits from a mesoclimate marked by small temperature ranges, far from the influence of the Vosges massif and enjoying maximum sunshine. It is one of the earliest-ripening hillsides in Alsace, with the particular feature of a small temperature range. The small parcels, generally planted in the direction of the slope, are interspersed with dry-stone walls. The calcium-magnesium soils, derived from Oligocene calcareous conglomerates and marls, are favourable to deep rooting and not conducive to very high yields. Production is regular and not very sensitive to the vintage effect. Drought risks are very rare.
@@HB
@@HUM1
The work by Médard Barth, “Der Rebbau des Elsass und die Absatzgebieten seiner Weine”, written in 1958, already praises this lieu-dit, recognised today. History reveals that the wines of the Mambourg were renowned as early as 783. The first altar wine mentioned in Alsace at that time is said to have come from the Mambourg. The divine character of the terroir indeed appears as early as the Carolingian era, in view of the interest shown in it by churchmen. In the 10th century, seven abbeys, three of them non-Alsatian, claimed possessions in the present-day Mambourg.
